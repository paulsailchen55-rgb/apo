# Open Questions

## Highest priority

### 1. How can the system sense that movement is needed?

Possible signals to investigate:

- duration of immobility
- position
- pressure distribution
- movement asymmetry
- muscle activity
- user-requested movement
- physiological signals
- patterns in an individual's history

**Status:** UNKNOWN / RESEARCH

### 2. How can it determine the appropriate amount?

Questions:

- What constitutes enough?
- How can beneficial movement be distinguished from excessive movement?
- How should fatigue change the response?
- How should the system respond when sensation is limited?

**Status:** UNKNOWN / RESEARCH

### 3. How does it know when to stop?

Possible inputs include explicit user command, discomfort, resistance, changed muscle response, changed movement quality, pressure changes, safety thresholds, time limits, and clinician-defined constraints.

Technical stopping and human consent are separate problems.

**Status:** UNKNOWN / RESEARCH

### 4. How can a person with minimal voluntary control communicate preference?

Possible interfaces include physical controls, pressure switches, voice, switch access, tactile controls, caregiver-assisted controls, or other accessible interfaces.

The system must not equate inability to communicate with willingness to continue.

**Status:** PROPOSED / UNKNOWN

### 5. How can the system preserve body awareness?

Questions:

- Can movement provide meaningful proprioceptive information?
- Can vibration or pressure provide useful feedback?
- Can the device help detect emerging voluntary control?
- Can feedback be individualized?

**Status:** UNKNOWN

### 6. What is the correct role of external electrical stimulation?

Electrical stimulation may be useful, but it should not be assumed to be the central solution.

**Status:** PROPOSED

### 7. What should be mechanical rather than electrical?

Which movements are better produced by passive motion, assisted motion, repositioning, or muscle contraction?

**Status:** UNKNOWN

### 8. How should the system respond to changing ability?

A recovering person may need progressively less assistance.

**Status:** PROPOSED

### 9. How should it handle a person who wants too much movement?

The project raised movement-seeking or compulsive-use concerns.

**Status:** UNKNOWN

### 10. What counts as movement maintenance?

Potential domains include range of motion, muscle contraction, pressure redistribution, posture, breathing-related movement, sensory stimulation, and small spontaneous movements.

**Status:** UNKNOWN

## Ethical questions

- How do we prevent the machine from overriding the user?
- How do we prevent caregiver convenience from replacing attention?
- Who owns movement data?
- Can bodily data be deleted?
- How does the person remain the authority over their own body?

## Fundamental question

> Can a machine assist bodily movement without taking ownership of the person's body?
