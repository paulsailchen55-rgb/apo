# Vertical Commons / Adaptable Residential Infrastructure

## Status
Housing design proposal / potential prototype. Not an engineering, zoning, financing or code-compliance plan.

## Concept
Explore a pillar-based, modular residential foundation that can support mixed uses and changing needs over time. The mental model is a durable infrastructure platform onto which modular residential, commercial, accessibility, senior, affordable or other units could be placed, moved, replaced or reconfigured where feasible.

The conversation considered a driveable/loading-capable foundation concept so truck-loaded containers or modular units could potentially be moved into position, while trying to minimize unnecessary tree removal and preserve future adaptability.

Possible configurations included residential units, accessible units, senior/affordable or supportive housing, small commercial or micro-lease spaces, and a green/park-like upper level. These are design possibilities, not claims of feasibility.

## Principles
- Build the durable part for long life and let occupiable modules change.
- Preserve future choice rather than locking the site into one use.
- Favor modularity, reversibility and repairability where practical.
- Integrate accessibility from the beginning.
- Consider shared infrastructure and micro-leasing where appropriate.
- Evaluate transportation/loading requirements before assuming containerized construction is practical.
- Avoid unnecessary removal of existing trees or other site assets where alternatives exist.
- Separate design hypotheses from actual structural, seismic, fire, accessibility, zoning, coastal, utility and financing requirements.

## Boundaries
This is distinct from santa-cruz-modular-public-infrastructure, which focuses on distributed public-service infrastructure and micro-leasing. It is also distinct from minimal-functional-continuity, which asks how buildings can maintain basic function during outages.

## Open questions
- Can the structural platform meet California structural/seismic and fire requirements?
- What module dimensions, loading systems and truck/crane requirements are realistic?
- How would utilities connect while retaining modularity?
- What ownership, financing and title structures fit?
- How would accessibility and egress work as units change?
- What site types are compatible?
- What lifecycle cost or embodied-material advantages can actually be demonstrated?

> Preserve the possibility before deciding what it is.
