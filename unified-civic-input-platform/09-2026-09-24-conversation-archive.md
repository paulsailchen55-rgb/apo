# Conversation Archive — 2026-09-24

## Trigger

While listening to Santa Cruz County Board of Supervisors public comments from September 15, 2026, the user observed residents attempting to communicate long-running civic problems through the limited public-comment period.

## Initial insight

The user proposed a public community board, potentially available physically through a library and digitally, where residents could report local problems without needing to know the correct department.

The reports could be grouped, summarized, triaged, and routed upward through the appropriate public-service levels.

## Ticket-system insight

The user connected the idea to prior experience with corporate and government software.

The central object is a **ticket/case**.

A ticket allows a bottom-up report to enter an administrative system and then acquire:

- assignment;
- routing;
- status;
- approvals;
- inspections;
- finance-related processing;
- decision records;
- actions;
- escalation;
- and closure.

The proposed civic innovation is therefore not necessarily a new ticketing system. It is a **public-originated entry point into appropriate existing government case machinery**.

## Important design correction

The library should be an access point, not the administrative owner of difficult civic cases.

A government-controlled platform is proposed so that public employees with appropriate authority can handle routing, legal/privacy questions, records, and escalation.

## AI

AI is proposed as an assistant for:

- classification;
- routing;
- grouping;
- summarization;
- translation;
- accessibility;
- trend detection;
- and case-history synthesis.

AI should not automatically determine legal status, emergency status, enforcement, or policy decisions.

## Privacy

The discussion identified three broad information classes:

1. public civic information;
2. restricted case information;
3. legally protected information.

The user expressed concern that sensitive civic/legal matters need appropriate protection. The project therefore should not place all civic reports into one undifferentiated public database.

## Open-source/public infrastructure

The user also raised concern about perpetual government software subscriptions.

The project records this as a research and procurement question:

> Can essential civic infrastructure be built or procured so that government retains control of its records, can inspect the system, can export data, and can migrate without permanent vendor lock-in?

This remains PROPOSED/RESEARCH NEEDED rather than an established conclusion about any particular vendor or agency.
