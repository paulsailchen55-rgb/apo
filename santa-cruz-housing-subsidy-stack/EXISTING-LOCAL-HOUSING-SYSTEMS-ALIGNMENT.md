# Existing Local Housing Systems Alignment

## Status
**RESEARCH / WORKING DOCUMENT**

This document records an early research direction for the Santa Cruz Housing Subsidy Stack project. It is intentionally not a recommendation to the County or City.

## Research Question
What housing-production, permitting, financing, affordability, and stability tracking systems already exist in Santa Cruz County and the City of Santa Cruz, and where do they connect—or fail to connect—along the path from planned housing capacity to occupied and stable housing?

## Why This Belongs in the Subsidy Stack
The existing local system is not one program. It is a stack of housing elements, RHNA implementation, zoning and rezoning, development-code rules, permitting, streamlining projects, financing, affordable-housing programs, supportive housing, rental assistance, and housing-stability programs.

The first research task is therefore to map the existing stack before proposing another layer.

## Working Transmission Chain
NEED → HOUSING GOALS / RHNA → LAND / ZONING CAPACITY → FEASIBLE PROJECT → FINANCING → APPROVAL → PERMIT → CONSTRUCTION → OCCUPANCY → AFFORDABILITY → RETENTION → STABILITY

This is a research framework, not a claim that every local program currently measures every transition.

## Initial County Alignments

### Housing Element / RHNA
County housing planning establishes housing needs, development capacity, programs, and implementation responsibilities.
Research question: What is measured between RHNA allocation and actual housing outcomes?

### Rezone / Housing Capacity
County housing-element implementation includes rezoning and other capacity measures.
Research question: After capacity is created or preserved in zoning, how is that capacity followed through to projects, permits, construction, and occupancy?

### Streamline Santa Cruz County
The County's Streamline Santa Cruz County workplan contains multiple projects addressing development-code complexity, permitting procedures, pre-application review, plan-review cycles, inspections, communications, and disaster rebuilding.
Particularly relevant existing measurements include plan-check cycle data; repeated-review analysis; permitting process maps and SOPs; pre-application timing targets; permit and inspection process improvements; and CZU application status and processing-time analysis.
Research question: Are these measurements connected across projects into a common housing-production pathway?

### Comprehensive County Code Update
Project 1 is intended to analyze development codes and recommend changes to simplify standards and procedures and improve permitting efficiency.
Research question: Can the effect of code changes later be traced beyond process improvement to project feasibility, construction, occupancy, and stable housing?

### Affordable Housing / Financing
County affordable-housing programs, fees, incentives, and other financing mechanisms form another layer of the stack.
Research question: How are subsidy inputs connected to actual completed, occupied, affordable, and retained units?

## Initial City Alignments
The City of Santa Cruz has its own housing-element implementation, zoning and objective-design standards, affordable-housing programs, ADU programs, permitting/streamlining efforts, and affordable-housing financing.
Research question: Which City systems correspond to the County transmission points, and where are the measurement systems different?

## Important Preliminary Observation
The initial research indicates that many pieces of a housing-transmission audit already exist inside local programs.

This means the project should **not assume a new audit is necessary**.

The next research task is to determine whether existing systems:
1. use compatible definitions;
2. track the same projects or units across stages;
3. preserve provenance from planning through occupancy;
4. measure friction, delay, cost, and loss at comparable interfaces;
5. connect affordable-housing production to occupancy and continued affordability;
6. connect housing production to long-term stability where appropriate;
7. publish enough information to independently reconstruct the pathway.

## Evidence Discipline
Use the project evidence tags:
- **KNOWN** — directly documented.
- **CALCULATED** — derived from documented information.
- **TESTED** — independently checked or reproduced.
- **PROPOSED** — suggested future method.
- **SPECULATIVE** — plausible but not established.
- **UNKNOWN** — not yet established.

Do not convert an apparent absence from a public webpage into proof that a County or City dataset does not exist.

## Research Principle
**Map the system before proposing another system.**

The objective is to find the actual transmission points, existing measurements, duplicated efforts, missing connections, and measurable losses before asking public agencies to change anything.
