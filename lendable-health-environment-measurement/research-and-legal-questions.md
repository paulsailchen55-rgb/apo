# Research and Legal Questions

Status: research agenda, not legal advice and not a statement that any proposed arrangement is currently lawful.

Library route
------------
Working hypothesis: existing Library of Things / lendable-technology infrastructure may be a lower-complexity starting point than creating a new county healthcare program. This requires local verification.

Research tracks
---------------
A. Library authority and operations: donation/purchase authority; collection approval; lending agreements; eligibility; sanitation; accessibility; loss/theft; insurance and indemnification.

B. Medical-device regulation: FDA status; home-use labeling; whether lending changes obligations; boundary between measurement access and medical practice/diagnosis.

C. Environmental measurement: usefulness; calibration and quality control; which measurements have regulatory significance; when data are informational versus evidentiary.

D. Privacy: library records; health information; consumer health data; consent; de-identification; retention; deletion; access; correction; breach response.

E. Healthcare integration: OCHIN/MyChart interfaces; patient-generated data workflows; external-app security review; whether an equipment-lending nonprofit could become a healthcare data intermediary.

F. Nonprofit/funding: appropriate structure; grants for equipment, maintenance, software, training, evaluation, research; donation/procurement rules; ownership after donation.

G. Agency sharing: lawful recipients; evidentiary standards; direct resident submission; when aggregation or independent verification is needed.

Pilot boundary
--------------
The first pilot should avoid continuous surveillance, automated diagnosis, mandatory data sharing, and devices that cannot be operated safely without extensive training.

The first pilot should demonstrate:
borrow -> measure -> understand -> retain -> optionally share.

Unresolved
----------
No legal conclusion is made here. Specific California, local, federal, library, FDA, privacy, and healthcare requirements need primary-source research and, where appropriate, qualified legal, clinical, and security review.
