# KINSHIP Architecture

## Dome
The initial structural concept is a repetitive dome using standardized members and triangular geometry.

> One geometry, few parts, few tools, repeatable fabrication.

Early fabrication uses jigs for exact member lengths and repeatable triangular assemblies. Exact geometry, member classes, joints, tolerances, buckling behavior, and fabrication methods remain unresolved.

## Buoyant lower pod
The dome sits on a circular buoyant lower pod intended to be watertight, buoyant, stable/self-leveling, potentially self-righting, and capable of storing protective panels and equipment.

Floating does not automatically mean self-righting. Stability must be engineered through center of gravity, center of buoyancy, waterplane geometry, ballast, freeboard, compartmentation, and testing.

## Four articulated arms
Four arms are envisioned as multifunctional environmental manipulators rather than merely legs.

Potential functions: walking, leveling, anchoring, controlled release, aquatic propulsion/stabilization, fins/paddles/hydrofoils, habitat connection, retrieval, repair, and possible independent operation.

Potential end effectors include land feet, wheels, anchor interfaces, rock-anchor interfaces, water fins/paddles, and connectors.

Critical questions include load paths, traction, slope capability, obstacle crossing, turning, actuator energy, redundancy, and behavior after one-arm failure.

## Anchoring
Core principle:

> Standardize the habitat interface; allow the anchor to be site-specific.

Possible anchors include concrete/masonry attachment points, soil anchors, temporary stakes, rock anchors, and engineered docking points.

Conceptual sequence:

**Anchor → hold → detect transition condition → controlled release → change mode.**

## Aquatic mode
The concept combines a buoyant pod, attitude control, possible gyroscopic stabilization, four articulated arms, deployable fins/paddles/hydrofoils, and potentially hydrodynamic lift.

A gyro can contribute to attitude control but does not replace passive hull stability. Real implementation requires inertia, control authority, power, sensing, and failure analysis.

## Transforming envelope
The habitat may use stored protective skins rather than one permanent wall attempting every hazard.

Conceptual states:

**OPEN → WEATHER → FIRE → IMPACT → EXTREME**

Manual deployment should remain possible where practical. Autonomous/robotic deployment is an optional capability layer.

Possible mechanisms include overlapping panels, accordion folds, hinged plates, sliding triangular tiles, rolled sheets, tensioned skins, nested shells, and stored panel magazines.

Key question:

> Where is each capability stored when it is not being used, and how does it move into position without compromising the underlying structure?

## Networked habitats
Multiple units may connect to share structural bracing, anchoring, power, water, communications, storage, emergency passage, and repair/support functions.

## Exploratory aerial concepts
Emergency aerial stabilization and airship transport remain speculative future branches. They are not baseline capabilities.
