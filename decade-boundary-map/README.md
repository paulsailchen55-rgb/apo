# Decade Boundary Map

Classification: research question / framework concept.

A separate research body from Information Archaeology. It asks why institutions, technologies, contracts, datasets, and planning systems sometimes use multi-year horizons, especially ten years.

Origin: the conversation noticed an apparent coincidence between a ten-year maximum discussed for domain registration and the U.S. Census ten-year cycle. This is preserved as an observation, not evidence of a common cause.

Core question: Where do decade-long boundaries occur, why do they occur, and what happens to information, rights, obligations, or system state when those boundaries are reached?

Possible comparison subjects include domain registration periods, censuses, records retention, patents, scientific datasets, standards review cycles, infrastructure planning, leases, warranties, environmental monitoring, demographic datasets, and election or redistricting cycles.

For each subject record the actual interval, governing rule or practice, stated reason, what changes at the boundary, renewal rules, archival or disposal behavior, responsible party, and whether the interval is legal, customary, technical, economic, or convenient.

Boundary with Information Archaeology: that project studies historical information survival and discoverability; this one studies the institutional meaning of multi-year boundaries. Do not merge them.

Unresolved: the conversation did not establish that domain-registration limits were created to prune abandoned websites, nor that Census and domain rules share an underlying design principle. Those explanations require source verification.
