# Lived Experience and Field Notes

This document preserves direct observations separately from externally verified claims. The questions below are invitations to understand the experience more carefully, not judgments about the person describing it or the people involved.

## Ten questions for further inquiry
1. What happened, in the order it happened, and what details can be recalled directly?
2. Which parts are direct observations, and which parts are interpretations or later conclusions?
3. What housing options were actually offered, and what alternatives were known at the time?
4. What conditions affected the experience: cost, safety, location, rules, transportation, privacy, or stability?
5. What choices did the person feel able to make, and what choices felt unavailable?
6. Which details may be specific to one situation, and which might warrant broader research?
7. What documents, messages, photographs, dates, or witnesses could help establish the record?
8. What would have made the process clearer, safer, or more respectful?
9. Whose perspectives should be included before drawing broader conclusions?
10. What additional question should a researcher ask next without assuming the answer?
