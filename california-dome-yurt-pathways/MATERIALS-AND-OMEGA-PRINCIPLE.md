# Materials and Omega Principle Framework

**Status:** PROPOSED policy and research framework  
**Scope:** Dome and yurt housing pathways in California, beginning with Santa Cruz County

## Core Direction

This project does not treat the easiest commercial installation pathway as the only pathway. It is intended to help people who have already chosen dome or yurt housing investigate options that minimize newly installed plastic and other fossil-derived or difficult-to-manage materials, while meeting actual safety and legal requirements.

The objective is not to reject a material merely because it is synthetic. The objective is to evaluate whether its function can be achieved with a safer, more durable, more repairable, less toxic, more recoverable, or more naturally derived alternative.

## Omega Principle

For this project, the Omega Principle means that material decisions must account for the full life cycle:

1. Extraction and feedstock
2. Manufacturing and additives
3. Transport and installation
4. Occupant exposure and maintenance
5. Fire, weather, earthquake, flood, and other failure conditions
6. Repair, replacement, and adaptation
7. Disassembly, reuse, recovery, recycling, biodegradation, or disposal

Future Phoenix Project and Dragonfly Project concepts may address material transformation, destruction, recovery, or end-of-life infrastructure. They are PROPOSED concepts and must not be assumed to exist today.

## Material Evaluation Questions

For every proposed assembly, record:

- What is it made from?
- Does it contain plastic, petrochemical feedstock, persistent additives, or composite layers that are difficult to separate?
- Can individual parts be repaired or replaced?
- What happens when it burns, degrades, floods, or reaches end of life?
- Can it be safely disassembled and reused?
- Are local tools, skills, and replacement materials available?
- Does it meet the required structural, fire, moisture, energy, health, and accessibility performance?
- Is there product-specific engineering and jurisdictional acceptance?
- What evidence supports each claim?

## Candidate Material Families to Investigate

These are research categories, not preapproved solutions:

- Earth-based systems, including rammed earth and stabilized earth
- Straw, light-straw-clay, and other agricultural-fiber systems
- Bamboo and other plant-based structural or finish materials
- Timber and engineered wood, including repair and end-of-life considerations
- Mineral plasters, masonry, ceramics, and other mineral-based finishes
- Metals where durability, disassembly, and recovery justify their impacts
- Limited synthetic materials where no safer feasible substitute currently meets the required function

Natural origin alone does not prove fire safety, seismic adequacy, durability, low toxicity, or code compliance. Every assembly requires specific verification.

## Research Rules

- Do not use purchase price or permit familiarity as the only decision criteria.
- Do not treat manufacturer marketing as independent evidence.
- Do not assume that “natural,” “green,” “non-toxic,” “recyclable,” or “fire-resistant” applies to every product in a material family.
- Evaluate the complete assembly, including coatings, membranes, adhesives, insulation, fasteners, sealants, and foundations.
- Compare alternatives by function and life-cycle consequences, not by appearance alone.
- Preserve uncertainty when evidence is incomplete.

## Evidence Categories

- **KNOWN:** supported by reliable documentation for the specific claim
- **CALCULATED:** derived from stated assumptions or measurements
- **TESTED:** supported by a documented test or field result
- **PROPOSED:** intended design direction not yet validated
- **SPECULATIVE:** hypothesis requiring investigation
- **UNKNOWN:** information not yet established

## California Context

California’s embodied-carbon work treats extraction, manufacturing, transportation, installation, maintenance, and disposal as relevant life-cycle stages. The project should therefore use life-cycle assessment and material-specific evidence as research inputs, while separately verifying code compliance and site-specific hazards.

## Initial Deliverables

- Materials screening rubric
- Assembly comparison worksheet
- Plastic and petrochemical content disclosure template
- Fire and end-of-life question set
- Repairability and disassembly checklist
- Source register for technical studies, product documentation, and agency decisions
- Plain-language explanation for residents and community organizations

## Limitation

This framework does not itself approve any material or construction method. Final decisions require qualified design professionals, applicable testing, and the relevant authority having jurisdiction.
