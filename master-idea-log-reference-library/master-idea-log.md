# Master Idea Log — Reference Library
Date: 2026-09-18
Status: personal working reference / archive, not a single project

This is the detailed reference layer behind several smaller functional packets. It preserves ideas discussed across the conversation so they can be recalled, connected, questioned, or selectively shared with other people.

Status labels are provisional:
- PRINCIPLE = organizing principle
- QUESTION = unresolved question
- INVESTIGATE = needs evidence or expert review
- PROPOSAL = a concrete suggestion
- EXPERIMENT = testable prototype or trial
- SUPPORTING IDEA = useful component of another body of work
- PERSONAL/PHILOSOPHICAL = metaphor or reflection, not a factual policy claim

Core method:
You notice → tell me → organize → test → identify known/unknown → clarify the question → decide whether it is worth showing someone else.

## 01–06 — Core principles
1. Posterity Principle — PRINCIPLE. Meet present needs while preserving future generations' ability to choose.
2. Build for Posterity, Not Permanence — PRINCIPLE. Durable infrastructure need not mean an unchangeable building.
3. Today's Needs / Tomorrow's Choices — PRINCIPLE. The central recurring question: can today's solution preserve tomorrow's options?
4. Planning for Uncertainty — FRAMEWORK. Design for changing technologies, uses, ownership, regulations, economics, climate, and community needs rather than pretending the future is known.
5. Short Clock / Long Clock — FRAMEWORK. Compare financial, governmental, community, physical, and generational time horizons.
6. Become Better Ancestors — PERSONAL/PHILOSOPHICAL PRINCIPLE. Evaluate present decisions partly by the choices and conditions they leave to people later.

## 07–19 — Housing, buildings, land and adaptability
7. Long-Life Foundation / Changeable Building — PROPOSAL. A durable structural/infrastructure base with replaceable or reconfigurable occupiable components.
8. Layered Lifespan Map — FRAMEWORK. Map foundation, structure, utilities, envelope, finishes, equipment, uses, and ownership by expected lifespan.
9. Legos With Logic — CONCEPT. Use standardized interfaces and components so future changes are possible without treating a building as disposable.
10. Modular / Pillar-Based Structural System — EXPERIMENT/PROPOSAL. Explore a pillar/platform infrastructure that can support changing modular units.
11. Preserve + Add Capacity — PROPOSAL. Preserve useful existing buildings/assets where feasible while adding housing or infrastructure rather than assuming demolition is the only route.
12. Adaptable Corridor — FRAMEWORK. Treat major corridors as places where development may need to adapt to future transportation, commercial, housing, and public-realm changes.
13. Buildings as Infrastructure for Unknown Futures — CONCEPT. Design buildings partly as long-lived civic/physical infrastructure rather than single-purpose objects.
14. Productive Vertical Commons — EXPERIMENT/PROPOSAL. Vertical mixed-use infrastructure combining housing, common space, productive uses, green space, accessibility, and potentially micro-leased space.
15. Distributed Housing / Multiple Sites — PROPOSAL. Explore distributing a required housing total across several sites where lawful and feasible, reducing concentration and allowing varied building forms.
16. Demolition Alternatives Assessment — RESEARCH QUESTION. Before demolition, compare reuse, partial retention, addition, relocation, modular replacement, and other alternatives.
17. Clean-House Principle — CONCEPT. A building should have a clear, understandable relationship between its components, maintenance, replacement, and eventual removal.
18. Long-Life / Short-Life Matching Problem — RESEARCH QUESTION. Are short-lived components being embedded in systems expected to last much longer, and can interfaces be improved?
19. Future Builder Test — RESEARCH QUESTION. If a future builder inherits the property decades later, can they understand what can be changed without destroying useful information or assemblies?

## 20–27 — Mobility, temporary capacity and productive land
20. Movable Platforms — EXPERIMENT. Explore whether standardized platforms can allow structures or equipment to move between prepared locations.
21. Call-a-Car / Call-a-Platform Vision — CONCEPT. A mobility service in which a prepared platform or vehicle comes to a user rather than requiring every use to have fixed infrastructure.
22. Road / Rail-Compatible Modular Movement — EXPERIMENT. Explore whether standardized modules could be transferred by truck, rail, or other systems where engineering and regulation permit.
23. Prepared Parking / Transfer Lots — SUPPORTING IDEA. Existing paved or prepared spaces could potentially serve as temporary transfer, staging, or modular-capacity nodes.
24. Microleasing / Temporary Capacity — PROPOSAL. Lease small amounts of space or capacity for short periods so underused property can support changing community needs.
25. Urban Productive Layers — CONCEPT. Add food, small production, gardens, common space, or other productive uses as layers within urban infrastructure.
26. Small-Scale Animal Husbandry in Urban/Vertical Systems — EXPERIMENT/QUESTION. Earlier discussion considered chickens, goats, sheep, or similar uses where appropriate; requires strict review of zoning, animal welfare, sanitation, nuisance, structural, fire, and neighborhood constraints.
27. Mobile / Microleased Agricultural Capacity — EXPERIMENT. Explore temporary or movable agricultural uses on suitable land or infrastructure rather than assuming agricultural capacity must be permanently fixed.

## 28–35 — Materials, lifecycle and building information
28. Material End-of-Life Plan — PROPOSAL. For major materials, document service life, repair/replacement path, separation method, recovery/reuse/recycling/disposal destination, and responsibility.
29. Material Concentration & Recovery — RESEARCH/EXPERIMENT. Where difficult-to-recover materials are necessary, consider whether they can be concentrated in accessible assemblies instead of permanently dispersed.
30. Omega Plan — FRAMEWORK. Every system that creates a material should have a considered end-of-life path. "Omega" is a planning metaphor, not a technical standard.
31. Right to Repair a Building — POLICY QUESTION. Future owners/occupants should, where legally and technically possible, be able to repair or replace components without unnecessary barriers.
32. Don't Bury the Instruction Manual — CONCEPT. Critical information about a building should not become inaccessible merely because it is physically enclosed.
33. Building Passport — POSSIBLE PROJECT. A long-term, transferable record that stays with a property. Possible contents: structural drawings, utility maps, major material lists, manufacturers, installation dates, expected service life, maintenance requirements, replacement procedures, repair information, known hazards, recovery information, and renovation history.
34. Open-Source Building Principle — CONCEPT. Make enough non-proprietary building information available to permit future maintenance, repair, adaptation, and understanding, subject to legitimate security/privacy constraints.
35. Open Building Scorecard — RESEARCH/PROTOTYPE. Develop a way to describe adaptability, repairability, documentation, material recovery, and future-use flexibility without pretending that a single score proves overall quality.

## 36–47 — Civic information, participation and authority
36. Community Civic / Memory Library — POTENTIAL PROJECT. A public archive for project documents, meeting notes, plans, maps, timelines, questions, answers, contacts, proposals, and decisions.
37. Community Project Note System — PROTOTYPE. Simple structured records for observations and questions so useful civic information survives beyond a meeting.
38. Accessible Participation — PROPOSAL. Civic information should be usable by people without specialized software, high-speed internet, or high levels of technical literacy; libraries and assisted access can be part of the infrastructure.
39. Library as Civic Infrastructure — CONCEPT. Public libraries can serve as physical access points to civic records and digital tools.
40. Human-Scale Governance / Child Test — FRAMEWORK. Explain a process so a person without specialized knowledge can understand what is happening and what they can do next.
41. Translation Layer — CONCEPT. Translate between resident language, technical language, legal language, and administrative process without changing the underlying claim.
42. One-Page Civic Decision Guide — PROTOTYPE. A concise map showing what is proposed, what is known, what remains open, who decides, what evidence matters, and the next decision point.
43. Who Decides? Map — FRAMEWORK. Identify who can answer, decide, review, fund, regulate, approve, enforce, or route a question.
44. Map of Constraints — FRAMEWORK. Separate constraints created by law, regulation, site conditions, finance, safety, infrastructure, ownership, and policy choice.
45. Where Is the Choice? / Choice Audit — FRAMEWORK. Identify which decisions remain genuinely open at each stage instead of assuming every outcome is predetermined.
46. Question Before the Demand — METHOD. Start with a clear question and evidence request before converting uncertainty into a demand.
47. Authority / Agency / Posterity — FRAMEWORK. Examine three separate dimensions: who has authority now, who has agency to act, and what choices remain for people later.

## 48–53 — Finance, dependency and resilience-adjacent concepts
48. Micro-Credit as Stabilization — RESEARCH QUESTION. Could very small, appropriately designed credit help someone exit an immediate crisis rather than requiring prior stability as a condition of access?
49. Credit as Capacity-Building / Stability Credit — CONCEPT. Consider whether lending models can evaluate the borrower's path toward stability, not only current stability.
50. Necessity Test — FRAMEWORK. Before accepting a costly or binding arrangement, ask what is actually necessary, what is optional, and what future choices are consumed.
51. Subscription Future — RESEARCH QUESTION. Examine how recurring payments can turn access to basic needs into long-term obligations and how that affects future choice.
52. Dependency Audit — FRAMEWORK. Map dependencies in a system: money, utilities, vendors, proprietary components, data, institutions, leases, regulations, and maintenance.
53. Idea Archive — PROJECT/SUPPORTING INFRASTRUCTURE. Preserve ideas with provenance, status, unresolved questions, and links so they do not disappear when a conversation ends.

## 54 — Archive principle
54. Nothing Should Disappear Just Because the Conversation Ended — PRINCIPLE. The Master Idea Log is a memory/reference layer, not proof that an idea is correct or a commitment to pursue it.

Finance 48–52 is a separate research direction. The archive itself is supporting infrastructure.

Religious or biblical language discussed in conversation is retained as personal/philosophical provenance and metaphor. It is not treated here as engineering, legal, economic, or historical evidence. Claims about particular people or organizations being secretly hostile, surveilling, or hacking are not converted into facts without evidence.
