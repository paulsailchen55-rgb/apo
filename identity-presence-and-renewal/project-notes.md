# Project Notes

## Origin

The immediate trigger was an attempt to determine whether an existing About.me profile could serve as a public identity hub that is readable by people and discoverable by search engines and AI retrieval systems.

The important discovery was that the objective is broader than finding a replacement for About.me. The objective is to understand and test a web presence in which a person can be consistently identified without making social media participation mandatory.

## About.me as the initial specimen

The user's existing About.me page was treated as a reference specimen for an older model of personal web identity: a human-readable page containing a person's name, location, description, professional identity, body of work, projects, and links.

The downloaded HTML/MHTML copy became the practical source because direct automated retrieval of the live About.me page was unsuccessful.

The project should not assume that the old platform's current search visibility or AI accessibility is equivalent to ordinary browser accessibility.

## Search first, social second

A central observation is that search is the primary discovery layer. Social or professional profile services are secondary identity surfaces that can reinforce or explain an identity.

This reverses the common assumption that a person must first participate in a social network and then become discoverable through it.

## Identity network rather than account collection

The goal is not to accumulate accounts. The goal is to establish consistent signals across a small number of useful public locations:

- a person-level identity page;
- project-level pages and repositories;
- search-indexable content;
- selected identity/profile services;
- links among authoritative representations.

## Singular identity versus singular tracking

The phrase "singular identity" must not be allowed to become synonymous with "singular tracking identity."

A desirable architecture could allow a person to demonstrate that multiple public profiles represent the same person when appropriate while permitting particular searches, transactions, library use, government research, or other activities to remain anonymous or pseudonymous where appropriate.

## Library analogy

A library card provides a useful conceptual analogy. A person can be present as an authorized user of a public institution without every individual act of inquiry becoming a public identity statement.

This raises a broader design question: how can online systems provide accountable presence without making every activity permanently attributable?

## Daily and temporary identities

The project recognizes at least three broad persistence patterns:

1. Immediate or short-lived identities, including temporary or "burner" accounts designed to expire quickly.
2. Medium-term identities maintained for a particular period, project, event, or responsibility.
3. Persistent identities representing a person, institution, long-term project, or continuing body of work.

The appropriate persistence period remains an open design question.

## Equipment analogy

The user's practice of periodically shutting down, inspecting, cleaning, repairing, and restoring equipment provides a practical analogy for identity maintenance.

An object can remain the same object while being temporarily taken out of operation. Likewise, an identity can remain part of a person's continuing presence while individual accounts, credentials, records, or services are paused, repaired, renewed, archived, or retired.

## September 19, 2026 context

The project was consciously examined during a period of rest. The user described ordinary morning activities, food preparation, giving equipment rest, and continuing some maintenance while intentionally reducing continuous operation.

The project treats that context as part of the origin story rather than as a requirement that all users adopt the same religious or calendar practice.
