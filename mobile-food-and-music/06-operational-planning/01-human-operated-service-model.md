# Human-Operated Service Model

## Principle

The machine supports service delivery, but a trained human remains present. The human role is to protect participant choice, prevent dispensing errors, observe safety issues, document incidents, and help maintain trust.

## Core Duties

- Verify startup and sanitation checks
- Confirm water-system status before service
- Explain ingredient choices and warnings
- Dispense only selected packets
- Monitor hot-water temperature and safe handling
- Prevent tampering, contamination, and unauthorized access
- Record equipment faults, incidents, and corrective actions
- Maintain orderly queues and respectful participation
- Shut down the unit safely

## Data and Accountability

The service should maintain simple operational records without collecting unnecessary personal information. Records may include the device ID, location, time, operator, water-system checks, batch numbers, and incidents.

Any participant information should have a defined purpose, limited access, retention rules, and appropriate security controls.

## Service Philosophy

The operator is not intended to control personal dietary choices. The role is to provide accurate information, preserve the available options, and prevent unsafe or unauthorized dispensing.

## Research Questions

1. What training is required for the operator?
2. Can the system use a clear checklist for every setup and shutdown?
3. What events must be reported to a supervisor or public authority?
4. How can the human role remain supportive rather than intrusive?
5. What staffing level is required for different attendance levels?
