# Pauline and Biblical Letter Model

The project uses biblical letters as rhetorical inspiration, not as a claim that the modern letter has scriptural authority.

Romans 16 provides the clearest structural inspiration. Paul commends Phoebe and then greets numerous people by name, recognizing relationships, work, and community connections.

Transferable pattern:

identify people -> recognize their work -> recognize the network -> encourage the community.

Romans 16:23 mentions Erastus with a civic office. This is useful as an example of a civic role appearing in a personal letter, while not proving that all named people were officials.

Acts 18:1-3 connects Paul with practical trade work, resonating with the author's construction background.

Potential supporting passages include Romans 16; Romans 8:19-23; Acts 18:1-3; Mark 1:35; Luke 5:16; Luke 6:12; Matthew 10:14; Mark 6:11; and Luke 9:5.
