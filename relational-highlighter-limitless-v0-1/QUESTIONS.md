# Open Questions — Version 0.1

## Core mechanism

1. What causes an observation to become highlighted?
2. Is highlighting based on relevance, novelty, contradiction, repetition, emotional salience, prior memory, or some combination?
3. Can the system record *that* something popped out without pretending to know exactly why?
4. Why 3–4 active highlights? Is that a design constraint, a cognitive metaphor, or something that should be experimentally tested?
5. How does an active highlight become ghost memory?
6. How long does ghost memory persist?
7. What causes a ghost memory to reactivate?
8. How are overlaps represented?
9. What counts as a genuinely new emergent observation rather than a restatement of existing information?
10. Can multiple weak overlaps combine into one strong emergence?
11. How does the system prevent an attractive but unsupported connection from becoming “fact”?
12. What does human confirmation actually change?
13. Can a person reject an emergence while retaining the observations that produced it?
14. How should forgetting work?
15. Can the entire system remain portable across different AI models and databases?

## Technical questions

- Can the mechanism initially be implemented without a vector database?
- Would sets, graphs, relational tables, embeddings, or a hybrid representation be most transparent?
- What is the smallest possible experiment?
- How should provenance be attached to every highlight?
- How should uncertainty be represented?
- Can the system explain why a highlight appeared without fabricating an explanation?

## Conceptual question

Is the fundamental unit of AI memory actually a **memory item**, or is it a **relationship among observations**?

That question remains open.
