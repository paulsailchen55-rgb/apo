# 10 — Research Agenda and Open Questions

## Physical work

1. Reconstruct the two-basin sink setup precisely.
2. Record basin and drain geometry.
3. Test both drains open, one blocked, and controlled boundary conditions.
4. Record vortex onset, duration, direction, and repeatability.
5. Identify plumbing and air pathways.
6. Separate fluid-flow effects from pressure and air-entry effects.

## Information theory

1. Define the signal in the sink experiment.
2. Define sender, receiver, channel, and noise.
3. Determine whether information about one basin can be recovered from observations of the other.
4. Compare mutual information and transfer-entropy approaches.
5. Study transient channels.
6. Determine what information survives after a pathway disappears.

## Fluid dynamics

1. Study vortex formation and stability.
2. Study coupled reservoirs and connected drains.
3. Examine flow topology.
4. Investigate transient coherent structures.
5. Identify reproducible experimental conditions.

## Astrophysics

1. Review wormhole and Einstein-Rosen-bridge models.
2. Distinguish traversable and non-traversable constructions.
3. Review causal connectivity and information-transfer constraints.
4. Examine ER=EPR as a theoretical conjecture, not established fact.
5. Determine whether any fluid mathematical structure has a legitimate spacetime analogue.

## Human and AI information

1. Model humans as observers and participants.
2. Study representation changes across language, mathematics, images, theories, and AI.
3. Define representational fidelity and distortion.
4. Study repeated transformation and reconstruction.
5. Investigate provenance preservation in AI-mediated transformations.

## Philosophy and theology

1. Clarify image versus source.
2. Clarify "one breath" as a scale and temporality metaphor.
3. Examine apophatic and transcendent language.
4. Formalize the possibility that local degradation does not imply absolute disconnection.
5. Keep theological propositions distinct from empirical claims.

## Central unresolved question

> Can one mathematical language describe how a finite, temporary system can be coupled to a larger system, transmit information through a transient pathway, transform that information through representation, and remain connected while entering states of degradation or restoration?

This is the point at which the archive could become an interdisciplinary research program rather than a collection of metaphors.
