# Modular Material Recovery System

This folder preserves the machine concept as a separate technical research/prototype direction. It is not an approved appliance, engineering design, safety certification, or demonstrated recovery technology.

## Core architecture

The proposed system is a family of standardized, enclosed Material Recovery Modules rather than one universal machine.

**Identify → Route → Process → Verify**

Incoming furniture or household components should first be identified and classified. They are then routed to a specialized module whose processing method and operating profile are validated for the material and its condition. A module may be reprogrammable, but reprogrammability does not mean unrestricted universal processing.

## Physical concept

The conversation developed a sealed cabinet/vessel with internal robotic handling. One or more arms may hold, rotate, inspect, separate, or present components to a fixed or movable processing station. Possible downstream functions include screening, controlled vibration, magnetic separation, density/air classification, optical sensing, and contained collection.

## Blasting and dust

The original concept included blasting/processing components into very small particles, followed by vibration/separation and contained blocks. This is retained as a hypothesis, not as an established safe technology.

Abrasive blasting, grinding, crushing, or pulverizing can create respirable dust, heat, noise, sparks, fragments, and contaminated mixtures. The preferred engineering principle is:

> Controlled material liberation and separation, not destruction into dust.

Where a component can be removed intact, intact recovery is preferred.

## Specialized processing profiles

Each material module should have a validated profile defining permitted material class, condition limits, exclusions, handling method, processing method, operating envelope, expected output, verification criteria, shutdown conditions, and maintenance requirements.

AI and sensors may assist identification, routing, anomaly detection, monitoring, and verification. They should not be treated as unrestricted authority to invent physical processing parameters in real time.

## Output blocks

The concept includes compact, stackable containers for known recovered streams. Outputs should remain identifiable and have real downstream destinations. The proposed microbial outer skin remains a research hypothesis; alternatives include reusable rigid containers, controlled liners, molded fiber shells, and appropriate binders.

## Lego-like modularity

Potential modules include loading/isolation, robotic handling, identification, disassembly, processing, vibration/screening, separation, filtration, controls/safety, power, fire control, and output containment.

Potential common interfaces include mounting, power, data, emergency-stop, airflow, material inlet/outlet, identification, maintenance, and safety interlocks.

Product modularity means things can come apart. Machine modularity means recovery equipment can be repaired, upgraded, specialized, and reconfigured.

## Safety boundary

A home or apartment version should not be assumed to process all household waste. Unknown, hazardous, contaminated, battery-containing, chemical, asbestos-containing, or otherwise specialized materials require rejection, quarantine, or a qualified downstream pathway.

## Development

Begin with clean, non-hazardous bench experiments, then professionally engineered enclosed prototypes and institutional testing before residential deployment is considered.

## Current status

Conceptual architecture. Particle reduction, vibration separation, packaging/skin, material identification, AI-assisted routing, and specialized processing profiles all require validation.

## Relationship

This system is the potential downstream closure component of the Apartment Circular Materials Standard, but remains a separate project so engineering questions do not get confused with product/material design questions.
