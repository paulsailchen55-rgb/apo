# Research Questions

## Title and legal capacity

1. Who may hold legal title to California real property?
2. Which persons are legally capable of acquiring property but practically unable to do so?
3. Are homelessness, incarceration history, parole/probation, debt, lack of credit, or lack of a fixed address legal prohibitions, practical barriers, or both?
4. What differences exist between fee title, an ownership interest, a beneficial interest, a leasehold interest, a membership interest, and an occupancy right?
5. Which entities can hold title, and what responsibilities accompany each?

## Community ownership

6. How do community land trusts divide land ownership from building ownership or occupancy?
7. How do housing cooperatives allocate rights and responsibilities?
8. How do nonprofit corporations, trusts, and LLCs differ when used for community housing?
9. Can an organization hold land while individuals hold durable, transferable, inheritable, or otherwise protected interests in use or occupancy?
10. What happens when a resident loses income, leaves, dies, violates an agreement, or becomes unable to manage responsibilities?

## The property-less entry problem

11. What pathways exist for someone with no property to obtain a legally recognized interest in land or housing?
12. Which barriers are caused by property law itself and which arise from financing, credit, insurance, zoning, income screening, administration, or market conditions?
13. Could public or community institutions acquire property first and then allocate defined rights to people who could not have acquired the property individually?
14. What responsibilities can be separated from title while remaining enforceable and transparent?
15. Is there a useful category between ordinary tenancy and individual fee ownership?

## System incentives

16. Which institutions receive public or private funding for housing, shelter, incarceration reentry, behavioral-health services, or property management?
17. What incentives can unintentionally produce long-term dependency or repeated institutional placement?
18. Which claims about financial benefit are documented, and which are only hypotheses?
19. Does a measurable institutional incentive exist to keep people from acquiring stable housing or property, or are barriers better explained by separate institutional objectives and market conditions?
20. What evidence would distinguish systemic feedback loops from intentional coordination?

## Historical and philosophical comparison

21. How have societies historically classified people without stable household, land, citizenship, family, or community status?
22. What changes occurred between religious charity, poor relief, orphan/foster systems, asylums, public institutions, modern behavioral-health systems, shelters, and housing programs?
23. Which parts of the user's "unclaimed/fatherless" analogy are historical observations, and which are theological interpretations?
24. How can the metaphor be preserved without treating spiritual claims as empirical evidence?

## Design question

25. If existing legal structures are insufficient, what exactly is missing?
26. Is the missing element a new title category, a new ownership entity, a new beneficial interest, a new occupancy right, a financing mechanism, or a governance arrangement?
27. What minimum rights and responsibilities would a new model need?
28. Could such a model preserve a person's pathway from temporary access toward greater control or ownership?
29. What protections would prevent exploitation, coercion, arbitrary eviction, or institutional capture?
