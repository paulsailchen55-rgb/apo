# Santa Cruz County Demonstration Research Plan

**Project:** California Adaptive Dome & Yurt Housing Pathways  
**Initial demonstration area:** Santa Cruz County, California  
**Status:** PROPOSED research and verification plan

## Purpose

Use Santa Cruz County as the first demonstration area for a repeatable process that helps people who have already chosen dome- or yurt-based housing understand what they need to do next.

This is a navigation and access project—not a campaign to persuade people to choose domes or yurts, and not a guarantee of permit approval.

## First Rule: Identify the Governing Jurisdiction

Before giving a pathway, identify whether the parcel is governed by:

- County of Santa Cruz
- City of Santa Cruz
- City of Watsonville
- City of Capitola
- City of Scotts Valley
- Another incorporated or special jurisdiction

County information must not be presented as automatically applying inside a city.

## Initial Research Tracks

### 1. Parcel and Land-Use Screening

For a specific parcel, identify:

- Governing jurisdiction
- Zoning district and General Plan designation
- Existing legal structures and permitted uses
- Coastal Zone status
- Agricultural, timber, or park land status
- Environmental constraints and protected resources
- Fire district, water provider, sewer or septic status
- Access, roadway, and emergency-response constraints
- Potential grading, drainage, geotechnical, flood, landslide, or other hazard reviews

### 2. Development Pathways

Evaluate each pathway separately:

1. Remove an existing home and replace it with a dome or yurt.
2. Keep an existing home and add one or more smaller units.
3. Develop a vacant or newly assembled property.
4. Develop multiple connected structures or a community-scale arrangement.
5. Convert or adapt an existing permitted structure where legally and technically possible.

The project must not assume that an unconventional shape or construction system changes the underlying requirements for legal dwelling use, structural safety, utilities, fire protection, accessibility, or occupancy.

### 3. Agencies and Human Contact

Record the responsible office, official webpage, phone number, email, appointment process, and questions to ask for:

- Planning and zoning
- Building and safety
- Environmental planning
- Public Works and grading
- Environmental Health and septic
- Fire protection district
- Water and sanitation agencies
- Coastal review authorities, when applicable

### 4. Code and Technical Verification

For every proposed design, determine which current code and review topics apply, including:

- California Building Standards Code / Title 24 edition in effect at application
- Residential, building, energy, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, and fire provisions as applicable
- Structural design and professional engineering requirements
- Foundation and anchorage
- Wind, seismic, rain, wildfire, flood, and slope conditions
- Egress, sanitation, ventilation, accessibility, and emergency access
- Product approval or alternative-material/process documentation, if applicable

A manufacturer statement, diagram, or informal interpretation is not treated as equivalent to jurisdictional approval.

## Current Observations to Verify and Track

- County ADU materials are being updated to reflect recent state-law changes.
- County ADU review may involve zoning, environmental planning, Public Works, fire districts, and sanitation or septic review depending on the site and proposal.
- County materials identify special considerations for coastal areas, agricultural/timber/park land, protected resources, and environmental hazards.
- City and County rules, contacts, and review pathways must be kept separate.

## Limitations

This plan is a research framework. It does not constitute legal advice, engineering approval, zoning confirmation, permit approval, or a determination that any specific dome or yurt can be occupied as a dwelling.
