# Government Authority / Responsibility Map

## Classification
Civic research framework.

## Purpose
Map who has authority, responsibility, review power, timing, information and decision points in a government process. The framework is intended to reduce the common problem in which a resident knows that something matters but cannot tell which institution can answer the question.

## Questions to map
- Who can decide?
- Who can recommend?
- Who can review?
- Who can enforce?
- Who provides information?
- What is the legal or procedural trigger?
- What happens before and after the decision?
- What clock or deadline applies?
- What can a resident appeal, comment on, or request?
- Where can the process pause, branch or end?

## Comparative research direction
The conversation considered differences among federal committees, state commissions/committees, county Boards of Supervisors, city councils, planning bodies and other agencies. These institutions should be compared using current legal and procedural sources rather than assumed to have identical powers.

Ways and Means and Federal Reserve interactions were also raised as subjects for research, not as conclusions about authority.

## Relationship to Civic Flow Chart
Authority Map asks **who is responsible for what**. Civic Flow Chart asks **what the resident encounters step by step**. They can inform each other without becoming one project.

## Status
Research framework. Current jurisdiction-specific powers and procedures remain to be verified from authoritative sources.
