# Twelve / 4×3 / 6-Pair Organizing Metaphor

## Classification
Personal / philosophical exploration; organizing hypothesis.

## Origin
The conversation explored whether the number twelve associated with Jesus' twelve disciples could serve as a conceptual organizing limit for thinking about committees, with possible arrangements such as four groups of three or six pairs with overlap.

## Important boundary
This is a personal metaphor or hypothesis for organizing thought. It is **not** a claim that governments are structured around twelve committees, four groups of three, or six pairs.

## Possible conceptual use
The metaphor was associated with:
- layers of authority;
- responsibility;
- time;
- maps;
- overlapping roles;
- a clock-like way of seeing decisions and relationships.

## Research question
If used as an organizing device, does a small bounded number of categories make complicated authority structures easier for ordinary people to understand without falsely implying that the real institutions have that structure?

## Public-use caution
If translated into a civic document, label it as a visualization or organizing model and separately show the actual institutional structure.

## Status
Philosophical exploration / design metaphor. Not an established civic framework.
