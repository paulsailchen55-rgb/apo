# Research Agenda

## Evidence categories to collect

1. **Thermodynamics:** Energy required for seawater cooling, freezing, desalination, and brine management.
2. **Marine chemistry:** Salinity, pH, alkalinity, dissolved oxygen, freezing-point effects, and brine behavior.
3. **Coral ecology:** Heat stress, bleaching thresholds, localized cooling, shading, and effects on associated species.
4. **Pollutant behavior:** Plastic, microplastic, nutrient, metal, pathogen, petroleum, and chemical movement during freezing and treatment.
5. **Engineering:** Pumps, heat exchangers, refrigeration, containment, sensors, autonomous or crewed vessels, and maintenance.
6. **Energy accounting:** Full life-cycle energy use, emissions, waste heat, fuel sources, and potential rebound effects.
7. **Operations:** Storm response, navigation, wildlife interaction, emergency shutdown, retrieval, and end-of-project removal.
8. **Governance:** Permits, environmental review, coastal-zone regulation, international waters, liability, and community consent.
9. **Monitoring:** Baseline data, control locations, measurable outcomes, uncertainty, and independent review.

## Proposed staged pathway

- Stage 1: Literature review and source inventory.
- Stage 2: Physical calculations and computer modeling.
- Stage 3: Laboratory tests in contained systems.
- Stage 4: Independent ecological and engineering review.
- Stage 5: Only if justified, a small permitted pilot with predefined stop conditions.

## Stop conditions to consider

A proposed intervention should stop if it causes unexpected salinity or chemical changes, harms organisms, disperses collected pollutants, creates unsafe navigation conditions, exceeds its energy or emissions budget, loses containment, or cannot be monitored reliably.

## Source-record template

For each study or experiment, record:

- Citation and link.
- Location and environmental conditions.
- Scale and duration.
- Materials and energy source.
- What was measured.
- What was not measured.
- Results and uncertainty.
- Whether the study supports, limits, or contradicts the project hypothesis.
- Whether the result can reasonably be extrapolated to an ocean setting.
