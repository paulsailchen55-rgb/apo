

## Boundary-Air Management

The processing material stream remains contained, but the appliance itself moves air and generates heat. A controlled appliance-air path may therefore be needed to transfer residual heat to the surrounding apartment.

Preferred principle: **no intentional release of processed material or particulate; only controlled, monitored, filtered appliance air may cross the occupied-space boundary.**

Potential safeguards include particulate filtration, airflow and temperature monitoring, filter-loading detection, abnormal-condition shutdown, and sealed filter-service access. This supersedes the earlier shorthand target of “no external exhaust”: the intended target is no uncontrolled process exhaust, while a filtered thermal/air path may be necessary.

## Moisture Management — New Design Track

Almost all ordinary household feedstock can carry some moisture. Breeze therefore cannot assume perfectly dry input even though the processing mechanism is currently intended to remain water-free.

**PROPOSED design principle:** the reduction chamber remains a dry-process chamber, while incoming moisture must be detected, tolerated within a defined envelope, removed/contained, or cause the cycle to stop.

Moisture questions include:

- free liquid and spills;
- surface moisture;
- absorbed moisture in wood and textiles;
- humidity introduced with feedstock;
- condensation caused by temperature changes;
- moisture released during material fracture;
- corrosion and electrical insulation;
- wet-particle agglomeration and clogging;
- biological growth during delayed operation;
- changes in abrasive behavior and acoustic particle transport.

Candidate approaches are **PROPOSED / UNKNOWN**: feedstock moisture limits, pre-cycle moisture sensing, condensate detection/collection, conditioned carrier air, limited desiccant/sorbent research, condensation control through thermal design, and automatic moisture fault shutdown.

The current concept remains **water-free inside the processing mechanism**. Introducing water as a routine processing medium would require a separate design decision.

Moisture is also expected to interact strongly with the proposed acoustic particle-transport system: damp fines may agglomerate, adhere to surfaces, clog passages, and behave differently acoustically. Testing therefore needs dry, moderate-moisture, and fault-condition feedstock classes.

## Updated Evidence / Open Questions

**PROPOSED**
- Controlled filtered appliance-air boundary.
- Dry-process moisture-management requirement.

**UNKNOWN**
- Quantitative moisture envelope for mixed household feedstock.
- Condensation-control method.
- Boundary-air filtration specification.
- Effect of moisture on acoustic transport, classification, collection, and abrasive reduction.

## Updated Research Sequence

After defining permitted feedstock, establish: **moisture envelope → heat generation → condensation risk → particle behavior → collection → filtered boundary-air performance.**
