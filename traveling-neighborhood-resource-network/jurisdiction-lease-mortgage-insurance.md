# Jurisdiction, Lease, Mortgage, and Insurance Research

Status: Research framework. Not legal advice.

## Core distinction

Four different permission questions must remain separate:

1. **Government law:** Is the proposed activity permitted by applicable state, county, city, zoning, parking, camping, occupancy, environmental, or other rules?
2. **Property authority:** Does the person have authority to offer the resource?
3. **Private contracts:** Does a lease, HOA rule, mortgage, management agreement, or other contract restrict the proposed activity?
4. **Insurance:** Is the activity covered, excluded, or subject to conditions?

A “yes” to one does not automatically answer the others.

## Jurisdiction matrix

For each pilot location, research at minimum:
- state
- county
- city/town
- zoning district when relevant
- parking rules
- overnight vehicle rules
- camping rules
- transient lodging / short-term rental rules
- occupancy rules
- accessory dwelling / guest-space rules when relevant
- wastewater and water-use rules
- vehicle repair or commercial-use restrictions
- fire and life-safety requirements
- applicable taxes or fees
- enforcement and permit pathway
- source date
- verification date
- unresolved questions

## Stay-length question

The user recalled a California limit of roughly 28 days and discussed a possible one- or two-night platform model.

Preserve this as a research question.

Do not encode “28 days,” “30 days,” or any other number as a universal legal threshold. Different doctrines can use different definitions and time periods, and the legal status of a stay depends on facts and governing law.

## Adverse possession / “squatter's rights”

Do not use “squatter's rights” as shorthand for ordinary short-term occupancy.

California judicial materials describe adverse possession as a distinct property doctrine requiring multiple elements, including five years of continuous possession and timely payment of assessed taxes. That doctrine is not a general rule saying that a short-term guest becomes an owner after a particular number of days.

Current legal review is still required for tenancy, transient occupancy, guest status, unlawful detainer, camping, and short-term rental questions.

## Renter pathway

A host registration form should ask:
- Are you the property owner?
- Are you a tenant?
- Do you have written permission from the owner or property manager?
- Does your agreement restrict subleasing or short-term rental?
- Does it restrict guests?
- Does it restrict parking or overnight vehicle use?
- Does it restrict commercial activity?
- Does it restrict repairs or vehicle work?
- Is there an HOA or similar governing document?
- What documentation can establish the participant's authority?

The form should not ask the host to certify a legal conclusion they cannot reasonably know.

## Mortgage / lender pathway

Questions may include:
- Does the mortgage or occupancy agreement contain relevant use restrictions?
- Is written lender approval required?
- Does the proposed activity affect owner-occupancy requirements?
- Does insurance required by the lender cover the activity?

These are research questions, not assumptions that ordinary mortgages prohibit hosting.

## Insurance pathway

Research:
- homeowner/renter policy
- commercial general liability
- short-term rental coverage
- host protection programs
- umbrella coverage
- vehicle-related liability
- property damage
- bodily injury
- theft
- abuse/exploitation claims
- exclusions
- claims handling
- state licensing and insurance regulation
- whether a platform can purchase or arrange coverage
- whether each host needs separate coverage

The phrase “shared network liability insurance” is a user concept. It is not a guarantee that one policy can cover every participant.

## Evidence model

Every legal entry should record:
- exact source
- jurisdiction
- effective date
- activity covered
- exact language or relevant excerpt
- interpretation
- confidence
- unresolved question
- human/legal review needed

## Positive reform question

If a restriction prevents a low-risk community service, ask:

> Is there a lawful, voluntary, narrowly defined pathway by which the owner, tenant, lender, insurer, and local government could permit the activity with clear responsibilities?

That question should precede any claim that a contract or law is invalid.
