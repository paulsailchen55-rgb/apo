# WAYFINDER Verification and Governance

## Purpose

WAYFINDER should improve the accuracy of community information without becoming a public reputation database.

The object being verified is the **information**, not the moral quality of an organization or person.

## Basic interaction

After using a listing, a person can report:

- **Yes — the information matched**
- **No — the information was not available as described**
- **Something was different**

The system can use those reports to trigger re-checking or replacement of stale information.

## Public status model

Possible public states:

- Confirmed
- Needs checking
- Reported unavailable
- Not enough recent information

Avoid public “top ten,” popularity rankings, organization scores, or permanent negative tallies.

## Source chain

A listing can have:

1. Original source.
2. Wayfinder extraction/interpretation.
3. Verification date.
4. Community confirmation signals.
5. Current status.
6. Next review date.

AI should not be the final judge of whether a community is “good” or “bad.”

## Privacy and abuse resistance

A future backend may use anonymous or pseudonymous verification identities, rate limits, anti-abuse measures, and minimal retention. The exact implementation remains open.

The system should not expose raw verification identities or create a public dossier about a participating organization.

## Governance principle

> WAYFINDER records information about opportunities, not judgments about people or organizations.

Negative feedback should trigger investigation or re-verification, not automatic condemnation.

## Non-territoriality

A WAYFINDER symbol does not mean:
- this place belongs to us
- these people are good
- those people are bad
- this is our block
- do not enter
- this organization has been judged

It means only:

> **There is useful information ahead.**

Physical placement should respect property permission and ordinary public-posting rules.
