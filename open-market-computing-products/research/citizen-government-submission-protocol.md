# Citizen–Government Submission Protocol

## Why this belongs in Open Market Computing Products

The Open Market Computing Products project asks whether ordinary people should have a clear, legitimate place to repair, study, modify, develop, and experiment with computing products.

That question depends partly on something broader:

**Can an ordinary citizen actually communicate a new policy idea to the institutions that govern the technology?**

The research developed in the separate Citizen–Government Access and Submission Protocol project is therefore directly relevant here.

## The connection

An open computing market is not useful to ordinary people if the policy system surrounding it is so difficult to navigate that only corporations, attorneys, universities, or specialized organizations can effectively communicate their concerns.

The two projects therefore meet at the point of **citizen access to authority**.

The Open Market Computing Products project concerns the boundaries around the machine.

The Citizen–Government Submission Protocol project concerns the boundaries around the citizen's ability to communicate with the institutions that establish or enforce those boundaries.

## Shared principle

> **Government authority may remain distributed while citizen access to that authority becomes easier to navigate.**

This does not require eliminating separate jurisdictions, agencies, committees, courts, regulators, or legislative bodies.

It asks whether the path through those institutions can be made understandable enough for an ordinary person to participate.

## Practical example

A citizen develops a proposal concerning:

- repair;
- firmware access;
- interoperability;
- intellectual-property clocks;
- consumer ownership;
- open computing products;
- independent experimentation.

The citizen should not have to become an expert in the entire governmental structure simply to determine:

> "Who should receive this?"

A better system could help identify relevant institutions and explain the available submission routes.

## Why it matters to open computing

The project is ultimately concerned with preserving meaningful space for independent experimentation.

That includes not only the ability to experiment with a machine, but also the ability to participate in the public systems that determine what experimentation is permitted.

Therefore:

**Access to the machine and access to the policymaking system are related forms of technological participation.**

## Boundary

This file does not claim that government intentionally prevents citizens from participating, nor does it claim that all governmental fragmentation is harmful.

Distributed authority can serve legitimate purposes, including checks and balances, specialization, jurisdiction, and the ability to pause or review actions.

The research question is narrower:

> **Can distributed governmental authority coexist with a simple, understandable path for an ordinary citizen to reach the appropriate authority?**
