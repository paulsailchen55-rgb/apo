# What I Mean by Civic Flex-Space

## A practical framework for using space more responsively

**The basic idea**

Civic Flex-Space treats space as a resource that can be allocated not only by **where** it is, but also by **when**, **how**, and **by whom** it is used.

In simple terms:

**Space × Time × Use × Responsibility**

The goal is not to eliminate zoning, safety standards, property rights, or public oversight. The goal is to give communities another tool: the ability to authorize appropriate uses for appropriate places and periods without assuming that every use must become a permanent land-use designation.

---

## 1. The Problem

Much of our built environment is organized around relatively fixed categories: commercial, residential, parking, public space, restricted space, and so on.

But actual human activity is often temporary.

A storefront may be empty for part of the day. A plaza may be unused at certain hours. A parking area may serve different purposes at different times. A vacant or underused space may be valuable for a pop-up business, community gathering, maker activity, emergency response, or another lawful temporary use.

When a temporary activity creates a problem, the response can sometimes become a broader or more permanent restriction than the original problem required.

**FlexSpace asks a different question:**

> Instead of beginning with “How do we prohibit this?” can we first ask, “What place, time, scale, and conditions would make this lawful use workable?”

---

## 2. The Principle

**Purpose–Place–Time**

Where an activity is lawful in itself, seek an appropriate place, time, scale, and operating conditions before resorting to broader prohibition.

**Pertinent Externalities**

Address the actual impacts of a particular use—such as obstruction, noise, sanitation, pollution, congestion, or safety—rather than treating an activity or person as permanently problematic.

**Accountability**

Every allocation should identify the relevant space, period, authorized use, responsible party, and applicable conditions.

**Freshness**

When a transaction ends, the system returns to the ordinary baseline unless current circumstances demonstrate a reason for additional conditions.

**Emergency**

Duly authorized emergency response remains able to temporarily override ordinary allocations when necessary.

---

## 3. The Bright Plaza Experiment

The proposed Bright Plaza/1214 Soquel Avenue project provides an opportunity to test this concept at a manageable scale.

The proposal submitted for the project asks whether a portion of the proposed ground-floor commercial space could be designed and authorized for **short-duration flexible uses**.

The original proposal identified approximately **20% of a 5,000-square-foot ground-floor commercial area—about 1,000 square feet—as a potential flexible-use area.**

Possible uses could include appropriate pop-up retail, makers, community activities, or other authorized temporary uses.

The point of the experiment is not to predetermine every future use.

The point is to test whether a properly designed space can support **multiple lawful relationships with the same physical space over time.**

---

## 4. How It Works

A simple FlexSpace transaction could look like this:

**1. Identify the purpose.**  
What is the person or organization trying to do?

**2. Identify an eligible space and time.**  
Is there a space available for that purpose during the requested period?

**3. Apply the baseline conditions.**  
Accessibility, fire/life safety, existing legal rights, capacity, utilities, sanitation, and other applicable requirements are checked.

**4. Assign responsibility.**  
The reservation or agreement identifies who is responsible for the use and for complying with its conditions.

**5. Use the space.**  
The authorized activity occurs within the defined time, space, capacity, and conditions.

**6. Complete and reset.**  
The space is cleaned, restored, and returned to the ordinary allocation system.

**7. If a problem occurs, respond proportionately.**  
Identify the actual problem, apply the smallest workable correction, and allow restoration when responsible use is demonstrated.

The underlying idea is:

**Simple → Problem → Proportionate Response → Demonstrated Responsibility → Restoration → Simple**

---

## 5. Safeguards

FlexSpace does not mean “anything goes.”

A workable system would need to preserve:

- legally protected property and contractual rights;
- accessibility and accessible paths of travel;
- fire, life-safety, and emergency access;
- public-health and sanitation requirements;
- appropriate capacity and occupancy limits;
- applicable environmental protections;
- clear responsibility for each authorized use;
- transparent reservation/allocation records;
- proportionate responses to demonstrated problems;
- a defined mechanism to pause an allocation when a material concern arises;
- restoration rather than unnecessary permanent exclusion;
- emergency authority when genuinely required.

The principle is:

> **Stable architecture, flexible occupancy.**

The guardrails should be reliable. The use within those guardrails can remain adaptable.

---

## 6. Questions for Workbench and the City

This proposal is intended to invite technical and civic evaluation, not to presume that every implementation question has already been answered.

**Questions for discussion include:**

1. Can a portion of the proposed commercial space legally and practically support short-duration authorized uses?
2. What uses could be permitted or pre-authorized without creating unnecessary administrative burden?
3. How should accessibility, fire/life safety, occupancy, sanitation, insurance, and liability be handled?
4. How should existing leases, property rights, and other pre-existing rights be protected?
5. What information would need to be recorded for each Space × Time × Use × Responsibility allocation?
6. What would a simple reservation, operating, cleanup, and accountability process look like?
7. What problems should trigger a pause, correction, or additional condition—and how should responsible use be restored afterward?
8. What can be tested at Bright Plaza now, and what questions should be left for later phases of the broader Civic Flex-Space model?

### The larger question

Can we design a civic system that provides **stable rules and protections while remaining responsive to changing conditions?**

FlexSpace is an attempt to explore that possibility at a small, practical scale—starting with one place, one project, and one measurable experiment.
