# Conversation Record — September 19, 2026

This document records the development of the project during the September 19, 2026 conversation.

## Starting point

The conversation began with an About.me page and an attempt to determine what modern services could reproduce its useful function: a public, searchable, human-readable identity page connecting a person to their work.

The initial candidates included LinkedIn, Linktree, Bento, Carrd, Wix, and Squarespace. The discussion then moved away from choosing a service and toward understanding the underlying problem.

## Major development

The user clarified that the objective is not primarily social-media participation.

The desired architecture is:

**search engines first → public identity/presence → project and institutional identities → social or professional profile services where useful**

The user expressed concern that the Internet has moved away from a broadly accessible, persistent public identity layer comparable in some respects to what Google+ once attempted.

## Global framing

The user emphasized that the problem is global because the Internet and social media cross national boundaries.

The experiment is personal and situated in Santa Cruz, California, but the research question is international.

## Identity and anonymity

The conversation distinguished a singular public identity from a singular tracking identity.

The user wants to explore whether a person can have a coherent identity across the Internet while still being able to conduct appropriate activities anonymously.

The library-card analogy was used to describe being present and accountable as an authorized person without necessarily exposing every individual act of inquiry.

## Rest and renewal

The user connected the inquiry to a day of rest and reflection. Equipment was being shut down or rested, ordinary morning activities continued, and some maintenance work remained appropriate.

This produced the analogy that identities, like bodies, clothing, writing, tools, and other things associated with people, may require recurring maintenance, cleansing, repair, rest, renewal, archiving, and retirement.

## Historical extension

The user raised historical examples involving bodily cleansing, clothing, bodily remnants, writing, expungement, burning, and transformation into ash.

The project records these as historical research questions rather than verified universal facts.

## Natural timing

The conversation raised the possibility that identity lifecycle events could use natural cycles as reference points without requiring agreement on one universal cultural calendar.

This remains an open research question.

## Current project direction

The project now combines:

1. a practical experiment in public online identity;
2. a technical inquiry into discoverability, identity, anonymity, and portability;
3. a lifecycle model for digital identities;
4. historical research into cleansing, separation, disposal, and renewal;
5. a philosophical inquiry into identity, neighbor recognition, responsibility, and rest;
6. a global question about how human identity should function on a worldwide network.

This record is not intended to imply that every idea discussed is established fact. It preserves the development of the inquiry so that later work can distinguish observation, hypothesis, design proposal, historical evidence, and philosophical premise.
