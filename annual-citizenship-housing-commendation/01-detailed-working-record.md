# Detailed Working Record

The author wants to send separate letters to public officials, public servants, program workers, housing staff, caseworkers, community organizations, and other people who contributed to a network through which he reached stable housing.

The letter is a commendation and expression of gratitude, not a complaint, legal filing, academic paper, or case history.

The author describes a lifetime of intermittent homelessness and housing instability in California and elsewhere. At age 48, he has now remained housed for nearly two years and has renewed his lease. Exact dates are deliberately not central.

The transition was difficult and not guaranteed. It involved instability, difficult environments, setbacks, public systems, recovery-related pathways, housing services, and the challenge of learning to live in stable housing after long periods without it. The difficulty must remain visible.

The author's experience involved court/probation-related processes, substance-use-disorder services, mental-health-related services, sober living, shelter/housing resources, caseworkers, and community organizations. Names mentioned during development include Encompass, Evolving Door, Homeless Garden Project, libraries, parks, Friends of the Library, Friends of State Parks, neighborhood/community associations, museums, and other civic spaces. Exact roles require verification.

The author wants to recognize that people experiencing homelessness remain neighbors and participants in civic life.

The author has hands-on building experience dating to childhood. This informs a housing philosophy emphasizing practical construction, repair, local knowledge, resident participation, durability, and responsible material life cycles.

The author understands gratitude through a relationship with God and believes God can work through neighbors, public servants, institutions, and ordinary acts of service. The outward letter should respect recipients who understand highest authority differently.

Romans 16 is a rhetorical model: recognize people by name, role, relationship, and contribution within a wider community. The modern letter does not claim scriptural authority or equivalence.

Housing should be more than placement inside a structure. Wherever practical, it should be durable, understandable, repairable, adaptable, locally appropriate, and supportive of resident participation. The author's Omega Principle asks builders and users to think about the end of a thing while creating or using it.

The author is also interested in civic flowcharts that make government pathways visible. That is a separate research project and should not overwhelm the commendation letter.

The letter should not become an academic biblical paper, MLA paper, personal case file, legal argument, medical history, accusation against government, generic success story, or technical housing proposal.

Do not publish unnecessary confidential medical, legal, case-management, or personal information.

The broader project explores an annual practice of citizenship and commendation, potentially associated with a meaningful civic date. Historical/calendar research remains separate.
