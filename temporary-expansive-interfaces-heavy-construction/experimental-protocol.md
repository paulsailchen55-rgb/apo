
# Experimental Protocol

## Purpose

Determine whether a temporary interface produces measurable mechanical advantage over a simple machine operating directly on a rough surface.

## Safety

Begin with loads that can be safely handled by ordinary laboratory/workshop equipment. Do not use hazardous expanding chemicals, high-pressure vessels, caustic rock-eating mixtures, or large suspended loads without qualified controls.

## Experiment 1 — Sand grading

Compare:
A. irregular bare surface;
B. dry granular layer;
C. controlled-moisture granular layer;
D. graded/compacted granular layer.

Measure pull force, displacement, surface deformation, moisture, and layer thickness.

## Experiment 2 — Foam/interface comparison

Compare a safe low-energy foam system with water and a non-foaming control.

Measure deformation under load, pressure if safely measurable, contact area, friction, and drainage/collapse time.

The objective is to test whether foam's structured interface produces behavior that differs materially from the liquid control.

## Experiment 3 — Incremental lift

Use a small load and a mechanical jack or other controlled device.

Measure whether an interface changes the force required to create and maintain a 1–10 mm clearance.

Do not infer large-scale performance from a single small experiment.

## Experiment 4 — Abrasive carrier

Using inert materials, compare water and a more viscous carrier for retaining abrasive particles at a controlled cutting interface.

Record cutting rate, particle retention, surface finish, debris removal, and temperature.

## Experiment 5 — Plant-derived foam

Candidate families may include food-safe or otherwise well-characterized plant/seaweed-derived surfactant systems.

Measure foam generation, foam half-life, drainage, bubble size, density, and repeatability.

Do not assume visible foam height corresponds to lifting capacity.

## Experiment 6 — Small slug-and-beetle demonstrator

Construct a simple reversible mover.

Target:
- known load;
- 1–10 mm incremental lift;
- known travel distance;
- repeatable cycles.

Compare:
1. machine alone;
2. machine + graded sand;
3. machine + graded sand + interface.

## Measurements

Record raw values:
- load mass;
- contact area;
- interface thickness;
- force;
- displacement;
- cycle count;
- time;
- moisture;
- temperature;
- material mass before/after;
- visible failure mode.

## Falsification

The hypothesis becomes weaker if foam adds no measurable advantage, pressure collapses too quickly, the interface increases friction, natural materials cannot maintain useful contact, material consumption is impractical, or the beetle mechanism gains no meaningful advantage from the interface.

A failed experiment is useful evidence.
