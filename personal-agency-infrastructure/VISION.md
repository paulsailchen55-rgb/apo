# Personal Agency Infrastructure

## Purpose

Personal Agency Infrastructure (PAI) is an auditable, user-controlled translation layer between people and institutions.

The system is intended to help a person communicate across institutional boundaries without requiring the person to first learn the institution's vocabulary, structure, or procedural language.

It is not an AI that speaks for people. It is infrastructure that helps people speak across institutional boundaries without losing their original voice.

## Core principles

- AI translates the person without replacing the person.
- Preserve the original human input.
- Distinguish experience, interpretation, allegation, evidence, rule, and verified fact.
- Preserve uncertainty instead of converting uncertainty into certainty.
- Preserve disagreement instead of forcing concurrence.
- Make every transformation auditable.
- Let the person review and authorize consequential transformations or transmissions.
- Keep the person's records portable and usable if the AI provider changes.
- Make institutional routing explicit: who can act, what authority they have, and what information they need.
- Support individual and collective action without destroying individual provenance.
- Design for exit: the infrastructure should not become an unavoidable dependency controlled by one institution.

## The central problem

People routinely have to translate lived experience into forms that housing authorities, employers, unions, government agencies, attorneys, medical systems, schools, insurers, and other institutions can process.

That translation burden can itself become a barrier to agency.

PAI proposes an intermediate layer that can accept ordinary human communication and produce institutionally usable representations while retaining the path back to the original communication.

## A useful test

For any final statement, a person should be able to ask:

> Where did this statement come from, who changed it, why was it changed, and what evidence or authority supports the change?

If the system cannot answer that question, the representation is not sufficiently auditable.
