# Safety, Trust, Privacy, and Abuse Resistance

## Threat model

The user identified a specific danger: a network designed to connect travelers with helpful households could inadvertently make those households easier to locate and exploit.

Potential threats include:
- theft
- stalking
- harassment
- unwanted repeat contact
- fraudulent bookings
- property damage
- identity theft
- location harvesting
- targeting of vulnerable hosts
- targeting of vulnerable travelers
- false reports
- account takeover
- misuse of reviews or references

These are threat categories, not claims about actual users.

## Privacy-first location model

Do not publish a searchable directory of exact residential addresses.

Possible sequence:
1. Publicly display only a general area.
2. Display the resource category and broad availability.
3. Require an account before requesting access.
4. Apply proportionate trust and verification measures.
5. Host accepts or rejects a request.
6. Reveal exact access instructions only when appropriate.
7. Limit unnecessary retention of location and transaction data.
8. Provide blocking, reporting, and emergency procedures.

## Trust model

Trust should be multi-dimensional rather than one score.

Possible signals:
- account age
- identity verification status
- references
- completed stays
- mutually confirmed transactions
- verified property authority
- community participation
- incident history handled through due process

Do not treat any signal as proof that a person is safe.

Avoid a public reputation system that turns a single complaint into a permanent accusation.

## Host safety

Hosts should be able to:
- refuse a request
- define availability
- define who may participate
- specify areas that are off limits
- limit stay length
- require a booking agreement
- cancel under defined conditions
- report incidents
- block accounts

## Traveler safety

Travelers should be able to:
- see the rules before accepting
- know what resource is actually being offered
- know the total cost
- know check-in and departure requirements
- report inaccurate listings
- report unsafe conditions
- leave a location without being trapped by the platform

## Data minimization

Collect only what is needed for:
- identity/account operation
- booking
- payment
- safety
- legal compliance
- incident response

Exact location, personal documents, and communications should not be retained indefinitely merely because the platform can collect them.

## Emergency principle

The platform should distinguish ordinary customer support from genuine emergencies. Emergency procedures should direct people to appropriate public emergency services rather than pretending that the platform itself is an emergency authority.

## Verification principle

Verification should answer:

> Is this person, property, resource, or claim what the system says it is?

It should not attempt to certify that a person is morally good or permanently safe.
