# Field Observations: Community Activities and Public Information

## Date

September 19, 2026

## Purpose

This record preserves firsthand observations relevant to WAYFINDER: discovering accessible community activities, documenting what is actually available, and examining how discrepancies between public descriptions and on-site conditions affect trust.

## Positive community-access observations

I attended a pedal-bike gathering in the Seabright neighborhood at a park whose exact public name should be verified. The event was present and active, with cyclists spending time together in the community.

The park also appears to host other recurring or occasional free activities, potentially including Tai Chi, martial arts, and related gatherings on Saturday or Sunday mornings. These details require verification before being published as current listings.

The observation suggests that WAYFINDER should record not only formal scheduled events, but also recurring public activities that people may discover through local observation—while clearly distinguishing firsthand observation from confirmed schedule information.

## Rio event information discrepancy

A separate observation concerned an event at the Rio that had been understood from advertising to be a free party or free pre-show connected with a block party show. I traveled there intending to attend without paying. At the ticket area, staff stated that there was no free show and that tickets were required.

An older woman who had also seen the event information in *Good Times* asked whether I had seen the block party show. She said she had gone to the same event and had been told the same thing.

The cause is not established. Possibilities include inaccurate or incomplete advertising, a last-minute change, a distinction between a free pre-show and a ticketed event, or a communication problem. The observation should not be treated as proof of intentional deception without further evidence.

## Research question: access, trust, scarcity, and social effects

Repeated situations in which something is presented as free, affordable, or accessible but requires payment or has different conditions upon arrival may weaken people’s trust in a venue, event, or neighborhood. Over time, people may become guarded or emotionally hardened toward a place because they expect an unpleasant surprise or an unexpected cost.

At the same time, limited or difficult-to-find information can sometimes make a discovery feel valuable. A person may appreciate learning about useful community resources that are not widely known. This raises a tension: how can a community preserve the pleasure and value of discovery without turning information into an exclusionary possession, status marker, or source of pride that encourages people to keep others out?

WAYFINDER should investigate this tension without assuming that scarcity is always harmful or that openness eliminates all value. The practical aim is dependable access information that supports participation, dignity, and informed choice rather than surprise costs, gatekeeping, or reputational judgment.

## Questions for further inquiry

1. How often do advertised free or low-cost opportunities differ from conditions encountered on arrival?
2. What information most clearly distinguishes a free event, a free portion, a ticketed event, and an event with optional paid activities?
3. How should last-minute changes be communicated to people who may already be traveling?
4. What does repeated access disappointment do to people’s trust in a venue or neighborhood?
5. How can public information remain useful without becoming a reputation-scoring system?
6. When does hard-to-find information create healthy discovery and community connection?
7. When might scarcity, secrecy, or gatekeeping encourage exclusion, pride, or unhealthy social dynamics?
8. Can information be shared in a way that preserves discovery while making basic access conditions clear to everyone?
9. How can firsthand observations be recorded while separating facts, interpretations, and unresolved questions?
10. What verification status should be shown when an event was observed in person but its recurring schedule is not yet confirmed?

## Preliminary direction

WAYFINDER can preserve both positive discoveries and access discrepancies. It should document what was observed, identify what remains uncertain, retain source and timing information when available, and avoid automatically assigning blame or judging people and organizations.
