# Resource Categories and Pilot

## Proposed resource categories

These are examples from the originating conversation, not an approved service list.

### Overnight / rest
- permitted overnight parking
- tent or small-camper space
- couch or indoor hospitality
- short rest period

### Basic resources
- water refill
- restroom access
- vehicle washing
- charging when legally and safely available

### Vehicle support
- designated place for limited work
- basic hand tools
- tire inflation
- cleaning supplies
- other explicitly defined resources

Each category requires its own legal and safety analysis.

## One- or two-night model

The user suggested emphasizing one- or two-night stays.

This is a product-design hypothesis that may reduce complexity compared with long occupancy, but it is not a legal guarantee.

## Pilot should be narrower than the full vision

A first pilot should not begin with:
- every state
- every county
- every kind of property
- every service
- open public addresses
- an assumption of universal insurance coverage

A more controlled experiment could begin with one jurisdiction and one or two resource types.

## Possible first pilot

Candidate sequence:
1. Select one jurisdiction.
2. Research the exact local rules.
3. Recruit a small number of participants who can document property authority.
4. Offer one narrowly defined resource.
5. Use manual approval instead of automated eligibility.
6. Keep exact addresses private until a booking is accepted.
7. Record incidents and near-misses.
8. Measure whether the service is actually useful and manageable.

## Evaluation

Measure both positive and negative outcomes:
- successful stays
- cancellations
- conflicts
- property damage
- safety incidents
- privacy incidents
- cost to host
- cost to traveler
- administrative burden
- repeat participation
- whether users found lawful alternatives
- whether the service created unexpected neighborhood impacts

Do not claim that the pilot reduces homelessness or crime unless evidence supports the claim.

## Vehicle-work resource

The “kit next to the garage” idea is preserved as a feature experiment. A host might offer a clearly labeled kit for permitted basic work. This should not imply that the host is offering unrestricted mechanical repair services.

## Affordability

Around $50 was discussed as an example ceiling. The actual price should be determined by local costs, legal requirements, insurance, accessibility, and the intended use.
