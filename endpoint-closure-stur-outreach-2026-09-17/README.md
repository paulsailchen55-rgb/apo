# Endpoint Closure / STUR Outreach — September 2026

This folder archives a working outreach package developed in conversation.

It is a reference/archive artifact, not a declaration that the underlying policy proposal has been adopted, validated, or completed.

## Purpose

Preserve the working idea that systems should provide a clear, proportionate, and practically usable endpoint for people who need to correct, transfer, complete, or end a relationship with the system.

The outreach is intentionally cross-sector. It is meant to reach people who regulate, administer, advocate, report on, build, maintain, or experience systems.

## Outreach sequence

1. Government and public institutions
2. Privacy / civil liberties
3. Civil rights / accessibility
4. Local and public-interest journalism
5. Environmental / physical product stewardship
6. Civic technology
7. Open source / hackerspaces / makerspaces / software builders
8. Further loose-end organizations across the above categories

The sequence is exploratory. It is not a claim that these groups endorse the proposal.

## Outreach batching rule

- Use batches of up to ten recipients.
- Prefer one organization per slot rather than multiple people at the same organization.
- Once an organization has been contacted for this campaign, do not resend merely because another address at that organization is discovered.
- If an address cannot be verified or is unusable, replace it before presenting a ten-recipient batch.
- Preserve unresolved contacts as unresolved rather than silently converting them into verified contacts.
- The objective is breadth across organizations and communities, not repeated contact within the same institution.

## Core policy question

“When an ordinary person is finished with this system, can they actually get out?”

## STUR questions

SPACE — Where is it?

TIME — What clock governs it?

USE — What is it being used for?

RESPONSIBILITY — Who is responsible?

CLOSURE — What happens when it ends?

The working name is STUR: Space, Time, Use, Responsibility. Closure is treated as the endpoint question that tests whether the relationship can actually conclude.
