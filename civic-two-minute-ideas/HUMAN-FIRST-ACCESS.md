# Human-First Access and Civic Idea Support

**Status:** PROPOSED — Stage 1/2 design input
**Evidence classification:** PROPOSED; service availability requires future verification

## Foundational distinction

Human assistance is a primary entrance, not merely a fallback after digital tools fail. When the Civic Communication Lab opens, it should first offer a clearly visible path for people who are tired of technology, do not want to use an app, lack reliable internet, prefer conversation, or simply want to speak with another person.

The system should then offer additional paths:

- Call or text a verified human-help resource.
- Visit a local library, civic organization, community group, or other verified location.
- Use a website or downloadable HTML tool.
- Talk with an AI assistant.
- Use optional practice, printing, follow-up, and experimental Labs.

## Local resource discovery

A future connected version may search for local services based on the user's jurisdiction and needs. Each resource record should include:

- Organization or service name.
- Phone, text, or other contact method.
- Location and accessibility information when available.
- What the service actually offers.
- Eligibility, hours, cost, language, and communication limitations when verified.
- Source URL and retrieval date.
- Verification status and unresolved uncertainty.

The system must not claim that 211, a library, or another organization provides civic speech development or idea-coaching services unless an authoritative source confirms it.

## Separate project possibility

A related project may develop a human civic-idea support network: trained volunteers, librarians, peer mentors, community organizations, or paid facilitators who help people clarify an idea and prepare for legitimate civic participation by phone, text, or in person.

This should be tracked as a possible related project rather than silently treating it as an already-existing service.

## Safeguards

- Do not require AI use.
- Do not imply that human support is inferior to digital support.
- Do not expose personal information without user consent.
- Present multiple legitimate participation routes without ranking them.
- Verify current contact details before displaying them as available now.
- Explain emergency or crisis services separately from ordinary civic communication assistance.
