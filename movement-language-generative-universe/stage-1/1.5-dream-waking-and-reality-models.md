# 1.5 — Dream, Waking, and Reality Models

## Foundational Question

How can a person tell whether they are creating the universe, discovering the universe, participating in a universe that responds to them, or experiencing a reality that feels imposed upon them?

This question can be explored philosophically, psychologically, artistically, and technologically. The project must distinguish between:

- A person's lived experience.
- Observable behavior and movement.
- Clinical or psychological explanations, where relevant and responsibly studied.
- Cultural and spiritual interpretations.
- Fictional or generative-world design.

Difficulty distinguishing dreams from waking experience can occur in different contexts and should not be treated as a single condition or explained casually. The project should use respectful language and avoid diagnosing participants from movement data.

## Three Initial Universe Models

### 1. Creation Model

Movement generates the environment or its changes. The participant's actions function as inputs that produce an output.

### 2. Discovery Model

The environment and its rules already exist within the system. Movement reveals, navigates, or interprets what is present.

### 3. Participation Model

The participant and environment influence one another. Movement changes the world, while the world responds in ways that shape subsequent movement.

A fourth interpretive possibility should also be recorded:

### 4. Imposition Model

The participant experiences events, rules, or meanings as being imposed upon them rather than generated through their own agency. In a digital prototype, this can be modeled as externally controlled or unpredictable input, without making claims about the participant's real-world experience.

## Experimental Comparison

Use the same movement input in simplified prototypes representing the creation, discovery, participation, and imposition models. Compare:

- Sense of agency.
- Understanding of the rules.
- Surprise and uncertainty.
- Engagement.
- Repeatability.
- Complexity.
- Whether the experience feels like command, discovery, relationship, or external control.

The experiment should ask participants what they experienced rather than imposing an interpretation on them.
