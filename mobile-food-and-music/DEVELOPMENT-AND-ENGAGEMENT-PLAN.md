# Mobile Food and Music — Development and Engagement Plan

## Status

**Classification:** Potential project / community service prototype

**Purpose:** Develop a mobile, human-operated food, drink, water, and acoustic-music service that can operate at approved sites while emphasizing participant choice, ingredient transparency, safety, and dignity.

This document is a development roadmap, not an operating authorization or legal opinion.

## Development Stages

### Stage 1 — Freeze the Concept Boundaries

- [ ] Define the mobile unit, transport method, setup footprint, and teardown requirements.
- [ ] Decide which services are in scope for the first pilot: hot drinks, soup/broth, noodles, water, music, or a smaller subset.
- [ ] Keep the human operator central for sanitation, ingredient selection, records, safety, and participant assistance.
- [ ] Separate confirmed facts, design hypotheses, user preferences, and future possibilities.
- [ ] Define what the service will not do, including medical diagnosis or unsupported allergy testing.

### Stage 2 — Regulatory and Technical Feasibility

- [ ] Obtain a preliminary authority determination from Santa Cruz County Environmental Health based on the exact menu, equipment, water source, and service method.
- [ ] Determine whether the concept fits a charitable feeding pathway, mobile food facility pathway, temporary event pathway, or another category.
- [ ] Verify potable-water, wastewater, handwashing, hot-water, food-temperature, storage, and power requirements.
- [ ] Evaluate atmospheric water generation separately from food-service approval; do not assume generated water is automatically potable.
- [ ] Identify required food-handler cards, food-safety certification, permits, inspections, and event approvals.
- [ ] Review electrical, battery, inverter, heat, fire, transportation, and equipment-maintenance risks.

### Stage 3 — Supplier and Information System

- [ ] Identify candidate manufacturers and suppliers for the water, heating, power, cart/trailer, and food components.
- [ ] Request specifications, warranties, maintenance requirements, recalls, service obligations, and limitations of liability.
- [ ] Require ingredient lists, allergen declarations, cross-contact information, lot/batch identifiers, storage rules, and shelf life.
- [ ] Design a paper and phone-accessible ingredient receipt.
- [ ] Establish a rule that packets are not distributed when required information is missing or uncertain.
- [ ] Define batch tracking, complaint handling, incident reporting, correction, and recall procedures.
- [ ] Minimize personal data and define retention, access, and deletion practices.

### Stage 4 — Organizational and Contracting Structure

- [ ] Compare operation through an established nonprofit, fiscal sponsor, independent contractor, employee team, or volunteer team.
- [ ] Draft a responsibility matrix for the sponsor, operators, property owner, suppliers, and participants.
- [ ] Develop manufacturer/equipment agreements and warranty review questions.
- [ ] Develop food-supplier agreements and delivery/temperature requirements.
- [ ] Develop site-use, event, insurance, indemnity, and cleanup provisions.
- [ ] Review volunteer protections and worker-classification issues with qualified counsel.
- [ ] Define authority to stop service when water, power, sanitation, equipment, or staffing is inadequate.

### Stage 5 — Site and Community Engagement

- [ ] Identify candidate sites, including Gateway Plaza and other approved public or private locations.
- [ ] Distinguish property-owner permission from city/county permits and event approvals.
- [ ] Consult nearby businesses and residents about sound, crowding, access, cleanup, and operating hours.
- [ ] Engage nonprofits serving unhoused people and people with disabilities.
- [ ] Develop a low-disruption acoustic-music policy.
- [ ] Prepare a short concept brief and questions for agencies and potential sponsors.

### Stage 6 — Controlled Pilot Preparation

- [ ] Start with the smallest lawful and technically manageable service set.
- [ ] Create a written opening checklist, service checklist, closing checklist, and emergency stop procedure.
- [ ] Train operators in food safety, ingredient communication, respectful assistance, and incident documentation.
- [ ] Test the receipt/label process with people who have different literacy, language, sensory, and technology needs.
- [ ] Run a non-public equipment and workflow test before serving the public.
- [ ] Secure all required permissions, insurance, inspections, and site agreements before operation.

### Stage 7 — Evaluation and Revision

- [ ] Track water and food checks, packets served, ingredient questions, incidents, waste, costs, and cleanup.
- [ ] Collect voluntary feedback without making participation dependent on personal-data disclosure.
- [ ] Evaluate whether the service supports choice and dignity without increasing risk.
- [ ] Review supplier performance, equipment reliability, and operator workload.
- [ ] Decide whether to revise, pause, expand, or discontinue the pilot.
- [ ] Preserve the results and unresolved questions in APO.

## Open Questions

- What exact service configuration can be permitted at the proposed site?
- Can the atmospheric water system meet the applicable potable-water requirements?
- Which food formats minimize complexity while preserving participant choice?
- What is the appropriate nonprofit or fiscal-sponsor structure?
- What insurance and contractual protections are proportionate to the pilot?
- How can people without phones receive the same essential information?
- What is the minimum viable service that can be operated safely and consistently?
