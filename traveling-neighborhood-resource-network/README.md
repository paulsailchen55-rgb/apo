# Traveling Neighborhood Resource Network

Status: Potential project / marketplace prototype / legal and safety research.

Guiding principle: **Preserve the possibility before deciding what it is.**

## Concept

A distributed network could connect travelers and participating neighbors or property participants for very short, explicitly permitted use of small resources. Examples discussed in the originating conversation include overnight parking, a place to sleep, water refill, vehicle washing, restroom access, and limited vehicle-work resources.

The user imagined a person traveling by car, van, bicycle/trike, or small camper and moving from one participating location to another rather than remaining in one place for a long period.

The project is not defined as “Airbnb for homeless people.” It is better described as a proposed network for **short, accountable, low-cost access to private or community resources**, with a particular interest in people who may otherwise have difficulty finding lawful temporary places to stay.

## What is known versus unresolved

Known from the conversation:
- The concept comes from the user's experience of being homeless and wishing for a legitimate, affordable way to pass through communities.
- One- or two-night stays were discussed as a product-design preference.
- Around $50 or less was discussed as an example of affordability, not a final price.
- Privacy, host safety, lease restrictions, mortgage terms, insurance, and local rules were identified as major constraints.

Not established:
- That a particular stay length is universally legal.
- That any network can automatically eliminate host liability.
- That a shared insurance pool can legally operate across states without additional structure.
- That the network would reduce crime, homelessness, or neighborhood damage.
- That existing services do not already provide some or all of these functions.

## Design objective

Create a lawful, privacy-conscious pathway for short-term participation while preserving host and traveler agency and making restrictions visible before a transaction occurs.

## Core design rule

The app should never tell a host, “You are allowed to do this,” merely because a form was completed. It should identify what must be checked and route unresolved legal, contractual, insurance, and safety questions for human review.

## Relationship to other apo projects

This project is related to WAYFINDER, Lease Harm Analysis, Property Access and Community Title, Community Hygiene/Laundry/Meal Access, and Personal Agency Infrastructure, but it remains separate from each.

WAYFINDER is primarily an information/verification layer. This project would add property participation, booking, permissions, transactions, and risk controls.

## Immediate research sequence

1. Define resource categories.
2. Build a city/county/state jurisdiction matrix.
3. Separate owner, renter, manager, and other property-authority cases.
4. Identify lease, mortgage, insurance, zoning, parking, camping, occupancy, and environmental questions.
5. Design privacy and anti-abuse controls.
6. Identify appropriate insurance/legal structures.
7. Prototype the smallest non-transactional app flow.
8. Only then consider a controlled pilot.
