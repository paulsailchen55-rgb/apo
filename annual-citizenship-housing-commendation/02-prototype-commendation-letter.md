# Prototype Commendation Letter

**Subject: A Commendation of Public Service, Community, and the Path to Housing**

Dear [Name],

I am writing to express my gratitude for the part you have played in my life and in the wider community. I want to recognize not only your individual work, but also the network of people, programs, institutions, and neighbors through which I eventually found a place to live.

At 48 years old, after a lifetime of intermittent homelessness and housing instability in California and elsewhere, I have reached a point I once could not be certain I would experience. I have now remained housed for nearly two years and have renewed my lease. I do not need to establish the exact dates or every detail of the journey to recognize the significance of this arrangement and the people who helped make it possible.

I want to describe this experience as honestly as I can. Reaching stable housing was not a simple or guaranteed process. There were difficult circumstances, setbacks, uncertainty, and experiences that challenged my ability to remain safe, stable, and connected to the community. Moving through public systems and learning how to live within a new housing arrangement brought challenges of its own.

Nevertheless, a pathway existed.

Through different circumstances, I came into contact with public servants, probation and court-related systems, substance-use-disorder services, mental-health-related services, sober-living arrangements, shelter and housing resources, caseworkers, community organizations, and people who helped me continue moving forward. Organizations and programs such as Encompass, Evolving Door, the Homeless Garden Project, libraries, parks, and other community resources have been part of my broader experience of reconnecting with people, responsibility, work, and community.

I recognize that each program and individual has a specific role, and I do not want to misrepresent the responsibilities of anyone involved. My purpose is to acknowledge the larger network of assistance through which a person can move from homelessness and instability toward housing. I experienced portions of that network myself, and I eventually reached a place where I could sign a lease and remain housed.

To me, this is something worthy of serious gratitude.

I also want to recognize that people experiencing homelessness are not separate from society. They are our neighbors, residents, workers, family members, and fellow participants in the life of a community. Their circumstances may be difficult to understand from the outside, but they remain connected to the same civic systems, public resources, land, institutions, and responsibilities that affect everyone else.

My own life has brought me into contact with many different communities. I have worked with my hands, participated in construction and building activities since childhood, helped with community work, and taken part in cleanup, environmental, gardening, and other activities. Through experiences such as these, I have come to believe that housing is not only a structure or an agreement. It is also a relationship between a person, a place, a community, and the systems that support everyday life.

I believe that public service can be understood as a form of service to our neighbors. My relationship with God shapes how I understand that responsibility. I believe that God can work through ordinary people, public servants, institutions, and practical acts of care. When I thank the people who helped me, I am also expressing gratitude to God as I understand God.

I recognize that not everyone shares my religious beliefs or understands the highest authority in the same way. My intention is not to impose a religious interpretation on anyone. Rather, I want to offer appreciation in a way that respects both personal faith and the civic life we share. We may have different beliefs, but we can still recognize the importance of helping our neighbors and maintaining a community in which people have the opportunity to live with dignity.

The tradition I have been reflecting on is the practice of recognizing people individually for their work, service, and participation in a larger community. I think of the letters of Paul, particularly the way he acknowledged many different people and their contributions rather than presenting the work of a community as the achievement of one individual. I would like to carry something of that tradition into the present—not by claiming equivalence with the past, but by applying the basic practice of commendation to the people and institutions around me.

I am writing as one person who has passed through a difficult period and has experienced a meaningful change. I want to say, in effect: thank you, neighbor. Thank you for the work you have done. Thank you for the responsibilities you have carried. Thank you for participating in a network that helped me reach a more stable part of my life.

I also hope that my experience can contribute to constructive thinking about the future of housing.

My background in construction has influenced how I think about buildings and the people who inhabit them. I believe housing should be more than something people are placed inside. Wherever practical, it should be understandable, durable, repairable, adaptable, and designed in a way that allows residents to maintain a meaningful degree of participation and responsibility.

I am interested in smaller and potentially modular forms of housing, locally appropriate materials, practical maintenance, and designs that do not leave people dependent on distant systems for every repair or replacement. I believe we should consider not only how a building is constructed, but also how its materials will be maintained, reused, removed, or responsibly returned to the surrounding environment when its useful life ends.

This reflects a broader principle I have been developing: when we build or use something, we should consider its proper and honorable end. What we bring into a place should have a responsible pathway for removal, reuse, or restoration. The ability to take something apart and remove it responsibly can be just as important as the ability to put it together.

I do not present these thoughts as a complete solution or as a criticism of every existing housing program. I understand that safety, funding, professional expertise, regulations, and institutional responsibilities are real considerations. I hope, however, that residents, builders, public agencies, and communities can continue exploring ways to create housing that supports dignity, practical knowledge, participation, and long-term stability.

My experience has also made me more aware of the importance of public understanding. Many people do not know how the various systems connected to homelessness, behavioral health, probation, shelters, housing, and community services relate to one another. I believe that clear and accessible explanations of these pathways could help residents better understand how their government and community organizations work.

This is part of why I am interested in documenting these systems separately from this letter. The purpose would be to make the civic pathways more visible and understandable, while respecting the privacy and dignity of the people who move through them.

The letter itself is not intended to be a complete report. It is a personal commendation.

I want to recognize the people who helped create a pathway where I could eventually find housing. I want to acknowledge that the process involved many different individuals and organizations, some of whom may never know the full extent of their connection to the outcome. I want to encourage continued support for programs that help people move toward stability. And I want to express hope that the systems we build together can become increasingly accessible, understandable, humane, and effective.

I am grateful to be living in a community where these pathways exist. I am grateful for the opportunity to continue learning, working, participating, and contributing. I do not take the arrangement of stable housing for granted, and I recognize that many people are still seeking the stability that I have been able to experience.

Thank you for the part you have played in my life and in the community. Whether your contribution was direct or part of the larger network, I want you to know that I recognize the value of public service, community work, and the people who help others move forward.

May we continue to serve our neighbors, care for the places in which we live, and develop systems that allow more people to find stability, responsibility, dignity, and belonging.

With gratitude,

**Paul Statchen**
Santa Cruz, California
