# Information Archaeology and Information Half-Life

Classification: research direction / potential project.

This archive emerged from an everyday observation about YouTube advertising, LED lighting, GoDaddy, domain-registration horizons, search-engine discoverability, and possible disappearance of older public information.

The project does not assume censorship, suppression, retaliation, or deliberate disappearance. Its starting point is testable: information can remain technically online while becoming difficult to discover, verify, or recover through ordinary search.

Core question: How long does publicly available information remain accessible, searchable, verifiable, and independently recoverable?

Model: existence -> accessibility -> searchability -> discoverability -> preservation -> independent recoverability.

A page can survive at one layer while effectively disappearing at another.

Proposed study: construct historical corpora around selected subjects and compare successive periods. Measure whether older material remains easy to locate and independently corroborate. Potential measurements include whether the original URL still resolves, whether an archived copy exists, whether ordinary search retrieves it, how many search steps are required, whether only secondary references remain, whether multiple independent copies survive, and whether access barriers or newer material affect recovery.

Evidence discipline: distinguish observation, documented fact, interpretation, hypothesis, allegation, and unresolved question. Failure to find an old page is not evidence by itself that somebody intentionally removed it.

LED lighting test case: ask whether older public claims and scientific evidence about LED lighting, temporal light modulation/flicker, visual effects, circadian effects, or other proposed health effects can be recovered today and how present discoverability compares with historical availability. The archive does not establish that LED/PWM exposure causes long-term injury. Scientific claims require primary or authoritative literature.

Relationship to existing apo work: related to internet-complexity-voice-and-knowledge-access, but separate. That package studies contemporary cost and complexity of accessing information; this package studies historical survival and discoverability. It also connects to personal-agency-infrastructure because provenance can preserve an information trail, but it is not the same architecture.

Status: no search engine, archive corpus, statistical result, or causal explanation has yet been established here. This is a research design preserved from the conversation.
