# Santa Cruz Network — Present-Tense Relational Research

**Archive status:** exploratory / reference package  
**Purpose:** preserve a research method and conversation context for understanding local networks without turning historical associations into permanent present-day labels.

## Scope

This folder grew from research into [PERSON_042], Mike Brodkin, a 2019 Santa Cruz political-organizing list, and related questions about how to approach living people and networks in the present.

It is **not** a roster, endorsement list, political recommendation, or authoritative account of anyone's current beliefs. Historical material is retained as historical material.

## Core principle

> **Use the past to find the present, rather than making the past govern the future.**

A historical list can show who was publicly associated with a particular effort at a particular time. It does not, by itself, establish a person's present identity, beliefs, relationships, availability, or role.

The archive should therefore distinguish:

- documented fact
- historical self-description
- current public information
- interpretation
- hypothesis
- unresolved question

## Relational research model

When researching a person for possible present-day contact, record only information that has a legitimate purpose for that relationship and is appropriately public.

Suggested fields:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Present identity | What does the person publicly do now? |
| Historical identity | What did they identify as or participate in at the time being studied? |
| Organizations | What documented organizational connections are relevant? |
| Political/philosophical commitments | What principles have they publicly articulated, and when? |
| Economic/social views | What documented positions are relevant to the relationship? |
| Religious/spiritual worldview | Is there voluntarily public, relevant information? |
| Local relationships | What documented collaborations or disagreements are relevant? |
| Current relevance | Why does contact make sense now? |
| Evidence date | When was the information verified? |
| Status | Known / inferred / unknown |
| Sunset/review date | When should the information be reconsidered or allowed to expire? |

### Known / inferred / unknown

Example:

- **Known:** [PERSON_042] and [PERSON_071] discussed identifying as socialist-feminists in a 1979 interview conducted after their election to Santa Cruz City Council.
- **Known:** The interview discusses local organizing, housing, feminism, socialism, and municipal politics in that historical context.
- **Unknown:** What either person currently believes about those subjects.
- **Not justified without current evidence:** treating a 1979 political identity as a 2026 identity.

## Anonymization / dignity principle

This package intentionally avoids constructing an unnecessarily comprehensive dossier on living people.

Names may be retained when they are necessary to preserve the provenance of the historical research, but the archive should prefer:

1. public, purpose-relevant information;
2. current verification rather than stale assumptions;
3. descriptions of relationships and documented actions rather than ideological labels;
4. explicit uncertainty where evidence is incomplete;
5. expiration, review, or deletion of information when its practical purpose ends.

The goal is not to make a permanent list of people. The goal is to understand enough context to communicate respectfully in the present.

## Philosophical note: equilibrium

The originating conversation used **"equilibriumist"** as a personal working term for an approach that seeks balance between masculine and feminine energies rather than treating either as inherently dominant. It also expressed a willingness to distinguish economic or political labels from the values and practices underneath them.

This is preserved here as a **personal philosophical concept**, not as an established political category or a claim about other people's beliefs.

The relevant methodological implication is: do not assume that disagreement over a label prevents respectful relationship, and do not assume agreement over a label establishes substantive agreement.

## 2019 recall-list provenance

The originating research referenced a 2019 Facebook post in the "Santa Cruz Politicos" group that appeared to contain names associated with opposition to the 2019–2020 Santa Cruz City Council recall effort.

Because the supplied Facebook link was a Google redirect rather than a directly inspectable post, the original list should be treated as **provenance for further research, not as a verified roster**. Individual inclusion, current status, and current relevance should be independently verified before use.

## Archive lifecycle

This folder is intentionally not a finished project. It preserves a research direction and a method.

If this work later becomes an active contact-management or civic-research project, that project should establish its own data-retention rules rather than treating this archive as a permanent people database.

The archive principle remains:

> **Preserve the possibility before deciding what it is.**
