# 02 — Transient Connectivity and Information Pathways

## Research question

Can a dynamical system containing multiple locally distinct regions enter a transient state in which its flow topology establishes a continuous pathway between otherwise separated regions?

A narrower information-theory question is:

> What physical conditions are required for information to propagate between two regions that are locally separated but dynamically coupled through a larger system?

## Transport versus information

Moving a physical object is a transport problem. Propagating a distinguishable signal is an information problem.

The relevant question is not necessarily whether the entire source region travels to the destination. It may instead be whether a state change in A becomes detectable or recoverable in B.

Let:

- A = first region;
- B = second region;
- S_A(t), S_B(t) = their states;
- C(t) = coupling;
- P(t) = pathway availability;
- I(A→B) = recoverable information;
- D = transmission distortion;
- T = duration of usable connectivity.

A transient pathway could have P(t) > 0 only during a finite interval.

## Wormhole comparison

Traversable wormholes are theoretical spacetime constructions. They can be used as a comparison case for unusual causal or geometric connections, but the sink experiment is not evidence for them.

The common abstraction under investigation is:

SEPARATED REGIONS → COUPLING → PATHWAY → TRANSMISSION → ALTERED STATE

That abstraction can be compared across fluid dynamics, information theory, networks, and spacetime physics without asserting that the mechanisms are identical.
