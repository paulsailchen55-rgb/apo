# Concept Notes — 2026-09-18

## Origin of the concept

The concept emerged during a coastal walk after observing discarded plastic and other manufactured waste. The idea is to investigate whether existing energy, refrigeration, freezing, water-treatment, vessel, sensor, and communications technologies could be combined to provide temporary protection for vulnerable ocean environments.

## Concept A: Localized cooling and floating ice

A mobile or stationary system could potentially use electricity, recovered waste heat, or another energy source to create cooled water or ice near coral reefs experiencing thermal stress. The intended function would be a temporary protective zone while broader climate and ecological solutions are developed.

Questions requiring investigation:

- Whether the cooling effect could reach a biologically meaningful area.
- Whether ice would remain near the target ecosystem or disperse.
- Whether freezing would change salinity, brine chemistry, dissolved oxygen, pH, or nutrient distribution.
- Whether the cooling would benefit coral while harming other organisms or disrupting seasonal cycles.
- Whether the energy and emissions required would outweigh the benefit.
- Whether a mobile system could be safely operated during storms and strong currents.
- Whether the intervention could be ended and removed without leaving persistent impacts.

## Concept B: River-mouth and coastal water treatment

A second concept involves mobile vessels or other platforms near major river outlets, including the Mississippi River and Gulf Coast, to capture or treat pollutants before they spread into marine environments.

The term "pollution" must be divided into specific categories before engineering can be evaluated:

- Nutrients and agricultural runoff.
- Sediment and suspended solids.
- Pathogens.
- Metals.
- Pesticides and industrial chemicals.
- Petroleum-related compounds.
- Plastic fragments and microplastics.

A treatment system would need to identify which substances it removes, where those substances go, how they are stored, and how secondary waste is handled.

## Concept C: Freezing-based plastic separation

The working hypothesis is that freezing may cause some materials to separate from water or become concentrated in a remaining liquid or ice fraction. This must be investigated substance by substance. Freezing should not be assumed to remove all plastics or make contaminated ice safe.

Research should examine:

- The behavior of different plastic types during freezing and thawing.
- Microplastic movement and concentration.
- The effects of salinity and dissolved organic matter.
- Whether pollutants become concentrated in brine, ice, or sediment.
- Collection and containment methods.
- Safe processing, recycling, disposal, or energy recovery.
- Risks of producing fuel from collected plastic, including emissions and toxic residues.

## Concept D: Mobile monitoring and coordination

Possible supporting systems include sensors, databases, vessel-based equipment, remote monitoring, and satellite communications. These systems would need clear data governance, maintenance plans, cybersecurity protections, emergency shutdown procedures, and transparent reporting.

A database should not be treated as the intervention itself. It would support monitoring, comparison, documentation, and decision-making.

## Research safeguards

The project should distinguish among:

- Direct observation.
- Established scientific findings.
- Engineering feasibility.
- Speculative extrapolation.
- Cultural or spiritual interpretation.
- Policy proposal.

No field deployment should occur without regulatory approval, environmental review, independent scientific evaluation, community consultation, and a plan for failure, removal, and long-term monitoring.

## Desired long-term purpose

The purpose is not to permanently control ocean processes or replace emissions reduction, watershed protection, habitat restoration, or pollution prevention. The desired question is whether a temporary, reversible intervention could reduce immediate harm while more durable solutions are developed.
