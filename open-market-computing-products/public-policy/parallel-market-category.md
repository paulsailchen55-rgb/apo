# Parallel Market Category

## Concept

The central proposal is a parallel-market model.

A computing manufacturer may continue to sell proprietary, fully integrated products while also offering a product specifically designed to remain open to independent development.

## Two categories

### Closed commercial product

The manufacturer controls the integrated software and protects proprietary technology.

### Open-market product

The manufacturer sells a platform intentionally designed for independent modification and development, with clearly defined hardware and software openness.

## Why the categories can coexist

The two products address different customer needs.

Some customers want a finished product that simply works.

Others want a machine they can study, modify, repair, program, or replace the software on.

The existence of the second category does not require the first category to disappear.

## Key policy issue

The challenge is defining what "open" means sufficiently well that a manufacturer cannot satisfy the policy merely by adding a superficial developer feature while retaining the restrictions that prevent meaningful independent development.

The research should therefore establish functional criteria rather than relying on marketing language.

## Possible criteria for study

- owner modification rights;
- firmware replacement or meaningful modification;
- bootloader access;
- hardware documentation;
- driver/interface documentation;
- repair documentation;
- development tools;
- open reference software;
- independent software installation;
- documented security boundaries;
- documented safety boundaries;
- availability of replacement parts where relevant.

These are research candidates, not final legal requirements.

## Status

Foundational policy concept.
