# Discussion Notes

## 2026-09-22 — Project Intent Clarification

### User intention

The project is for people who have already chosen dome- or yurt-based housing. It is not intended to persuade people that domes are superior.

### Central direction

Make the pathway as easy as possible through AI, bots, computers, databases, websites, internet resources, libraries, books, agency phone numbers, and clear step-by-step guidance.

### Values

- Honor past generations and relevant housing traditions.
- Support future generations.
- Preserve user choice.
- Reduce unnecessary administrative complexity.
- Support individuals, families, people with disabilities, older adults, nonprofits, cooperatives, and communities.
- Cover tiny dwellings through large homes and connected dome/yurt communities.

### Design interpretation

The tool should answer “What is the easiest responsible next step?” rather than “Should you choose this type of housing?”

## 2026-09-22 — Materials and Omega Principle Clarification

### User intention

The pathway must not treat every commercially available dome or yurt as equivalent. The user specifically wants to minimize newly installed plastic and other fossil-derived or difficult-to-manage materials in dome and yurt housing.

The project is intended to help people pursue natural, repairable, responsibly sourced, and life-cycle-conscious dome/yurt construction using infrastructure available now, while preserving structural, fire, seismic, moisture, accessibility, health, and code requirements.

### Important distinction

Commercial products that already have an established installation pathway are not automatically the intended endpoint of this project. The project should investigate alternatives rather than simply route applicants toward premanufactured plastic-heavy products.

### Omega Principle relationship

The Omega Principle is treated here as a design and evaluation direction: useful life, maintenance, repair, reuse, recovery, and end-of-life handling must be considered before installation. Future concepts such as the Phoenix Project and Dragonfly Project remain PROPOSED and must not be assumed to exist or justify additional difficult-to-manage materials today.

### Research consequence

Material research must begin at the whole-building life-cycle level, not only at purchase price or ease of permitting. Each candidate assembly should be examined for material origin, petrochemical content, toxicity, fire and seismic performance, weather durability, repairability, local maintenance, disassembly, reuse, recycling, biodegradation or safe disposal, transportation, embodied impacts, and jurisdictional acceptance.

### Evidence discipline

The project must not claim that a material is safe, natural, recyclable, biodegradable, fire-resistant, or code-compliant without source-backed verification for the specific product, assembly, climate, hazard condition, and jurisdiction.
