# Civic Letter: An Invitation to Imagine What Comes Next

Status: draft civic outreach artifact.

I am writing as an American citizen from Santa Cruz, California, with a simple request: please pause—not to stop progress, but to make room for a better idea.

What if the answer is not a better version of what we are already doing?

When we look at ocean resources and the activities built around them, it is easy to become focused on limits: how much impact can be permitted, mitigated, regulated, measured, or compensated for. Those questions matter. But there is another question that may deserve more room.

What if we asked what we could invent instead?

Consider something as ordinary as a ship’s propeller. We can ask how to make a propeller quieter, more efficient, or less disruptive. But we could also ask: what could move a ship if we were not starting with a propeller?

That kind of question changes the conversation. It moves from defending an existing standard to exploring a new one.

Ocean extraction, transportation, energy use, waste, plastics, and other human activities are not isolated from one another. The ocean is connected to the systems around it. I am not claiming to have all the answers, and I am not asking anyone to accept an unsupported conclusion.

I am asking whether we have given ourselves enough freedom to look for something genuinely different.

The people receiving this letter have access to scientists, engineers, universities, capital, manufacturing capability, public institutions, and practical experience that an individual citizen does not have. Those resources could be used not only to improve existing systems, but to imagine systems that make some old tradeoffs unnecessary.

Instead of asking only, “What are we allowed to keep doing?” we could also ask, “What can we build that makes the old way unnecessary?”

The longer we postpone asking fundamentally different questions, the more expensive and difficult those questions may become.

We may not be able to change everything that has already happened. But we can decide what direction we take from here.

Please do not only ask what we have to give up. Ask what we have not yet imagined.

Paul Statchenn
Santa Cruz, California
