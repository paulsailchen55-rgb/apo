# AI, Human, and Local Information Infrastructure

People may submit links, flyers, observations, event details, needs, and corrections through a simple web form, browser share action, bookmarklet, QR code, SMS/voice route, public computer, or embeddable HTML widget.

AI can extract dates, times, locations, requirements, and categories; detect missing fields; group duplicates; prepare calendar entries; translate or simplify text; and flag records for review. Humans should verify sensitive, ambiguous, high-impact, or disputed information.

The system should prefer direct submissions, public APIs, feeds, structured data, and permission-friendly access. It must not bypass authentication, access controls, paywalls, or technical protections. It should identify its crawler, respect published crawling instructions, use reasonable request rates, preserve source URLs, and provide correction and removal pathways.

The system should treat robots.txt as a crawling instruction mechanism, not as a complete legal permission document. Copyright, terms of use, privacy, contractual restrictions, and applicable law require separate review. The prototype should begin with sources that expressly permit reuse or provide structured public data.
