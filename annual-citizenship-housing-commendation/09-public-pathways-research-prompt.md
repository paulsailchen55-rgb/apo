# Public Pathways from Homelessness to Housing

## Santa Cruz City and County, 2020-Present

Research how ordinary people may move through public institutions, government programs, nonprofits, and community services when experiencing homelessness, incarceration, substance-use concerns, mental-health involvement, probation, shelter placement, and eventual housing.

Use official City of Santa Cruz, County of Santa Cruz, California, federal, court/probation, public-health, behavioral-health, housing, homelessness-response, and publicly funded nonprofit sources.

Research possible pathways rather than assuming one sequence. Identify routes, eligibility, referrals, agencies, program names, roles, transitions, funding, barriers, delays, and reentry points.

Investigate:
1. unsheltered homelessness and initial contact;
2. outreach, assessment, shelter, and housing navigation;
3. law enforcement, incarceration, courts, probation, mental-health and substance-use pathways;
4. sober living, transitional, supportive, and permanent housing connections;
5. roles of caseworkers, probation officers, clinicians, outreach workers, housing managers, and public/nonprofit employees;
6. federal, state, county, and city funding;
7. movement between agencies/programs;
8. gaps, barriers, delays, and reentry;
9. existing flowcharts, program maps, reports, evaluations, and directories;
10. accessible civic flowcharts.

Distinguish official procedures, eligibility rules, program goals, reported outcomes, individual experiences, interpretations, and unknowns.

Do not disclose private personal information or reconstruct confidential medical, legal, or case-management records.

Final paper: executive summary; agency/program directory; possible pathways; public links; glossary; flowcharts; gaps and unanswered questions; relation of the author's experience to documented systems; bibliography.

Goal: civic understanding, public transparency, and recognition of the people and systems involved in helping residents move from homelessness toward housing. Informative rather than accusatory; preserve dignity and agency.
