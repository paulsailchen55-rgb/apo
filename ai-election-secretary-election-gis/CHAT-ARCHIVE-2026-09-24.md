# Conversation Archive — AI Election Secretary / Election GIS

Date: 2026-09-24

## Origin

The conversation began by searching for an Android AI note-taking/secretary application that could accept text, Markdown, PDF, conversations, and ideas; develop rough ideas into fuller ideas; and publish or route work to websites, applications, social media, and other destinations.

The discussion then shifted from finding a single app to designing an **AI Secretary / AI Router**.

## Key transition

Instead of asking:

> Which AI should I use to decide how to vote?

the concept became:

> Build an AI Election Secretary that discovers what a person is actually being asked to decide, creates a research brief, gathers evidence, performs adversarial research, compares sources, maps relationships, and routes specialized tasks to the appropriate AI or tool.

The human remains the decision-maker.

## Research architecture developed

### Discovery
Determine every actual contest, measure, judicial question, district decision, and applicable ballot item.

### Briefing
Create a concise executive brief plus expandable detail. The brief is not restricted to one physical page.

### Evidence collection
Create a separate evidence dossier containing claims, primary sources, independent sources, calculations, dates, disputed claims, and unknowns.

### Adversarial research
Explicitly search for the strongest evidence that could challenge the current interpretation.

### Source comparison
Show what different sources say, what is directly documented, what is inferred, what is contested, and what remains unknown.

### Candidate dossier
Candidates are treated as research objects rather than simple biographies.

### Relational candidate map
Candidates can be represented as layers over geographic/institutional terrain, similar conceptually to a GIS. Layers can include office, jurisdiction, policy positions, legislation, votes, organizations, donors, endorsements, statements, affected populations, and evidence.

### Candidate side-by-side visualization
Two candidates can be placed on the same map and their decision trees/relationships can grow outward from the relevant jurisdiction.

The visual metaphor may resemble land growing, changing, burning, eroding, or being restored, but visual states must represent research relationships or uncertainty rather than declare a political winner.

### Ballot decision tree
The election should become a tree of actual human decisions:
- people
- statewide measures
- federal choices
- state choices
- county choices
- city choices
- district choices

### Three circles
1. My ballot — what I can vote on.
2. My government — who governs me even when not up this cycle.
3. My environment — decisions that affect my community even when I cannot vote on them.

The crucial distinction is:

**VOTABLE BY ME** versus **RELEVANT TO ME**.

## Santa Cruz 2026 context

The user is researching the November 3, 2026 California General Election as a Santa Cruz-area registered voter.

Officials discussed during the conversation included Justin Cummings, Sonja Brunner, Gail Pellerin, John Laird, Adam Schiff, and Jimmy Panetta. The project must verify each office, district, term, and actual 2026 ballot status rather than assuming that a current officeholder is a current candidate choice.

The conversation identified 14 statewide propositions certified for the November 3, 2026 ballot and Santa Cruz County local measures requiring district-specific verification.

## Global extension

The user explicitly expanded the concept beyond U.S. elections to elections worldwide, including single-party systems.

The system therefore needs a comparative layer that asks not merely "who is on the ballot?" but:
- What is the electoral institution?
- What choices are actually available?
- How competitive is the contest?
- What information is available?
- Who administers the election?
- What constraints exist on opposition and political participation?
- What does participation mean in this political system?
- What functions does the election serve?
- What can the voter or citizen actually influence?

The archive must not assume that every election is a competitive democratic transfer-of-power mechanism.

## Why this matters

A system capable of deeply researching elections could itself become politically consequential.

The project therefore needs safeguards against:
- hidden persuasion
- recommendation masquerading as research
- selective evidence
- personalization designed to manipulate
- fabricated citations
- model hallucinations
- source laundering
- automated political targeting
- concentration of political research power
- opaque model routing
- privacy loss
- permanent political profiles
- automated publishing without human review

The project should preserve the human's ability to inspect the path from source -> analysis -> synthesis -> decision.
