# Identity, Presence, and Renewal

## Project status

This is a research and design inquiry arising from a September 19, 2026 examination of online identity, search discoverability, privacy, persistence, maintenance, and renewal.

It began with a practical experiment: examining an existing About.me profile as a possible public identity hub and asking how a person can remain discoverable across search engines and online services without making social media the foundation of identity.

The inquiry has expanded into a broader question about human identity and digital identity.

## Central question

How can a person maintain a persistent, portable, discoverable Internet identity that can be recognized across search engines and participating services while preserving appropriate anonymity, privacy, accountability, and control?

## Deeper question

Who am I, who is my neighbor, and how do we find one another when we need one another?

## Working premise

Identity is not only an identifier. It is also presence: a way for a person to be found, understood, contacted, helped, held responsible when appropriate, and connected to a body of work.

The project distinguishes identity, presence, discovery, authentication, privacy, anonymity, accountability, portability, and persistence rather than assuming they must all be provided by one platform or one tracking system.

## Why this is a global question

The Internet and major online services operate across national and cultural boundaries. The problem therefore cannot be reduced to one person's account setup or to one country's social-media environment. The practical experiment is personal, but the underlying question is international.

## The practical experiment

The September 2026 experiment is to build a coherent public identity around a person and their projects using:

- search-discoverable public pages;
- a personal identity/profile page;
- project repositories and archives;
- selected professional or identity-profile services where useful;
- direct links between authoritative representations;
- privacy-preserving and temporary accounts where appropriate.

The experiment does not assume that LinkedIn, Facebook, About.me, or any other platform is the correct permanent answer.

## Identity as a lifecycle

A digital identity may be created, used, maintained, corrected, renewed, rested, archived, retired, or removed.

Persistence is not automatically good, and disappearance is not automatically bad. The appropriate lifecycle depends on the purpose of the identity, responsibilities attached to it, privacy requirements, and the period for which the identity remains meaningful.

## Natural cycles and timing

An open research question is whether recurring features of physical reality—day and night, seasons, biological cycles, aging, decay, regeneration, and other observable cycles—can provide common reference points for designing periods of use, rest, maintenance, renewal, and retirement.

The project does not assume that one cultural calendar must become universal. It asks whether different calendars can be understood as representations of recurring realities that provide some common ground.

## Religious and philosophical provenance

The September 19 inquiry was undertaken as a deliberate period of rest, reflection, cleaning, renewal, and examination. The user's religious framing includes Sabbath, sanctification, blessing, love of God, and love of neighbor.

Those ideas are part of the provenance of the inquiry. The technical and civic investigation remains open to people of different faiths and to people without a religious framework.

## Evidence boundary

This archive distinguishes personal observation, philosophical premise, historical hypothesis, technical proposal, and externally verified fact. Historical examples of cleansing, disposal, bodily remnants, clothing, writing, and ritual practice require separate research before being presented as established historical claims.
