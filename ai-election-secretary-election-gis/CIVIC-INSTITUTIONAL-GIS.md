# CIVIC INSTITUTIONAL GIS

Status: PROPOSED / RESEARCH CONCEPT

## Purpose

Extend Election GIS into a time-aware map of geography, authority, institutions, people, programs, funding, property, decisions, and outcomes.

## Core model

A civic system is larger than its elected officials:

VOTERS -> ELECTED OFFICIALS -> APPOINTMENTS / ADMINISTRATORS -> DEPARTMENTS / BOARDS -> CONTRACTS / FUNDING / REGULATION -> PROGRAMS / SERVICE PROVIDERS -> RESIDENTS

Feedback also matters:

OUTCOMES -> REPORTS / EVALUATION -> BUDGETS / RECOMMENDATIONS -> FUTURE DECISIONS -> FUTURE PROGRAMS

## Layers

- GEOGRAPHY — districts, jurisdictions, properties, facilities, service areas
- AUTHORITY — legal, administrative, contracting, regulatory, and appointment authority
- PEOPLE — elected and appointed officials, administrators, staff, contractors, documented professional trajectories
- ORGANIZATIONS — agencies, nonprofits, contractors, vendors, related entities
- MONEY — taxes, grants, reimbursements, contracts, subsidies, loans, expenditures
- PROGRAMS — public services, housing, infrastructure, health, education, safety
- PROPERTY — ownership, leasing, development, rehabilitation, maintenance, inspection, compliance
- DECISIONS — laws, budgets, contracts, appointments, regulations, elections, measures
- TIME — dates, transitions, contract periods, property history, institutional change

## Time-aware trajectories

A static dossier asks:

> Where is this person or institution?

A time-aware map asks:

> Where did it come from, what changed, what relationships appeared or disappeared, and where did those changes lead?

Possible snapshots:

T0 -> T1 -> T2 -> T3 -> T4 -> T5

Changes should be represented without treating change itself as evidence of wrongdoing.

## Relationship types

Examples:

- employed by
- appointed by
- supervised by
- contracted with
- funded by
- regulated by
- owns
- leases
- provides services to
- reports to
- advises
- formerly worked for

A documented relationship is not automatically evidence of influence or conflict.

## Economic-flow layer

The map can follow:

FUNDING SOURCE -> CONTRACT / PROGRAM -> ORGANIZATION -> PEOPLE / PROPERTY / VENDORS / SERVICES -> LOCAL OR EXTERNAL DESTINATION -> DURABLE CAPACITY / CONSUMPTION / FUTURE COST

Questions include:

- What remains as durable local capacity?
- What supports local employment?
- What supports property and infrastructure?
- What goes to outside entities?
- What is restricted?
- What is administrative?
- What future public costs are avoided or created?

The purpose is descriptive analysis, not a predetermined conclusion about extraction.

## Institutional knowledge

Formal procedures do not necessarily contain all operational knowledge.

Experienced personnel may carry historical context, relationships, property history, procedural sequencing, exceptions, timing, and practical dependencies.

Therefore continuity can be treated as a systems variable:

- How is knowledge transferred when staff leave?
- Are critical processes cross-trained?
- Are handoffs documented?
- Are long-running regulatory, property, and service processes protected from turnover?
- Does funding support the personnel required for continuity?

## Sustainable Service Capacity Review

For publicly funded service systems, compare:

1. Contractual obligations
2. Actual documented cost
3. Required staffing
4. Property and infrastructure capacity
5. Regulatory and administrative burden
6. Available funding
7. Institutional-continuity capacity
8. Measured outcomes

The objective is to identify evidence-based capacity gaps rather than assume them.

## Election GIS connection

Election GIS supplies electoral territory:

- voter jurisdiction
- office jurisdiction
- measure jurisdiction
- district boundaries
- overlapping authorities

Civic Institutional GIS adds the machinery underneath:

- agencies
- appointments
- contracts
- funding
- programs
- property
- personnel
- time

Together they can ask:

> Which decision is being made, by whom, under what authority, involving which institutions, funded through what mechanisms, affecting which geography and population, and how did the present system get here?

## Evidence discipline

Use:

KNOWN / CALCULATED / TESTED / PROPOSED / SPECULATIVE / UNKNOWN

Also, where useful:

DOCUMENTED CLAIM / CONTESTED / UNVERIFIED

Do not silently convert:

relationship -> influence

or:

financial flow -> extraction.

## Privacy

Public project documentation should not expose an individual's exact address, housing or service-provider relationship, health information, personal finances, or inferred political preferences.

Local case studies should be anonymized unless identifying details are necessary and explicitly authorized.

## Human agency

The map should help people understand systems without:

- ranking political actors
- inferring voting preferences
- declaring hidden motives
- labeling relationships as corrupt without evidence
- substituting AI conclusions for human judgment

## Open questions

1. What minimum data model represents geography, authority, people, money, programs, property, and time?
2. How can relationship provenance be preserved?
3. How can historical records be reconciled without silently filling gaps?
4. How can financial flows be represented without confusing accounting categories?
5. How can institutional trajectories be shown without implying improper influence?
6. How can local economic retention be measured?
7. How can service capacity be compared with actual documented costs?
8. How should uncertainty propagate through a relational map?
9. How can the map remain useful without becoming a political profiling system?
10. Who should control the map and its underlying data?
