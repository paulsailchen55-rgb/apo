# Materials Screening Rubric

## Purpose

This rubric is an early screening tool for proposed materials and assemblies used in:

- New dome/yurt construction.
- Existing-home material transitions.
- Victorian, historic, Adobe, earthen, older, and contemporary buildings.
- Phased repair, retrofit, replacement, and redesign.

It is not engineering certification, legal advice, permit approval, hazardous-materials clearance, or a substitute for qualified professional review.

## Core principle

Evaluate a material before installation by examining both:

1. **Material identity:** What is it made of?
2. **Material burden:** What happens during maintenance, repair, removal, reuse, recovery, recycling, transport, and final disposal?

Do not automatically prohibit all synthetic materials or automatically prefer all natural materials. Evaluate the complete assembly, the building context, and the available evidence.

## Screening status

Each proposed material or assembly should receive one of these statuses:

- **PROCEED TO PROFESSIONAL REVIEW:** Evidence is sufficiently developed for the next design or permitting step.
- **CONDITIONAL:** Potentially workable, but specific risks, approvals, tests, or supply questions remain.
- **PAUSE AND INVESTIGATE:** Important information is missing or the burden is not yet acceptable.
- **REJECT FOR THIS APPLICATION:** The proposal fails a required safety, legal, environmental, accessibility, or end-of-life condition for the stated use.
- **UNKNOWN:** Evidence is insufficient to classify responsibly.

## Evidence categories

Record evidence using the project categories:

- **KNOWN:** Supported by reliable documentation or direct verification.
- **CALCULATED:** Derived from stated assumptions or transparent calculations.
- **TESTED:** Supported by relevant testing, inspection, or field performance.
- **PROPOSED:** A design or pathway not yet demonstrated.
- **SPECULATIVE:** A possibility requiring substantial validation.
- **UNKNOWN:** Information not currently established.

## Screening record

For every material or assembly, record:

- Project and property location.
- Building type, age, and historic or cultural status.
- Proposed use and exposure conditions.
- Exact product, manufacturer, formulation, and assembly details when available.
- Installation method and adjacent materials.
- Proposed action: retain, repair, clean, encapsulate, remove, substitute, redesign, or monitor.
- Evidence category and source date.
- Responsible reviewer or professional discipline.

## Evaluation domains

### 1. Material composition

- Natural, mineral, biological, synthetic, or mixed composition.
- Petrochemical or fossil-derived content.
- Resins, polymers, foams, laminates, coatings, sealants, adhesives, and binders.
- Additives, flame retardants, pigments, preservatives, and contaminants.
- Ability to identify and separate components at end of life.

### 2. Human health and exposure

- Indoor emissions and off-gassing.
- Dust, fibers, fumes, and worker exposure during installation or removal.
- Fire and combustion products.
- Hazardous constituents and required testing.
- Exposure during ordinary use, maintenance, demolition, and disposal.

### 3. Structural and building performance

- Structural capacity and connection behavior.
- Seismic performance and compatibility with the building.
- Fire resistance and wildfire exposure where relevant.
- Moisture movement, drainage, vapor behavior, mold risk, and drying potential.
- Wind, weathering, ultraviolet exposure, pests, and temperature cycling.
- Accessibility and safe use by occupants.

### 4. Repair and maintenance

- Can damaged parts be repaired individually?
- Are components replaceable without destroying adjacent materials?
- Are tools, skills, and replacement parts locally available?
- Is maintenance understandable to the owner?
- Are inspection intervals and failure indicators documented?
- Does repair extend service life or merely postpone disposal?

### 5. End-of-life pathway

Document the actual pathway, not just a marketing claim:

- On-site reuse.
- Salvage or donation.
- Reuse in another building.
- Source-separated recycling.
- Mixed-material processing.
- Manufacturer take-back.
- Specialized treatment.
- Landfill acceptance.
- Hazardous or special-waste requirements.
- Transport distance, handling, and cost.
- Facility confirmation date and contact.

A facility's acceptance must be verified for the specific material and condition. CalRecycle advises checking facility acceptance before delivery and notes that recycling opportunities can be limited. [CalRecycle C&D guidance](https://calrecycle.ca.gov/condemo/)

### 6. Lifecycle and environmental burden

- Expected service life.
- Embodied energy and emissions.
- Extraction and feedstock impacts.
- Transportation requirements.
- Maintenance materials and replacement frequency.
- Pollution during fire, weathering, degradation, or disposal.
- Reversibility and disassembly.
- Effects on soil, water, air, and surrounding materials.

### 7. Supply and market resilience

- Number and location of suppliers.
- Whether replacement parts are likely to remain available.
- Dependence on a single manufacturer or proprietary system.
- Local tradespeople and repair knowledge.
- Cost volatility and minimum order requirements.
- Availability of non-proprietary alternatives.
- Whether the proposed choice creates future dependence on a narrow supply chain.

### 8. Choice, time, and informed consent

Record whether the owner received:

- A plain-language explanation.
- The exact reason a material is being recommended or required.
- At least one feasible alternative when available.
- Estimated initial and lifecycle costs.
- Maintenance and end-of-life information.
- Enough time to review the decision.
- Access to an independent professional or second opinion.
- A written record distinguishing legal requirements from contractor or supplier preferences.

Urgency may be legitimate, but urgency should not erase documentation, explanation, or meaningful choice.

### 9. Code, permits, and professional review

- Identify the governing jurisdiction and current code edition.
- Distinguish mandatory requirements from guidance, preference, and customary practice.
- Verify whether the material or assembly has an accepted approval pathway.
- Identify required architect, engineer, preservation specialist, industrial hygienist, or other review.
- Record unresolved interpretations in writing.
- Do not treat this rubric as proof of compliance.

## Decision sequence

1. **Identify the building and intended use.**
2. **Inventory existing materials and conditions.**
3. **Define the proposed intervention.** Retain, repair, remove, substitute, redesign, or monitor.
4. **Identify the exact material or assembly.** Avoid evaluating an entire category from a generic product name.
5. **Check safety and code constraints first.**
6. **Compare at least one feasible alternative when available.**
7. **Verify maintenance and end-of-life pathways.**
8. **Document costs, uncertainty, and decision time.**
9. **Obtain required professional and jurisdictional review.**
10. **Record the decision and the conditions for revisiting it.**

## Stop conditions

Pause the decision when:

- The composition is unknown and the uncertainty affects health, fire, structural, or disposal risk.
- A supplier claims a material is required but cannot identify the governing requirement.
- The proposed assembly cannot be repaired or separated and no credible end-of-life pathway is documented.
- The material's fire, moisture, seismic, or indoor-air implications are unresolved.
- The owner has not received understandable alternatives or sufficient review time, unless an immediate safety emergency is documented.
- The replacement creates greater demonstrated risk than the existing material.
- Historic, cultural, or earthen-building significance has not been evaluated.

## Required output for each screened option

- Option name and exact specification.
- Intended function.
- Benefits and risks.
- Known constraints.
- End-of-life pathway.
- Initial cost and lifecycle cost assumptions.
- Required specialists and approvals.
- Evidence category.
- Confidence level.
- Open questions.
- Recommended next verification step.
- Date for review and responsible person.

## Limitations

This rubric is a structured research and conversation aid. It cannot determine whether a material is safe in every context, guarantee regulatory acceptance, replace testing, or predict future markets. All conclusions must remain specific to the material, assembly, building, location, exposure conditions, and available evidence.
