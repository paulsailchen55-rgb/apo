# Identity Lifecycle

A useful model emerging from this inquiry is that identity has a lifecycle rather than a single permanent state.

## Possible states

**Creation** — establish an identity for a person, purpose, project, institution, or transaction.

**Use** — operate the identity for its intended purpose.

**Maintenance** — inspect accuracy, security, links, credentials, privacy settings, and associated information.

**Correction** — repair inaccurate or misleading information.

**Renewal** — intentionally reaffirm that an identity remains current and meaningful.

**Rest** — temporarily suspend operation without necessarily destroying the identity.

**Archive** — preserve historical material while distinguishing it from current identity.

**Retirement** — stop presenting an identity as active while preserving an appropriate record where necessary.

**Removal** — delete or expunge information when there is a legitimate basis and no overriding retention requirement.

## Design principle

Persistence should be purposeful.

A public identity should remain discoverable for as long as its purpose and responsibilities justify that discoverability. Temporary identities should be capable of expiring. Historical identities should be clearly marked as historical. Current identities should be distinguishable from obsolete representations.

## Privacy principle

Identity lifecycle controls should not become a mechanism for forced universal tracking.

The system should distinguish:

- knowing that a person has an identity;
- knowing that two public identities belong to the same person;
- knowing what the person is doing at a particular moment;
- retaining a record of what the person did;
- publicly exposing that record.

These are different levels of information.

## Timing question

The project asks whether identity lifecycle events can use recurring natural reference points without imposing a single cultural calendar.

Potential reference systems include daily cycles, seasonal cycles, biological cycles, service-specific retention periods, and explicitly chosen human dates.

This remains a research question rather than a settled rule.
