# Poll and Community Research Method

A possible community poll asks: **If you had three free hours and wanted to help, what would you be most likely to do?** Options may include direct food or water assistance, an established volunteer shift, safe cleanup, helping someone locate resources, environmental work, information verification, or another action.

Follow-up questions should ask what prevents immediate participation: lack of information, registration requirements, transportation, safety concerns, lack of supplies, uncertainty about usefulness, or limited time.

The project must distinguish stated preferences from observed behavior and actual available opportunities. Polls should not be used to identify, profile, or stigmatize people. Results should be presented as limited responses from a defined population and time period, not as the voice of an entire community.
