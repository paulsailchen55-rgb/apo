# Research Questions and Validation Plan

This is a research agenda, not a claim that the answers are known.

## 1. Space and leasing

- What City of Santa Cruz and Santa Cruz County properties could legally support temporary or partial-use leases?
- What private-property categories are most likely to have underused space?
- What lease terms are attractive to owners?
- What are current local market rates for parking, commercial space, and short-term/temporary use?
- Can standardized micro-lease agreements reduce legal and transaction costs?

## 2. Modular infrastructure

Identify commercially available or deployable modules for:

- showers;
- restrooms;
- handwashing;
- laundry;
- kitchen/food preparation;
- refrigeration;
- secure storage;
- charging;
- animal-support functions;
- lounge/day space.

For each, document cost, installation requirements, utilities, accessibility, maintenance, transportability, useful life, and permitting requirements.

## 3. Economics

Compare at least three models:

1. Permanent acquisition + construction.
2. Lease of existing building + fixed interior conversion.
3. Lease of small site area + transferable modular infrastructure.

Include capital cost, recurring lease cost, operations, maintenance, utilities, insurance, permitting, relocation, depreciation/useful life, and end-of-term restoration.

## 4. Property-owner incentives

Test whether owners receive a meaningful benefit from micro-leasing:

- direct lease income;
- reduced vacancy;
- predictable short-term revenue;
- reversible use;
- preservation of long-term development options;
- potential tax implications;
- community-benefit considerations.

Interview or survey property owners rather than assuming the economics.

## 5. Community character

Develop criteria for avoiding unacceptable neighborhood impacts:

- noise;
- traffic;
- sanitation;
- litter;
- safety;
- animal management;
- hours of operation;
- visual impacts;
- park compatibility;
- impacts on adjacent businesses and residences.

Test whether smaller distributed sites reduce or merely redistribute these impacts.

## 6. Legal and regulatory research

Review current requirements governing:

- zoning;
- building permits;
- fire/life safety;
- plumbing;
- food service;
- public health;
- accessibility;
- temporary structures;
- mobile/modular structures;
- public parks;
- historic districts;
- environmental review;
- procurement;
- public property leases;
- private leases by public agencies;
- insurance and indemnification.

No conclusion should be drawn until the relevant current code and agency guidance are reviewed.

## 7. Civic Space Exchange

Determine whether a City/County platform could support:

- owner listings;
- geographic search;
- availability windows;
- standardized site attributes;
- lease proposals;
- contract workflows;
- module inventory;
- maintenance schedules;
- installation/removal records;
- public reporting.

Evaluate whether existing commercial marketplace APIs could provide useful components without making them the governing system.

## 8. Pilot

A potential pilot should be deliberately small and reversible.

Possible structure:

- one or two leased sites;
- one standardized modular service package;
- fixed pilot term;
- measurable operating costs;
- community feedback;
- property-owner feedback;
- service utilization data;
- incident/sanitation data;
- restoration assessment after removal.

A pilot should have explicit criteria for continuation, modification, relocation, or termination.

## 9. Evidence standards

For each major claim, distinguish:

- documented fact;
- source-backed estimate;
- observed condition;
- participant report;
- hypothesis;
- proposal;
- unresolved question.

Do not use the archive itself as evidence that a claim is true.
