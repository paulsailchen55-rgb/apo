# Research Notes

## Why this folder exists

The immediate research question was prompted by finding historical material while looking into [PERSON_042] and [PERSON_072], followed by contact with people connected to that local history.

The broader question is how to understand a living person's present-day network and worldview without converting historical records into fixed identities.

## Historical anchor: Van Allen / Rotkin

The linked interview is a 1979 historical interview with [PERSON_042] and [PERSON_071] following their election to Santa Cruz City Council:

https://whorulesamerica.ucsc.edu/santacruz/rotkin_van_allen_interview.html

The interview is useful as evidence about what the participants publicly discussed and how they described their politics in that period. It should not be used alone to characterize their present-day beliefs.

## Working research questions

1. What public information establishes a person's current role?
2. Which historical affiliations are genuinely relevant to understanding that current role?
3. Which labels were self-descriptions, and when were they used?
4. What evidence exists for current beliefs rather than historical ones?
5. What relationships are documented, versus merely inferred from co-occurrence on a list?
6. What information is actually necessary for a respectful present-day contact?
7. When does retaining a piece of information stop serving the purpose for which it was collected?

## Present vs. historical identity

A person's historical association with a movement, campaign, organization, or political tendency is a time-stamped fact when adequately documented. It is not automatically evidence of current affiliation.

The same rule applies to relationships: appearing on the same historical list can establish that names appeared together in that artifact; it does not establish friendship, current collaboration, shared beliefs, or continued organizational connection.

## Personal philosophical concept preserved from the conversation

The conversation used the term **equilibriumist** as a personal description of seeking balance between masculine and feminine energies. It also expressed a preference for examining the values and actual practice underneath political/economic labels rather than treating labels as sufficient explanations.

This note records the concept because it shaped the research method. It does not claim that "equilibriumist" is an established political or philosophical school, nor does it characterize any other person as holding that view.

## Information lifecycle principle

A useful distinction is:

**archive → historical reference → current verification → present relationship → expiration/review**

A historical archive can remain useful even when a contact list should not. The existence of an old record does not create an obligation to keep treating it as current.

A future implementation could therefore make every current-data entry carry a verification date and review/expiration condition.

## Privacy and anonymization

This package deliberately does not attempt to assemble private contact details, sensitive personal information, or speculative psychological/religious profiles.

For living people, research should prefer information they have made public themselves or that is clearly relevant to their public roles. Unknowns should remain unknown until there is adequate evidence.

## Next research direction

If additional people are researched, use a per-person record with:

- historical source(s)
- current public source(s)
- self-described affiliations
- documented organizations
- documented public activities
- current relevance to the researcher's purpose
- uncertainty notes
- last verification date
- retention/review date

Do not create a permanent ideological classification system from these records.
