# Apartment Circular Materials Standard

This folder preserves an independent research/design proposal for an apartment-scale materials ecosystem. It is not an established standard or certified product system.

## Core proposition

Design household products around a limited number of compatible material systems that can be grown or sourced renewably where practical, separated when useful life ends, and given real downstream pathways rather than simply moved between waste categories.

Working material architecture:
1. Plant-fiber system: paper, agricultural residues, hemp, flax, molded fiber, and related plant-derived fibers.
2. Structural plant-material system: wood, bamboo, cork, and related durable plant structures.
3. Binding/joining/interface system: binders, adhesives, fasteners, seals, coatings, and flexible interfaces needed for function and durability.

Limited exceptions such as metal, silicone, or rubber may be necessary for safety, sealing, durability, repair, or specialized function. Their inclusion requires actual performance and lifecycle analysis.

## Product scope

Kitchenware, containers, cutting boards, bathroom goods, laundry goods, bedroom and living-room furniture, storage, and emergency/resilience goods.

A recurring concept is a reusable outer container with replaceable or controlled liners where moisture or contamination makes a permanent container impractical.

## Lifecycle

Grow/source → manufacture → use → repair → reuse → disassemble → identify → route → recover → remake/return → residual handling.

End-of-life should be considered before manufacturing.

## Richlite example

Richlite was discussed as a possible durable surface/cutting-board material and identified as a paper-based composite using phenolic resin. It therefore should not be described as plastic-free, fully biodegradable, or equivalent to untreated paper.

## Ecological inspiration

The Three Sisters agricultural tradition was discussed only as a limited design analogy. This archive does not claim equivalence to any Indigenous tradition and recognizes variation among Indigenous nations.

## Local production hypothesis

Growing materials in or near apartment communities may reduce transport or create local resource loops, but local production is not automatically environmentally superior. Water, land, energy, processing, durability, transport, and end-of-life impacts require study.

## Safety and uncertainty

Moisture, mold, contamination, fire behavior, durability, binders, coatings, and maintenance remain open engineering questions. Plant-derived does not automatically mean safe or environmentally preferable.

The associated recovery-machine concept is archived separately in modular-material-recovery-system/.

## Guiding principle

> Design homes and everyday necessities to cooperate with natural cycles while leaving people free to serve, create, rest, and care for one another — to protect and preserve.

This is a guiding design principle, not a scientific claim.
