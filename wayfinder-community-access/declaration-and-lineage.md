# WAYFINDER Declaration and Lineage Notes

## Working declaration

There have always been people who leave information for people coming after them.

A traveler leaves a mark.  
A stranger leaves a note.  
Someone prints a zine.  
Someone publishes a calendar.  
Someone tells the next person where to find what they need.

Technology changes. The underlying act does not.

WAYFINDER carries that tradition forward as a public information layer.

Useful information is a form of hospitality.

The community already contains many useful things. The difficulty is often finding them.

WAYFINDER does not tell people how to live. It helps people discover what is available so they can choose for themselves.

> Go where you are welcome. Learn what is there. Meet who you meet. Help where you can. Leave something useful for the next person.

## Lineage

The project has been discussed in relation to:
- traveler/hobo information traditions
- zines and DIY publishing
- Farmers’ Almanac-style practical reference
- recurring digest publications
- community calendars
- Internet hyperlinks and QR codes
- local-first computing
- AI-assisted information retrieval
- solarpunk and cyberpunk alternative-future thinking

These are influences and analogies, not claims of direct historical descent.

## Public language

Public-facing material should remain secular and accessible even when the user's personal philosophical or religious reflections are part of the project's provenance.

## Honor principle

A possible public-record ethic is:

> Record what is useful to remember and acknowledge contributions rather than creating a permanent dossier of failures.

This is a philosophical/design principle, not a biblical or legal requirement.
