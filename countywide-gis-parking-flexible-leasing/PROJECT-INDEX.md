# Countywide GIS Parking Identification and Flexible Parking Leasing

## Archival Classification

- **Primary classification:** Potential project / research direction
- **Secondary classifications:** Technical concept, civic-infrastructure proposal, policy research
- **Status:** Early concept; not an implemented system

## Purpose

Explore whether every authorized parking space in a county could be represented through a GIS-linked identifier and database, with jurisdictional rules, vehicle-related permissions, time-limited agreements, payment options, and voluntary nonprofit assistance.

## Core Concept

A parking-space system could potentially connect:

- A unique identifier for each mapped parking space.
- GIS location and jurisdictional overlays.
- City, county, state, private-property, accessibility, and road-rule information.
- Time-limited or recurring parking permissions.
- Authorized payment or sponsorship workflows.
- User-facing phone access, while preserving non-phone alternatives.
- Records of the agreement, responsible party, duration, and space condition.

The system must not assume that a county owns or controls every parking space. Ownership, jurisdiction, authority to lease, and enforcement powers would need to be identified separately.

## Relationship to Microleasing

Microleasing is the broader framework for short-duration access to spaces. Parking is a specialized application and should remain a separate project because it introduces additional technical, legal, privacy, transportation, and governmental questions.

## Proposed Research Areas

1. GIS standards and spatial identifiers.
2. Inventorying public and voluntarily participating private parking spaces.
3. Jurisdictional and ownership boundaries.
4. DMV and vehicle-information access restrictions.
5. Privacy, consent, and data minimization.
6. Parking regulations, accessibility, and roadway rules.
7. Temporary permissions and payment processing.
8. Nonprofit sponsorship or hardship assistance.
9. Notifications, appeals, and dispute resolution.
10. Human-centered and non-punitive enforcement alternatives.

## Illustrative Workflow

1. A space is mapped and assigned an internal identifier.
2. The system displays the applicable authority, restrictions, and availability.
3. An authorized owner or agency makes the space available for a defined period.
4. A user requests or accepts a time-limited agreement.
5. Payment, sponsorship, or nonprofit assistance is processed through an authorized channel.
6. The agreement records duration, conditions, and responsibilities.
7. Expiration, renewal, complaint, and appeal procedures are made clear.

This is a conceptual workflow, not a claim that agencies currently have the authority, data access, or infrastructure to operate it.

## Safeguards and Limitations

- Do not assume automatic access to DMV owner information.
- Do not use vehicle identification as an unrestricted surveillance mechanism.
- Do not treat every parking space as leasable.
- Preserve accessibility and emergency restrictions.
- Separate public-space authority from private-property consent.
- Provide non-digital access pathways.
- Establish data retention, correction, and deletion policies.
- Require legal and technical review before any pilot.

## Initial Research Leads

- City of Santa Cruz citywide permit-parking eligibility agenda item.
- City and county GIS departments.
- Public Works and transportation agencies.
- Parking districts and private-property operators.
- DMV data-access rules and privacy requirements.
- Existing parking-payment, curb-management, and digital-permit systems.

## Open Questions

- Who should own and maintain the master database?
- Would the city, county, or another regional body be the appropriate coordinating entity?
- What is the legal meaning of a time-limited parking agreement in each jurisdiction?
- How could private owners voluntarily participate?
- How could hardship assistance operate without exposing sensitive personal information?
- What would happen when a user cannot pay?
- How would unauthorized parking, emergencies, accessibility, and disputes be handled?
- Can the system begin with a small voluntary pilot rather than a countywide deployment?

## Provenance Note

The concept originated in a conversation about extending microleasing to parking spaces. The user proposed a mapped identifier for each space, jurisdictional overlays, instantaneous phone-based leasing, and a supportive workflow in which nonprofits might pay for a user's temporary parking agreement. These are user-originated proposals and should not be represented as existing government policy.
