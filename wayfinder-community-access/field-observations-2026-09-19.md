# Field Observations: Community Activities and Public Information

## Date
September 19, 2026

## Community activity observations

In Santa Cruz's Seabright neighborhood, I encountered a bicycle-related community event at a park referred to as Lookout Park or Overlook Park. The event was occurring, and pedal-bike participants were gathered and socializing. The observation suggests that useful community activities may exist in ordinary neighborhood spaces and may not always be obvious to people seeking constructive daytime activities.

The park may also host recurring weekend activities, potentially in the mornings, including Tai Chi, martial arts, and related practices. These details require confirmation before publication as a verified listing.

## Rio event information discrepancy

I traveled to the Rio after encountering information describing a free pre-show or free party connected with a block party event. At the ticket area, staff stated that there was no free show and that tickets were required. An older woman who had also seen the event information in *Good Times* reported that she went to the same event and received the same information.

This documents a discrepancy between the information that led people to attend and the conditions communicated at the location. It does not establish whether the cause was inaccurate advertising, a last-minute change, unclear wording, separate event segments, or another explanation.

## Research observation: trust, unexpected cost, and scarcity

Information that appears to promise an affordable or free opportunity but becomes unavailable or unexpectedly costly at arrival can feel like a bait-and-switch to the person who traveled there, even when intent and legal status are not established. Repeated experiences may reduce trust in a place's public information; this is a research hypothesis, not a universal conclusion.

Information about genuinely difficult-to-find opportunities can also feel valuable because someone discovered something useful. WAYFINDER should preserve the value of discovery without encouraging secrecy, pride, exclusion, insider status, or control of community knowledge by a small group. The intended direction is dependable public access to useful information, not a hierarchy of insiders and outsiders.

## Questions for inquiry

1. How should WAYFINDER distinguish an error from a last-minute change or unclear description?
2. What evidence should be retained when an advertised free opportunity is unavailable?
3. How can listings show their latest verification time?
4. How can independent reports be compared without creating reputation scores?
5. What happens to public trust when people repeatedly encounter unexpected fees or unavailable activities?
6. How can discrepancies be recorded without assuming intent or assigning blame automatically?
7. How can information remain discoverable without becoming an insider privilege?
8. How should uncertain observations about recurring activities be labeled until confirmed?
9. What format helps someone decide whether an activity is worth walking to?

## Preliminary principle

WAYFINDER should support both discovery and trust: help people find real opportunities, preserve uncertainty honestly, and make discrepancies visible for investigation without becoming a condemnation system, popularity ranking, or insider-control mechanism.
