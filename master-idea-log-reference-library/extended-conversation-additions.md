## 62–71 — Property access and community title
62. Property access and community title — RESEARCH QUESTION / POTENTIAL PROJECT. How can people without conventional wealth obtain durable, lawful interests in land or housing?
63. Community Stewardship and Access Model — POTENTIAL PROJECT. Explore a legally recognized structure in which an organization holds land while community members receive defined use, occupancy, membership, or beneficial rights. Not established California law.
64. Property-less entry problem — RESEARCH QUESTION. Distinguish legal capacity to own property from practical ability to acquire it.
65. Title / beneficial interest / occupancy distinction — LEGAL RESEARCH FRAMEWORK. Separate fee title from other recognized interests.
66. Existing-structure comparison — RESEARCH DIRECTION. Compare individual ownership, co-ownership, partnerships, corporations, nonprofits, LLCs, trusts, cooperatives, community land trusts, leases, and occupancy arrangements.
67. Institutional feedback-loop question — RESEARCH QUESTION. Ask whether funding, administrative, housing, correctional, or service systems can unintentionally reproduce instability; evidence is required before any claim of intentional coordination.
68. Historical “unclaimed/fatherless” analogy — PERSONAL/PHILOSOPHICAL EXPLORATION. Analogy only, not empirical proof.
69. Spiritual/demon-possession analogy — PERSONAL/PHILOSOPHICAL EXPLORATION. Kept separate from legal and empirical claims.
70. California real-estate textbook source trail — SUPPORTING RESEARCH. Exact editions and contents remain unresolved.
71. California DRE title-source check — SUPPORTING LEGAL RESEARCH. A lead for researching entities capable of holding title/interests; current-law verification is required.

## 72 — Omega
72. Omega Principle / Closure — FRAMEWORK / CONCEPT. Consider the proper end of a thing, process, relationship, material, or system while designing its beginning and use. Related to endpoint-closure work but kept separate.

## 73–80 — Lendable health and home measurement
73. Lendable Health & Home Measurement Technology — POTENTIAL PROJECT / RESEARCH PROGRAM. Explore library lending of simple health and home-environment instruments for approximately month-long self-measurement by renters and homeowners, including apartment residents.
74. Health/home measurement layered architecture — FRAMEWORK. Separate physical measurement, borrower device, clinical path, environmental path, voluntary sharing, and community/public data.
75. Autonomous-device selection framework — EXPERIMENT / EVALUATION METHOD. Evaluate whether a first-time user can operate a device without a class or specialist.
76. OCHIN/MyChart health-record pathway — RESEARCH QUESTION. Explore whether user-generated home measurements can be turned into a simple report or structured record for appropriate clinical review.
77. Privacy/security/provenance architecture for lendable measurement — TECHNICAL FRAMEWORK. Device identity, borrower privacy, encryption, de-identification, auditability, and lost/stolen status.
78. Environmental-data governance — RESEARCH QUESTION. Determine how measurements can be de-identified, aggregated, voluntarily shared, and distinguished from medical or legal interpretation.
79. Library versus neighborhood tool-shed model — COMPARATIVE SUPPORTING IDEA. Compare public library lending infrastructure with a community-owned tool-shed approach.
80. Public-infrastructure surveillance/privacy thought experiment — PERSONAL/TECHNICAL EXPLORATION. Consider how measurement infrastructure could increase surveillance risk and how privacy-by-architecture might constrain it.
