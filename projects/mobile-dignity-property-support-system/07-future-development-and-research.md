# Step 7 — Future Development and Research

## Research questions

- What frame geometry, wheel type, braking system, and cargo configuration best support pushing and riding?
- Which designs are accessible, repairable, weather-resistant, and affordable?
- How can a trike remain identifiable for recovery without tracking its user?
- What storage-pod designs meet security, ventilation, fire, accessibility, and emergency-access requirements?
- Which local transit agencies and facilities permit or restrict cargo trikes?
- What agreements are needed with libraries, nonprofits, clinics, shelters, legal-aid groups, and public agencies?
- How should consent, data retention, property inventories, and unclaimed belongings be handled?
- What funding model protects public-benefit access from commercial displacement?

## Possible development phases

1. Build and test the basic trike.
2. Conduct supervised user testing with varied participants and loads.
3. Improve cargo security, accessibility, and repair procedures.
4. Test a small, non-networked storage arrangement with a partner.
5. Develop recovery and referral procedures with written agreements.
6. Evaluate whether optional electric assistance or shelter modules are justified.
7. Explore privacy-preserving digital coordination only after the physical service is understood.

## Evaluation measures

- Safety incidents, near misses, braking performance, and maintenance frequency.
- Participant-reported dignity, autonomy, comfort, accessibility, and usefulness.
- Property loss, successful recovery, storage reliability, and response time.
- Cost per participant, repairability, staff burden, and equitable access.
- Unintended consequences, exclusion patterns, surveillance risks, and mission drift.

This document remains a research agenda. It does not establish legal authority, funding, technical feasibility, or commitments from potential partners.
