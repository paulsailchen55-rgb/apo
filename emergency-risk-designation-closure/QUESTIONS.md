# Questions

## A. Threshold and classification

1. What precisely constitutes a documented suicide-risk concern?
2. Who is authorized to create the initial designation?
3. What evidence is required?
4. Can a third party create a designation without the person's knowledge?
5. Can a designation be created from behavior alone?
6. Can substance use be used as a proxy for suicide risk?
7. What distinctions are maintained between vulnerability, substance use, suicidal ideation, suicide attempt, and immediate danger?
8. Can a historical crisis automatically produce a current-risk classification?
9. Can an AI system create or recommend a risk designation?
10. Is human confirmation required?

## B. Scope of intervention

11. What authority does a designation actually create?
12. Does a proclamation itself create operational authority, or is it ceremonial?
13. What separate policy, regulation, contract, protocol, or law would be required before monitoring occurs?
14. What information is necessary for the stated safety purpose?
15. What information is explicitly unnecessary?
16. Can monitoring extend beyond the immediate emergency?
17. Can family, employers, housing providers, schools, courts, or law enforcement receive the information?
18. Under what authority?

## C. Time and closure

19. How long does a risk designation remain active?
20. Does it automatically expire?
21. Who must review it before renewal?
22. What evidence is required to renew it?
23. How is “resolved” defined?
24. Can a person who has stabilized still carry a historical risk label?
25. Is there a formal closure event?
26. Is closure recorded separately from the original concern?
27. Does closure actually terminate monitoring?
28. Can a closed case automatically reopen because new data appears?
29. Who is allowed to reopen it?
30. Does reopening require new evidence?

## D. Data retention

31. What records are created?
32. Where are they stored?
33. How long are they retained?
34. Which records are temporary?
35. Which records are permanent?
36. Can unnecessary records be deleted?
37. If deletion is impossible, can information be segregated or access-restricted?
38. Are derived AI inferences retained?
39. Are model-generated risk scores retained?
40. Can deleted source information continue to exist in derived datasets?

## E. Secondary use

41. Can suicide-prevention information be used for a purpose unrelated to suicide prevention?
42. Can it be used in a criminal investigation?
43. Can it affect housing eligibility?
44. Can it affect benefits or services?
45. Can it affect employment?
46. Can it affect court proceedings?
47. Can it be used for predictive-risk systems?
48. Can it be sold, licensed, transferred, or shared with contractors?
49. Can another agency request it for a different purpose?
50. Is consent required for secondary use?

## F. AI and inference

51. Can an AI system infer that a person is suicidal without an explicit statement?
52. Can it infer substance-use severity?
53. Can it infer criminality, dangerousness, instability, or other unrelated characteristics?
54. Can an inference become part of the person's permanent record?
55. Can one inference become training data for another system?
56. Can AI-generated information be distinguished from human-observed facts?
57. Can the person challenge an AI-generated inference?
58. Can the person see the evidence supporting the inference?
59. Can the inference be deleted?
60. Does deletion of the source record also delete derived inferences?

## G. Feedback-loop failure

61. Can monitoring produce information that is then used to justify more monitoring?
62. What prevents that loop?
63. Is there an independent stopping rule?
64. Can the organization benefit institutionally from continuing to classify or monitor a person?
65. Could funding, staffing, performance metrics, or technology contracts create incentives for continued classification?
66. Can a safety program measure success by the amount of information collected rather than successful resolution?
67. Does “more data” become an implicit definition of “better safety”?

## H. Correction and appeal

68. Can a person learn that they have been classified?
69. Can they challenge an inaccurate designation?
70. Can they correct factual errors?
71. Can they contest an inference?
72. Can they request closure?
73. Who independently reviews the dispute?
74. Is there a documented correction history?
75. Can an incorrect historical label continue to influence future automated decisions?

## I. Public records and transparency

76. Which records are public?
77. Which are confidential?
78. What happens when a public-records request intersects with sensitive crisis information?
79. Are aggregate statistics separated from identifiable records?
80. Can published aggregate data be reverse-engineered to identify individuals?
81. Are contractors subject to the same restrictions?

## J. Resilience

82. What happens when an emergency system fails?
83. What happens when a false positive occurs?
84. What happens when a false negative occurs?
85. What happens when an AI model makes a classification error?
86. What happens when records are duplicated across agencies?
87. What happens when a person changes jurisdiction?
88. What happens when a database is breached?
89. What happens when the original program ends?
90. What happens when the technology vendor changes?

## K. Fundamental question

> **How can a system remain capable of intervening when intervention is genuinely necessary without becoming incapable of stopping when the justification for intervention has ended?**
