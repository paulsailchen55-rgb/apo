# Prototype 01 — Real-World Case: TMC / NORI Area D

## Status

**TESTED / DOCUMENTED CASE — INITIAL PASS**

This document applies Prototype 01 to one documented real-world case: The Metals Company (TMC), its wholly owned subsidiary Nauru Ocean Resources Inc. (NORI), and the NORI polymetallic-nodule exploration area in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone (CCZ).

This is an initial case trace, not a conclusion about deep-sea mining, TMC, Nauru, the International Seabed Authority (ISA), finance, or ocean governance.

The purpose is to test whether the prototype can represent a real system without forcing the evidence into the model.

---

# 1. Perspective Contract

- **Observer position:** resource-development project moving from exploration toward possible exploitation
- **Primary case:** NORI Area D / surrounding NORI contract area in the CCZ
- **Primary actors:** TMC, NORI, Government of Nauru, ISA, U.S. regulatory agencies, technology/industry partners, financiers/investors
- **Primary resource:** polymetallic nodules containing metals including nickel, copper, cobalt, and manganese
- **Time frame:** 2011–present, with particular attention to 2025–2026 developments
- **Starting question:** What changes in the available choice set when exploration access, investment, technology, regulatory pathways, and future production possibilities become connected?
- **Evidence rule:** documented facts are separated from interpretation and hypothesis
- **Known blind spot:** this first pass does not yet reconstruct the complete capital stack, all project expenditures, all counterparties, or the full environmental evidence base

---

# 2. BEFORE — Resource Opportunity and Industrial Context

The ISA describes polymetallic nodules as containing metals including nickel, copper, cobalt and manganese that are relevant to various industries, including renewable-energy and technology manufacturing. The ISA also explicitly states that these metals are not unique to the deep sea.

Therefore the initial statement is deliberately narrower than "society needs deep-sea mining":

> **There is an identified seabed resource containing metals with established industrial uses, creating an opportunity to investigate another potential source.**

The prototype does not yet establish:

- that a physical shortage existed;
- that terrestrial supply was insufficient;
- that deep-sea extraction was necessary;
- that deep-sea extraction would reduce or increase total system risk.

Those are later research questions.

**Evidence:** ISA FAQ, July 2025.

---

# 3. ENTRY — Exploration Rights

NORI received an ISA polymetallic-nodule exploration contract in July 2011 under the sponsorship of Nauru.

The NORI contract area covers approximately 74,830 km² in the CCZ. The exploration contract provides exclusive exploration rights and a priority right to apply for an exploitation contract in the same area, subject to the applicable rules and conditions.

NORI Area D is the portion where TMC has reported conducting the most resource-definition and environmental work.

The important transformation for the prototype is:

~~~text
RESOURCE OF INTEREST
        ↓
EXPLORATION RIGHTS
        ↓
CONTROLLED ACCESS TO RESOURCE AREA
~~~

This is not yet commercial extraction.

It is an **option-producing stage**: the system acquires information, rights, relationships, and a possible pathway toward a later decision.

**Evidence:** TMC SEC filings; ISA materials.

---

# 4. BENEFIT — What Is Received Before Commercial Production?

The immediate benefits documented in this case are not limited to physical minerals.

The exploration phase produces or supports:

- geological/resource information;
- environmental information;
- exploration access;
- technical development;
- project-specific knowledge;
- relationships with sponsoring and regulatory institutions;
- a pathway toward possible future exploitation.

TMC has reported resource-definition and environmental work in NORI Area D. It has also worked with industrial partners on collection and processing technology.

The prototype therefore expands the concept of "resource received now":

~~~text
EXPLORATION
   ↓
KNOWLEDGE
+
RESOURCE DEFINITION
+
TECHNOLOGY
+
INSTITUTIONAL POSITION
+
OPTION ON FUTURE ACTIVITY
~~~

The model must not treat an option as equivalent to a realized future benefit.

---

# 5. OBLIGATION — Contracts, Financing, and Future Capacity

The project creates institutional and financial relationships before commercial extraction occurs.

In May 2025, NORI, the Government of Nauru, and the Nauru Seabed Minerals Authority entered a revised Sponsorship Agreement. The agreement formalizes continued Nauruan sponsorship and establishes terms concerning future commercial production.

The agreement includes a seabed mineral recovery payment tied to nodules recovered under an ISA contract, subject to the agreement's conditions. TMC also entered a guarantee and indemnity arrangement concerning certain NORI obligations.

The 2025 agreement also includes a future-oriented continuity benefit tied to specified commercial-recovery conditions.

These arrangements demonstrate:

~~~text
EXPLORATION ACCESS
      ↓
INSTITUTIONAL RELATIONSHIPS
      ↓
CONTRACTUAL CONDITIONS
      ↓
FUTURE PRODUCTION BECOMES RELEVANT
~~~

This is a critical prototype distinction:

> **A future production claim is not the same thing as future production.**

The case therefore requires separate tracking of:

- present obligations;
- conditional obligations;
- anticipated future revenue;
- anticipated future production;
- actual production.

---

# 6. TECHNOLOGY — Building the Capacity to Continue

TMC has reported strategic work with Allseas on a pilot polymetallic-nodule collection system and development toward a commercial production system.

TMC has also reported processing and metallurgical development with other industrial and engineering partners.

This creates another feedback relationship:

~~~text
RESOURCE ACCESS
      ↓
TECHNOLOGY DEVELOPMENT
      ↓
GREATER CAPACITY TO USE RESOURCE
      ↓
RESOURCE OPTION BECOMES MORE CONCRETE
      ↓
GREATER INCENTIVE TO CONTINUE DEVELOPMENT
~~~

This is **reinforcement**, not yet "capture."

The prototype should not call the process capture unless the evidence shows that practical alternatives diminish or exit becomes materially harder.

---

# 7. FINANCIAL LAYER — Initial Observation

TMC has financed its development through multiple forms of capital, including equity offerings and other securities.

The first-pass map should not yet attempt to judge whether this financing structure is sustainable or extractive.

Instead, it asks:

> **What future activity must occur for present financial commitments and expectations to be satisfied?**

That question connects the financial layer to the physical and institutional layers without treating money as physical energy.

The next research stage should reconstruct:

~~~text
CAPITAL PROVIDED
      ↓
WHAT THE CAPITAL FINANCED
      ↓
PROJECT CAPACITY CREATED
      ↓
FUTURE ACTIVITY REQUIRED
      ↓
EXPECTED REVENUE / PRODUCTION
      ↓
FINANCIAL CLAIMS
~~~

This is the point where a conventional financial-risk analysis could be run **alongside** the systems map later, rather than replacing it.

---

# 8. CHANGED CHOICE SET

Once exploration rights, technical work, financing, institutional relationships, and regulatory pathways exist, the system has a different choice set than it had before entry.

Conceptually:

~~~text
CONTINUE EXPLORATION
        │
        ├── DEVELOP TECHNOLOGY
        │
        ├── COLLECT MORE DATA
        │
        ├── SEEK REGULATORY APPROVAL
        │
        ├── SEEK CAPITAL / PARTNERS
        │
        ├── MODIFY PROJECT
        │
        ├── PAUSE
        │
        ├── ABANDON / RENOUNCE
        │
        └── SEEK COMMERCIAL RECOVERY
~~~

The important question is not whether these choices exist on paper.

It is:

> **Which choices remain materially available after years of investment, specialization, institutional commitments, and project-specific knowledge have accumulated?**

That is an empirical question for the next stage.

---

# 9. EXIT TEST

TMC's filings have described mechanisms under which exploration rights can be renounced or otherwise terminated under applicable terms.

Therefore this case does contain a formal exit pathway.

But the prototype requires a second test:

~~~text
FORMAL EXIT
     ↓
ECONOMIC COST
     ↓
ORGANIZATIONAL COST
     ↓
TECHNOLOGY / ASSET WRITE-OFF
     ↓
CONTRACTUAL CONSEQUENCES
     ↓
REPUTATIONAL / STRATEGIC CONSEQUENCES
     ↓
MATERIALLY AVAILABLE EXIT?
~~~

We do not yet have enough evidence to answer the final question.

That is precisely why the distinction between **formal exit** and **materially available exit** belongs in the case.

---

# 10. CURRENT OUTCOME — Do Not Overstate the State of the System

The ISA states that it has issued exploration contracts but has **not approved commercial exploitation** under its seabed-mining regime.

Separately, TMC has pursued U.S. regulatory pathways for exploration and commercial recovery under the U.S. Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act.

As of this case-study pass, commercial recovery should therefore be represented as:

~~~text
EXPLORATION
      ↓
TECHNOLOGY DEVELOPMENT
      ↓
FINANCING
      ↓
REGULATORY APPLICATIONS
      ↓
POTENTIAL COMMERCIAL RECOVERY
~~~

not:

~~~text
EXPLORATION → COMMERCIAL MINING
~~~

The distinction is essential.

This is the first point at which Prototype 01 appears to describe an actual system rather than an abstract transaction.

# 12. What the Case Does NOT Establish

This case does not establish that:

- deep-sea mining is necessary;
- deep-sea mining is unnecessary;
- TMC is extractive in a moral or political sense;
- financial investment inevitably produces capture;
- exploration necessarily leads to exploitation;
- commercial exploitation will occur;
- environmental damage will exceed terrestrial alternatives;
- environmental benefits will exceed environmental costs;
- the project is financially viable;
- the project is financially unviable;
- the ocean is being used as an "escape" from terrestrial resource limits;
- the ocean is merely a continuation of an existing extractive system.

Those remain research questions.

# 13. Next Evidence Needed

To continue the case without jumping to conclusions, collect evidence in this order:

### A. Capital

Who supplied capital, when, through what instruments, and for what stated purpose?

### B. Project expenditure

What was actually built, purchased, tested, leased, or contracted?

### C. Future assumptions

What production volumes, prices, costs, timelines, and regulatory assumptions appear in technical/economic documents?

### D. Obligations

Which obligations are unconditional, conditional, contingent, or dependent on commercial recovery?

### E. Exit

What does abandoning or renouncing the project actually cost?

### F. Alternatives

What alternative sources, technologies, recycling pathways, substitution possibilities, or demand-side changes are documented?

### G. Physical system

What energy, materials, vessels, processing infrastructure, transportation, and maintenance are required?

### H. Ecological system

What environmental conditions and uncertainties are documented, and who bears the associated risks?

# 14. Working Hypothesis for the Next Stage

> **An initially optional resource opportunity can become increasingly structured by accumulated knowledge, technology, capital, contracts, expectations, and institutional relationships. The research question is whether this reinforcement merely increases capability or also progressively reduces materially available alternatives and exit options.**

This is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion.

# 15. Prototype Result

**Prototype 01 survives the first real-world case pass with modifications.**

The case suggests that the map needs several additional nodes that were less visible in the abstract model:

~~~text
OPTION
→ SPECIALIZATION
→ INVESTMENT
→ INSTITUTIONAL COMMITMENT
→ EXPECTATION
→ CAPACITY
~~~

These should be added carefully rather than treated as automatically harmful.

The next step is to test whether these nodes change the **actual choice set** and **exit conditions**.

## 16. The Dependency Question — Explicit for Whom?

The case now adds a more precise question to the prototype:

> **At what point does an optional possibility become a system requirement — and for whom?**

The phrase **for whom** is essential. A project can become a requirement for one actor, institution, contract, revenue model, investment thesis, government expectation, technology pathway, or community without becoming a requirement for the larger system.

This prevents the analysis from silently moving from:

`REQUIREMENT FOR ONE ACTOR`

to:

`REQUIREMENT FOR THE SYSTEM`

without evidence.

### Dependency should therefore be traced actor-by-actor

For each dependency, identify the first point at which it becomes explicit:

| Dependency question | What to identify |
|---|---|
| **Requirement for whom?** | Actor, institution, contract, project, market, or broader system |
| **First explicit statement?** | First dated document where the dependency is stated or contractually created |
| **What is required?** | Capital, approval, production, revenue, technology, sponsor, resource, price, etc. |
| **Required for what?** | Continuation, profitability, contract performance, financing, regulatory approval, or some other outcome |
| **Conditional or unconditional?** | Whether the requirement depends on a specified future event |
| **What happens if it fails?** | Pause, loss, renegotiation, termination, write-off, insolvency risk, reduced capacity, or other documented consequence |
| **Does the dependency propagate?** | Whether another actor becomes dependent on the same condition |

### The first-explicit-dependency test

Instead of asking only whether the project is "dependent," trace the sequence:

~~~text
OPTION
  ↓
FIRST EXPLICIT DEPENDENCY
  ↓
SPECIALIZATION
  ↓
INVESTMENT / COMMITMENT
  ↓
NEW CAPACITY
  ↓
NEW DEPENDENCY
  ↓
REINFORCEMENT
  ↓
REQUIREMENT FOR WHOM?
~~~

The research task is to find the **earliest documentary point** at which each dependency becomes explicit, rather than inferring dependency backward from the project's present state.

### Two separate propositions must be tested

**Proposition A — Actor-level requirement:**

> A future condition becomes necessary for a particular actor or project to achieve a stated objective.

**Proposition B — System-level requirement:**

> The same condition becomes necessary for the larger system, population, or public objective.

Proposition B requires substantially more evidence than Proposition A.

### Current case status

For the TMC/NORI case, the existing evidence already supports investigation of actor-level dependencies involving exploration rights, Nauruan sponsorship, financing, technical feasibility, regulatory approval, future production, and market conditions. It does **not** yet establish that commercial deep-sea recovery is a requirement for the broader economy, for the United States, for Nauru, for the ocean economy, or for society generally.

That distinction is now part of the prototype.

### Next evidence target

For Hour 2, build a dependency ledger with this structure:

`ACTOR → FIRST EXPLICIT DEPENDENCY → DOCUMENT DATE → REQUIRED CONDITION → PURPOSE → CONSEQUENCE IF ABSENT → PROPAGATION`

This should be built from primary documents where possible and should distinguish **stated dependency** from **analytical dependency inferred from the system map**.
