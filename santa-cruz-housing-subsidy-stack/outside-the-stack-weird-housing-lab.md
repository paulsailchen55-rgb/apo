# Outside the Stack — The Weird Housing Lab

**Project:** Santa Cruz County Housing — Subsidy Stack & Housing Stability  
**Status:** PROPOSED / RESEARCH IN PROGRESS  
**Date:** September 24, 2026

## Purpose

The project began by examining the housing subsidy stack:

LAND → ZONING → DENSITY → DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS → FEES/EXACTIONS → CONSTRUCTION → FINANCING → SUBSIDIES → AFFORDABILITY → MARKET REVENUE → ADMINISTRATION → STABILITY → FEASIBILITY

The next phase asks whether some of the stack can be redesigned rather than continuously funded.

> **What if the housing affordability problem is partly caused by repeatedly subsidizing transactions instead of designing a housing asset that requires fewer transactions to remain stable?**

This lab deliberately investigates unusual mechanisms that remove dependencies underneath the conventional system.

## 1. Remove land from the rent

Instead of subsidizing a rent that contains the cost of increasingly valuable land, keep land in public, trust, or community ownership and provide the building through a long-term building-right or ground-lease arrangement.

**Concept:** land becomes a permanent housing platform rather than a recurring speculative component of housing cost.

Vienna has used long-term building rights to retain public influence over land and reduce land-cost pressure in subsidized housing.

Source: https://www.wien.gv.at/regierungsabkommen2020/leistbare-stadt/leistbares-wohnen-der-zukunft/

## 2. Make developers compete on housing outcomes, not land price

Instead of awarding public land primarily through the highest land bid, use a transparent competition based on documented housing outcomes.

Possible evaluation dimensions:

- durable housing output;
- total long-run housing cost;
- construction and operating cost;
- maintenance and replacement reserves;
- accessibility;
- resilience;
- energy/ecological performance;
- community facilities;
- resident governance;
- long-term affordability;
- financial durability.

Vienna's developer competitions evaluate projects through architecture, economics, ecology, and social sustainability.

Sources:
https://www.wien.gv.at/spezial/regierungsabkommen2025/wiens-zukunft-nachhaltig-gestalten/leistbarer-wohntraum-und-lebenswerter-stadtraum-wien-wohnt-besser/
https://www.wien.gv.at/stadtplanung/aspanggruende-aktuelle-entwicklungsphase

## 3. Make the building the subsidy instead of the tenant

Instead of attaching every affordability mechanism to the household, design the physical housing asset to carry part of its own affordability burden.

Potential building resources:

- energy generation;
- energy storage;
- shared workspace;
- commercial space;
- childcare;
- community kitchen;
- gardens;
- repair services;
- telecommunications;
- mobility services;
- resilience infrastructure.

Proposed system:

BUILDING → produces revenue → avoids costs → provides shared services → builds reserves → supports housing affordability.

This is a proposed systems concept, not an established financing mechanism.

## 4. Permanent housing without permanent poverty classification

A household can become economically stronger without the building becoming unaffordable.

Separate:

**housing permanence**

from

**poverty classification.**

A mixed-income permanently affordable building could retain affordability in the physical asset while allowing resident incomes to change.

Community land trusts and limited-equity cooperatives are existing shared-equity structures designed to preserve long-term affordability.

Source: https://www.urban.org/apps/pursuing-housing-justice-interventions-impact/dedicated-funding-sources

## 5. Housing First — invert the bureaucracy

Instead of:

ELIGIBILITY → PROOF → PROGRAM → HOUSING → MORE PROGRAMS

test:

HOUSING → STABLE ADDRESS → OPTIONAL/COORDINATED SUPPORT → CONTINUITY

The resident's housing record could answer:

- What is owed?
- What is covered?
- What changed?
- What needs attention?
- What assistance is active?
- What deadlines matter?
- What support was voluntarily requested?

This is a proposed administrative architecture. It is not a claim that existing Housing First programs already operate this way.

## 6. The Housing Operating System

Treat the housing relationship as an operating system rather than many unrelated bureaucratic transactions.

Possible functions:

ADDRESS / RESIDENCY → payment → subsidy → utilities → maintenance → eligibility status → recertification → emergency assistance → notices → voluntary services → continuity/reserve

The important unit is the housing relationship, not the agency.

**Research question:**

> How much administrative transaction cost can be removed without removing accountability?

Privacy, consent, due process, and data minimization would be design requirements.

## 7. Turn subsidy into a reserve, not merely a monthly payment

A monthly subsidy addresses a recurring affordability gap.

A Housing Stability Reserve addresses disruption.

Potential triggers:

- temporary income interruption;
- security deposit;
- emergency relocation;
- short rent gap;
- utility interruption;
- emergency repair;
- transition between housing arrangements.

Questions requiring legal and financial research:

1. Who owns the reserve?
2. Is it attached to the person, lease, unit, or building?
3. Is it portable?
4. Is it an asset for means-tested programs?
5. Is it taxable?
6. Does unused value roll forward?
7. Can reserves be pooled?
8. What happens when a resident moves?
9. Who administers it?
10. What constitutes a qualifying emergency?

Conceptual distinction:

**SUBSIDY = continuous payment**

versus

**RESERVE = continuity capacity**

## 8. Make the building generate assets

The building becomes more than a shelter-producing structure.

Potential architecture:

HOUSING + ENERGY + STORAGE + TELECOM + WORKSPACE + FOOD + CHILDCARE + REPAIR + MOBILITY + COMMUNITY SPACE + RESILIENCE

The building can potentially generate:

**income + avoided costs + services + reserves**

that support housing stability.

Research must determine which combinations are financially realistic without creating excessive operating complexity.

## 9. No conventional landlord — but professional operations

The question is not whether buildings need maintenance, management, leasing, accounting, and legal operations. They do.

The question is whether ownership must be:

SPECULATIVE OWNER → LANDLORD → TENANT

Alternative structure:

LAND TRUST / PUBLIC LAND
↓
RESIDENT COOPERATIVE / NONPROFIT HOUSING ENTITY
↓
PROFESSIONAL OPERATIONS
↓
RESIDENTS

Community land trusts and limited-equity cooperatives provide existing examples of shared-equity structures that separate long-term affordability from ordinary speculative ownership.

Sources:
https://www.urban.org/apps/pursuing-housing-justice-interventions-impact/dedicated-funding-sources
https://www.urban.org/projects/shared-equity-research

Historical experiments in communes, cooperatives, intentional communities, mutual housing, and resident-owned communities should be studied without romanticizing them. Failures are evidence too.

## 10. Housing that does not care what your income is

This is deliberately extreme.

> **What if the building does not need to determine whether the resident is at 40%, 50%, 60%, 80%, 100%, or 120% of AMI in order to determine whether the resident can remain?**

Instead:

**housing cost = long-run cost of the asset**

Potential components:

- land;
- construction;
- financing;
- maintenance;
- management;
- replacement reserves;
- capital renewal.

Income-based assistance could become a secondary mechanism for households whose resources temporarily fall below that cost.

This would not eliminate federal or state income rules where programs require them. It is a research question about whether AMI should be the organizing principle of the housing asset itself.

## 11. The Stable Housing Utility

Combine the mechanisms:

PUBLIC/COMMUNITY LAND
↓
LONG-TERM BUILDING RIGHT
↓
COST-BASED HOUSING
↓
MIXED-INCOME RESIDENTS
↓
RESIDENT GOVERNANCE
↓
PROFESSIONAL OPERATIONS
↓
LOW-FRICTION ASSISTANCE
↓
HOUSING STABILITY RESERVE
↓
BUILDING-GENERATED REVENUE / COST SAVINGS
↓
LONG-TERM MAINTENANCE RESERVE
↓
NO FORCED INCOME CLIFF

The objective is not necessarily zero subsidy.

The objective is to investigate whether the system can require:

- fewer subsidies;
- fewer eligibility determinations;
- fewer transactions;
- fewer interruptions;
- fewer vacancies;
- fewer administrative failure points.

## 12. The AMI feedback-loop hypothesis

AMI is not simply a single local rule. Federal, state, local, financing, and program structures can use income classifications differently.

The research question is:

> **What happens when an upstream income-classification system becomes a downstream organizing principle for housing eligibility, rent restrictions, subsidies, compliance, and reporting?**

Potential chain:

FEDERAL / PROGRAM MEASUREMENT
↓
ELIGIBILITY
↓
LOCAL ADMINISTRATION
↓
DEVELOPMENT REQUIREMENTS
↓
TENANT CLASSIFICATION
↓
RENT CALCULATION
↓
RECERTIFICATION
↓
REPORTING
↓
COMPLIANCE

The hypothesis is that classification can become an administrative dependency throughout the system.

**This must be measured rather than assumed.**

## 13. Housing Stability Yield

The broader project proposes:

**Housing Stability Yield = Affordable Units × Successful Lease-Up × Occupancy Continuity × Program Continuity × Long-Term Financial Viability**

And:

**Administrative Efficiency = Housing Stability Yield ÷ Administrative Transaction Cost**

These are conceptual research metrics, not established government formulas.

They are intended to measure durable housing outcomes rather than rank households or programs.

## 14. The deeper inversion

Conventional question:

> How much subsidy do we need?

Weird-system question:

> **What dependency makes that subsidy necessary?**

Examples:

**Land is expensive** → remove land appreciation from the housing cost structure.

**Rent is too high** → reduce the cost basis rather than only paying the rent.

**Financing is fragile** → create a lower-risk, durable asset.

**Affordable units expire** → retain affordability permanently in the asset.

**Income changes** → do not make housing continuity depend on poverty status.

**Monthly instability causes eviction** → build continuity reserves.

**Buildings cost money to operate** → investigate building-generated revenue and avoided costs.

**Program transactions create friction** → investigate ownership and master-lease structures that reduce tenant-level transaction costs.

**Administrative fragmentation causes delays** → design the housing operating system around the resident's address and consent.

## 15. Santa Cruz Weird Housing Lab

The deliberately Santa Cruz question:

> **What housing arrangement would sound unreasonable under today's financing, AMI, zoning, and administrative assumptions — but become reasonable if those assumptions were changed?**

Research prompts:

1. What if land is never sold?
2. What if land appreciation is not required to sustain housing?
3. What if public land is awarded through outcome competitions?
4. What if the subsidy belongs primarily to the housing asset?
5. What if the building earns part of its operating resources?
6. What if housing remains available as income rises?
7. What if a housing reserve follows a person between homes?
8. What if a building has a collective reserve?
9. What if there is no conventional landlord but professional management?
10. What if one address replaces many disconnected administrative relationships?
11. What if AMI is used for targeted assistance rather than as the organizing principle of the housing asset?
12. What if housing is financed more like infrastructure than a speculative asset?
13. What if verified resilience reduces long-run operating/public-service burdens?
14. What if existing buildings are acquired and converted into permanent housing stock?
15. What if stability is measured at 5, 10, 20, and 30 years?
16. What if becoming economically stable does not require leaving affordable housing?

## 16. Evidence discipline

Use the project evidence tags:

- **KNOWN** — documented fact or existing program/model.
- **CALCULATED** — derived from stated inputs.
- **TESTED** — empirically evaluated.
- **PROPOSED** — design concept to investigate.
- **SPECULATIVE** — mechanism not yet demonstrated.
- **UNKNOWN** — information that still needs research.

Do not treat an unusual idea as proven merely because an analogous model exists elsewhere.

Do not treat a failed historical experiment as proof that the underlying mechanism can never work.

Preserve:

- successful examples;
- failed examples;
- legal barriers;
- financing barriers;
- operating failures;
- unintended consequences;
- transaction costs;
- resident experience.

### Shared-equity housing

Urban Institute describes community land trusts and limited-equity cooperatives as shared-equity/permanent-affordability models and notes that public/shared-equity housing can increase housing insulated from private-market pressures.

Sources:

### Housing stability

Research on community land trusts has found favorable housing-stability outcomes in studied populations, including fewer moves and less financial hardship relative to renters in the cited study. These findings concern specific studied populations and should not be generalized to every shared-equity model.

Source:
https://housingmatters.urban.org/housing-matters-research/do-community-land-trusts-improve-resident-outcomes
