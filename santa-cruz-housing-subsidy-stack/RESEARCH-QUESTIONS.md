# Research Questions

## Primary question

> How many households can obtain and retain stable housing through the system, and what economic and administrative capacity is required to make that outcome durable?

## Transmission hypothesis — PROPOSED

> California's housing challenge may be partly a transmission problem: intended housing outcomes pass through a growing sequence of technical, regulatory, financial, and administrative interfaces, and each interface can introduce friction, distortion, delay, or loss.

This remains a hypothesis until tested against historical and contemporary evidence.

## Affordability vs. stability

> Does a point-in-time affordability designation predict durable housing stability?

Proposed conceptual metric:

**Housing Stability Yield = Affordable Units × Successful Lease-Up × Occupancy Continuity × Program Continuity × Long-Term Financial Viability**

This is a proposed research metric, not a government formula.

## Administrative efficiency

**Administrative Efficiency = Housing Stability Yield ÷ Administrative Transaction Cost**

Also proposed; requires operational definitions and measurable data.

## Feasibility

Conceptual feasibility chain:

**Project Capacity − Land − Construction − Financing − Fees − Standards − Affordability Burden − Administrative Cost − Risk = Residual Feasibility**

This is an analytical framework, not an accounting or legal formula.

## Outside the Stack

> What if the housing affordability problem is partly caused by repeatedly subsidizing transactions instead of designing a housing asset that requires fewer transactions to remain stable?

Candidate models for testing include public land leases, community land trusts, limited-equity cooperatives, resident ownership, cost-based rents, shared-equity structures, building-generated revenue, and lower-friction assistance.
