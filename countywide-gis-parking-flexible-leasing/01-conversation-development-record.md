# Conversation Development Record

## Origin

The parking concept emerged while discussing flexible space access and microleasing in Santa Cruz. The user distinguished the existing sidewalk-vending program from the proposed microleasing framework.

## Development Path

1. Microleasing was described as access to indoor and outdoor spaces in short blocks, potentially about 45–60 minutes, with defined expectations and responsibility.
2. The user connected the idea to parking spaces and proposed mapping every parking space through GIS.
3. The user proposed unique identifiers, jurisdictional overlays, vehicle association, and instantaneous phone-based agreements.
4. The user imagined a supportive workflow in which an agency or authorized partner could contact a responsible user, explain options, and connect the user with a nonprofit that might pay for a temporary agreement.
5. The concept was separated into its own project because parking involves distinct transportation, data-access, privacy, property, and enforcement questions.

## Preserved Distinctions

- Sidewalk vending is an existing regulatory system, not the same thing as microleasing.
- Microleasing is a proposed general framework, not an established municipal service.
- The GIS parking system is a proposed specialized application, not a claim that a county currently maintains a complete inventory of every parking space.
- Countywide coordination is a hypothesis requiring study; city, county, state, private-property, and transportation authorities may have different powers.
- A positive, assistance-oriented workflow is a design objective, not a verified legal or technical capability.

## Research Leads Mentioned During the Conversation

- City of Santa Cruz agenda item concerning eligibility for citywide permit parking.
- Existing city and county parking regulations.
- GIS mapping and curb-management systems.
- DMV and vehicle-information privacy/access restrictions.
- Nonprofit payment assistance and temporary parking agreements.

## Unresolved Questions

- Whether parking spaces can legally and operationally be offered in time-based agreements.
- Who may authorize each category of space.
- What information can be connected to a vehicle or user.
- How to prevent the system from becoming a surveillance or punitive enforcement tool.
- How to provide access for people without smartphones or payment capacity.
