# Why This Inquiry Exists

## Purpose

This project is not only a research paper. It is an ongoing civic inquiry conducted through human–AI conversation to understand how large systems operate—from the lived experience of people with the fewest resources to the decisions made by institutions and leaders with the greatest formal power.

The inquiry asks how people can use available tools, including AI, to understand systems rather than only being acted upon by them. It is intended to support citizen learning, evidence-based questioning, public participation, and the preservation of observations that might otherwise disappear in ordinary conversation.

## Access, Opportunity, and the User's Experience

The user began using AI more actively after obtaining an apartment and gaining a more stable place from which to think, learn, and work. The user describes this transition as significant because access to housing, safety, time, and space created the practical conditions needed to use AI in a sustained way.

Before that point, the user's experience included homelessness and situations in which people or systems invested time and energy into helping someone only to the extent that the person could work for them—or did not invest that effort. This experience informs a central question:

> Do systems invest in people primarily for what they can extract or obtain from them, or do they also invest in their well-being, agency, understanding, and capacity to contribute freely?

This is preserved as personal context and a question for inquiry, not as a universal claim about every person or institution.

## Why AI Is Part of the Inquiry

The user observes that Democratic and Republican political actors have both invested in AI across different areas. This does not establish that all actors share the same motive. It does indicate that AI is being treated by major political and institutional actors as a tool of substantial strategic value. The specific stated purposes, investments, and effects should be documented and compared rather than assumed.

The project therefore examines AI from the perspective of a citizen who is learning to use it directly—not only from the perspective of programmers, technology companies, governments, or think tanks.

The inquiry includes questions such as:

- Who gets access to AI and under what conditions?
- What changes when a person has housing, time, safety, and reliable access to technology?
- Can AI help people with limited resources understand policies and institutions that affect them?
- Can AI preserve lived observations while distinguishing them from verified factual claims?
- Can AI help people investigate systems from the lowest levels of lived experience through the highest levels of institutional decision-making?
- How can AI support agency rather than merely making people more useful to existing systems?

## From the Lowest to the Highest

The project intentionally connects two perspectives:

1. **Lived experience from the bottom of a system:** housing insecurity, limited bargaining power, restricted access, and the experience of being affected by decisions made elsewhere.
2. **Institutional decision-making at the top:** presidents, central banks, governments, corporations, advisors, and other actors who possess greater formal authority and resources.

The purpose is not to claim that these positions are equivalent. Their power, responsibility, choices, and ability to distribute costs differ substantially. The purpose is to investigate how decisions move through systems and how their consequences reach people differently.

The user has also noted that presidential advisors and other institutional experts may be conducting related analysis through different tools and methods. This project does not assume access to their private work or claim equivalence with it. Instead, it asks what a citizen can learn, document, and contribute through an accessible human–AI inquiry.

## Working Principle

Access to a tool is not the same as access to meaningful opportunity. A person may possess intelligence, curiosity, experience, and a desire to contribute while lacking the housing, time, security, money, or technology needed to develop those capacities.

The inquiry therefore evaluates systems not only by their outputs or efficiency, but also by:

- Who can participate.
- Who receives the tools and support needed to participate.
- Whether support is conditional on extractable labor or value.
- Whether people gain greater agency and understanding.
- Whether the system protects people who have the least ability to absorb failure or disruption.
- Whether knowledge generated through the process is preserved and made useful to others.

## Relationship to the Main Project

This rationale supports the project's investigation of energy, finance, geopolitical shocks, household burdens, AI access, and the ocean as a possible next extractive frontier.

The common question is:

> When systems encounter people, resources, or limits, do they respond primarily through extraction and control, or can they be redesigned to increase resilience, agency, protection, and shared capacity?

The project should preserve both the personal origin of the inquiry and the evidence-based research that follows. Personal experience provides questions and perspective; reliable sources, transparent calculations, testing, and clearly marked uncertainty provide the basis for factual conclusions.

## Evidence Posture

- **KNOWN:** What is directly documented or reliably sourced.
- **PROPOSED:** Ideas and questions generated through the inquiry.
- **SPECULATIVE:** Possibilities requiring further investigation.
- **CALCULATED:** Conclusions derived through explicit calculations or models.
- **TESTED:** Claims evaluated through reproducible procedures or evidence.
- **UNKNOWN:** Questions for which sufficient information is not yet available.

The purpose of preserving this document is to ensure that the project remembers not only what it studies, but why the inquiry began and whose perspective it is intended to include.
