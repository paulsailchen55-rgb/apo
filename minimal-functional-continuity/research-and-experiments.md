# Minimal Functional Continuity — Research and Experiments

## Status

Supplement to the existing Minimal Functional Continuity archive.

## Core question

What is the smallest set of services and energy capacity required to preserve basic residential function during a short disruption?

Examples discussed include:
- phone
- Chromebook
- router/connectivity where available
- lighting
- refrigeration alternatives
- low-energy cooking
- fans/air movement
- air filtration
- basic charging

## Measurement approach

Record actual device wattage, duty cycle, hours of use, and charging frequency before choosing battery size.

Avoid designing around assumed household loads when direct measurement is available.

## Energy experiments

Potential low-power sources discussed:
- soil/earth rod trickle charging
- rain
- wind
- sound
- piezoelectric mechanisms
- solar

These remain experiments or research directions unless independently validated.

## Safety

Battery selection should consider chemistry, thermal behavior, certification, enclosure, installation, failure modes, and local code. The project does not assume that a single chemistry is automatically safe.

## Continuity principle

The target is **minimal functional continuity**, not full off-grid independence.

The project asks what can keep a person connected to essential functions long enough for normal systems to recover.
