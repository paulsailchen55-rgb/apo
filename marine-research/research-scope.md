# Marine Research — Scope and Provenance

## Classification
Environmental research direction.

## Origin
The conversation raised questions about ocean mineral extraction, including oil-related extraction, and about possible relationships among marine industrial activity, propeller cavitation, underwater noise, heat and ecological effects.

## What is established here
The conversation establishes that these are questions the user wants researched. It does not establish that a particular proposed activity causes a particular environmental harm.

## Research questions
1. What forms of ocean mineral extraction are currently proposed, permitted, prohibited or under study in relevant jurisdictions?
2. What physical mechanisms produce underwater sound from vessels, pumps, drilling, dredging or other equipment?
3. What is known about propeller cavitation, including acoustic output and localized pressure/temperature effects?
4. What evidence exists concerning ecological effects of underwater noise or thermal changes at relevant scales?
5. How do cumulative effects, vessel traffic and seasonal biological conditions affect interpretation?
6. Which agencies have jurisdiction over different activities?
7. What alternatives or pause/review mechanisms exist when evidence is incomplete?

## Provenance discipline
Keep separate:
- measured observations;
- established engineering mechanisms;
- peer-reviewed or agency evidence;
- community reports and concerns;
- hypotheses requiring testing;
- policy preferences.

## Public communication direction
The user's preferred public framing was to investigate alternatives and allow a pause/review where warranted, rather than using an absolute claim that the ocean must never be touched. Public materials should remain secular and evidence-based.

## Status
Research direction. Technical and regulatory claims require current external-source verification before use in advocacy or government correspondence.
