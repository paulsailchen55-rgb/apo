## The Butterfly — Distributed Biological Computation

A speculative concept developed from the fruit-compute discussion: extremely low-energy computational nodes could use biological energy as a limited computational budget and communicate as a distributed network. The engineering concept is intentionally distinct from the energy required for motors or other physical actuation.

The corresponding philosophical question is broader: **what if distributed computation is already present in living systems, including the collective activity of flying insects?**

This is preserved as a speculative meditation, not as a scientific claim. The project should distinguish clearly between documented biology, engineering prototypes, hypotheses, metaphor, and philosophical exploration.

See [butterfly/THE-BUTTERFLY-MEDITATION.md](butterfly/THE-BUTTERFLY-MEDITATION.md).

**Working principle:** biological energy may support information processing without making biological abundance an excuse for unlimited extraction.

**Omega Principle:** consider not only what a technological system can do, but its complete material and energetic life cycle — origin → use → consequence → end → return.

> Preserve the possibility before deciding what it is.

This repository is an archive.

It is a place to preserve ideas, conversations, experiments, designs, questions, fragments, and possible directions that may or may not become something else later.

Nothing here should be assumed to be a finished system.

Some material may be incomplete.
Some ideas may contradict one another.
Some proposals may turn out to be impractical.
Some may become useful only when combined with something discovered later.

That is part of the purpose.

## Why this exists

Ideas are easy to lose.

A conversation can contain a useful architecture, a question can become a research direction, and a seemingly unrelated observation can eventually become the missing piece of a larger system.

`apo` provides a persistent place to put those possibilities so they can be referenced rather than reconstructed from memory.

The repository is therefore intentionally broader than a conventional software project.

It may contain:

- conceptual architectures
- AI and human-interface ideas
- institutional-navigation concepts
- provenance and audit models
- accessibility ideas
- security concepts
- privacy and device-control ideas
- legal and policy research
- technical experiments
- questions that still need answers
- rejected or superseded approaches
- conversations that provide context for later work
- prototypes and other artifacts

## An archive, not an authority

The presence of an idea in this repository does not mean that the idea has been proven, validated, legally established, scientifically established, or endorsed.

The archive preserves **possibilities**.

Where appropriate, later work should distinguish between:

- observation
- personal experience
- interpretation
- hypothesis
- proposal
- documented fact
- external evidence
- unresolved question
- disputed claim
- working implementation

The goal is to preserve the path from an initial thought to whatever it eventually becomes.

## AI and conversation

Some material may originate in conversations with AI.

AI may help organize, translate, summarize, connect, or develop ideas, but AI output should not automatically be treated as human-authored fact or authority.

Where practical, future tooling can preserve provenance showing:

**original input → AI transformation → human revision → later transformation → resulting artifact**

That distinction matters.

The purpose of using AI here is not to replace human thought. It is to make more of that thought preservable, searchable, understandable, and reusable.

## Relationship to other projects

Individual ideas may eventually become:

- software projects
- research papers
- technical specifications
- legal research
- public-interest infrastructure
- experiments
- documentation
- standards proposals
- entirely separate repositories

When that happens, the resulting project can point back here to preserve its history and context.

Conversely, this archive may reference projects that have already developed elsewhere.

## Folder principle

Projects that become substantial should live in their own folders.

That keeps the repository usable as an archive without requiring every possibility to become a top-level project.

A folder can therefore be:

- a complete project
- an experiment
- a research collection
- a conversation-derived concept
- a prototype
- a historical snapshot
- a collection of related possibilities

There is no requirement that every folder reach completion.

## The basic rule

> **Preserve the possibility before deciding what it is.**

This repository exists so that ideas have somewhere to go while they are still becoming.
