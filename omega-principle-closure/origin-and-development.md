# Origin and Development Notes

## Provenance

The idea was raised in conversation on September 18, 2026, after the user described wanting "more clarity" on what they call the Omega principle.

The user's description was approximately:

- Think about the end of things, especially when first building them or while using them.
- Ask whether present use is conducive toward the proper, honorable use of its end.
- Apply the same concern to builders and makers.
- When making something from materials extracted from other things/the earth, have some form of faith or assurance that it can be put back into the earth somehow.
- The user and assistant had previously discussed this as a type of "closure."
- The broader idea was described as thinking about closure of everything.

This file records the conceptual provenance rather than treating the wording as a finalized definition.

## Assistant interpretation already present in the conversation

The assistant summarized the distinction as:

The Omega principle is not merely "think about the end." It is closer to letting the end inform the beginning and the present use.

The assistant also described a possible life-cycle sequence:

earth/materials → making → use → end → return, reuse, transformation, or dissolution.

That is an interpretation/organization of the user's idea, not an independent source or established definition.

## Relationship to existing work

There is already a separate endpoint-closure-stur-outreach-2026-09-17 archive in apo. That work concerns usable endpoints for systems, relationships, obligations, records, accounts, contracts, and similar arrangements.

The Omega principle overlaps with that work through the concept of closure, but the subjects are not identical:

- Endpoint/STUR work asks whether a system has a usable way to conclude a relationship or obligation.
- Omega asks whether the design and present use of a thing are oriented toward its proper end from the beginning, including possible material return/closure.

Do not merge these into one project merely because both use the word "closure."

Omega also has a possible relationship to modular infrastructure, resilience, housing, environmental research, and civic process work. Those relationships are currently supporting hypotheses rather than reasons to merge the projects.
