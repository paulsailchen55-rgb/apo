# Breeze — Residential Material Reduction Appliance

**Status:** PROPOSED / CONCEPTUAL  
**Date:** 2026-09-23  
**Related project:** Apartment Circular Materials Standard

## Working Name

**Breeze** is a working name for a residential material-size-reduction appliance concept. The name is intentionally provisional and refers to the concept of using controlled airflow, compressed air, or another low-energy air-assisted mechanism to reduce the physical volume of household materials.

The final mechanism does **not** have to use sandblasting or compressed air. Those are conceptual starting points. The engineering objective is to determine the safest, quietest, lowest-energy method.

## Core Idea

A resident could disassemble permitted bulky household materials as necessary and place them into a **fully enclosed processing chamber**. The appliance would reduce the material's physical size and volume, producing a contained output that remains part of the applicable waste/material-management system.

The appliance does **not** make waste legally disappear.

The resident remains responsible for following the approved input list, and the processed output remains subject to applicable collection, recycling, specialty-processing, and disposal requirements until an authorized downstream process accepts it.

## Intended Residential Use

Potential use cases include:

- broken furniture after appropriate disassembly;
- selected wood products;
- selected household materials;
- selected bulky residual materials;
- other materials determined safe and lawful for the appliance.

Potentially hazardous or specially regulated materials would be excluded through an explicit input specification.

Examples requiring special treatment or exclusion may include:

- batteries;
- refrigerants and refrigeration equipment;
- certain electronics/e-waste;
- hazardous chemicals;
- pressurized containers;
- materials containing unknown hazardous substances;
- other materials identified by applicable law or safety testing.

## Apartment Operating Model

### Day Mode

- Loading.
- Material inspection.
- Disassembly.
- Maintenance.
- Short permitted processing cycles when appropriate.

### Night Mode

The preferred operating model is an **automatic overnight cycle**.

The resident loads and closes the chamber during the day, starts one complete processing program, and allows the appliance to finish without continuous attendance.

Night mode should prioritize:

- very low noise;
- very low vibration;
- controlled energy consumption;
- sealed containment;
- automatic fault detection;
- automatic shutdown;
- no external exhaust;
- no exposed particulate release.

The machine should be designed to complete a cycle once started rather than requiring repeated user intervention.

## Apartment Compatibility Targets

These are proposed engineering targets, not established legal requirements:

- ordinary residential electrical service where feasible;
- no permanent building modification;
- no external compressed-air infrastructure;
- no exterior vent;
- no objectionable odor;
- minimal structure-borne vibration;
- substantially below applicable residential noise limits;
- safe unattended operation;
- sealed processing chamber;
- contained output.

## Waste-System Relationship

The concept is **not** intended to replace municipal or authorized private waste collection.

The intended pathway is:

**Household material → Breeze → contained reduced-volume material → existing collection or authorized downstream processor**

The project should investigate whether residential size reduction can occur within existing California, CalRecycle, county, city, building, fire, electrical, nuisance, noise, lease, and waste-management frameworks.

If a downstream biological or specialized treatment process is developed, it should initially be treated as a separate processing stage rather than assuming the residential appliance itself may perform regulated waste treatment.

## Biological Post-Processing — Separate Research Track

A separate research concept involves biological transformation of appropriately selected materials.

For clean lignocellulosic material, fungal cultivation concepts may be investigated where the substrate is known to be suitable and free of prohibited contaminants.

For selected plastics, enzyme- or microorganism-assisted degradation may be investigated as a separate controlled process.

These concepts are **PROPOSED / SPECULATIVE** until their chemistry, safety, environmental fate, and legal status are established.

The project must not assume that biological degradation automatically makes a material safe or legally disposable.

## Regulatory Principle

A central project principle is:

> **Physical size reduction is not the same thing as legal disposal.**

The appliance changes the physical state and volume of material. Legal responsibility continues until the resulting material is accepted by an appropriate collection, recycling, processing, or disposal pathway.

## Narrow-Path Regulatory Strategy

The preferred research strategy is not initially to seek a new law.

First determine whether a compliant residential appliance can fit within existing rules by controlling:

1. permitted inputs;
2. processing method;
3. electrical load;
4. noise;
5. vibration;
6. dust and particulate containment;
7. fire safety;
8. building compatibility;
9. lease restrictions;
10. output containment;
11. collection requirements; and
12. downstream processing requirements.

If existing regulations do not provide a workable category for the appliance, document the precise regulatory gap before proposing any change.

## Evidence Categories

**KNOWN**
- Existing waste systems regulate material handling and final disposition.
- Residential noise and appliance operation can be regulated independently of whether an appliance is otherwise lawful.
- Different household materials have different recycling, hazardous-material, e-waste, and disposal requirements.

**CALCULATED**
- Electrical loads, processing energy, cycle time, volume reduction, acoustic performance, and mechanical energy once a candidate mechanism is selected.

**TESTED**
- To be populated through controlled prototype testing.

**PROPOSED**
- Breeze working name.
- Overnight processing model.
- Sealed residential material-size-reduction appliance.
- Explicit permitted-input matrix.
- Existing-waste-system-compatible output pathway.

**SPECULATIVE**
- Air-assisted or compressed-air particle reduction.
- Biological post-processing pathways.
- Enzyme-assisted plastic transformation.

**UNKNOWN**
- Exact legal classification of a residential size-reduction appliance.
- Exact acceptable output specifications for local haulers/processors.
- Lease restrictions applicable to particular rental properties.
- Required product certification and permitting for a manufactured appliance.
- Whether particular biological processing methods would be permitted at residential or commercial scale.

## Naming Note

“Breeze” is a working name only. Other names involving wind, air, airflow, or easy/breezy operation may be considered later.

The name should not imply that the machine literally uses compressed air unless that becomes an established design feature.
