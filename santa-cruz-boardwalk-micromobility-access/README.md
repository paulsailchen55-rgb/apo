# Santa Cruz Boardwalk Micromobility Access — Archive Note

## Archive status

This folder preserves a conversation and emerging civic-policy idea as an **Archive of Possibilities** item. It is not presented as a finished project, legal opinion, or established finding.

The `apo` repository is being treated as an archive/reference repository rather than as the project itself.

## Original context

A photo was taken while walking through the Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk area. The photographed Boardwalk safety-rules sign includes, among other restrictions:

- No smoking
- No weapons
- No amplified music
- No pets
- No wheeled, roller, or sport devices
- No drones
- No disruptive, abusive, or threatening behavior
- No explicit or graphic imagery
- No tripods
- No glass containers
- No masks, except for health/medical-safety reasons
- No soliciting or sales

The immediate question concerned a **manual Razor scooter** and whether it may be used in the nearby public bikeway/transportation facility. The observation was that signs may appear on one side of the corridor but not the other, while separate signs elsewhere reportedly say no skateboarding or no skate devices. The underlying concern was whether a Boardwalk property rule is being confused with a City rule governing a public bikeway.

A related concern was broader: why transportation infrastructure may be organized around device categories such as bicycles while excluding other low-speed forms of movement such as scooters, skateboards, roller skates, or inline skates. The conversation explored whether a shared low-speed facility could address safety through operating rules rather than through narrow device categories.

A second, related access question concerned activities that may be permitted inside licensed or commercial beachfront spaces while being restricted in ordinary public areas. The point was not necessarily that private/commercial rules should be eliminated, but that people without paid access should have reasonable public alternatives where feasible.

## Core distinction to verify

The Boardwalk's own property rules are not necessarily the same thing as City of Santa Cruz traffic, sidewalk, or bikeway rules. A Boardwalk prohibition on wheeled devices should therefore **not automatically be treated as a prohibition on every public street or bikeway near the Boardwalk**.

The useful factual/legal question is:

> If a nonmotorized Razor scooter is not permitted on the specific public bikeway in question, what exact statute, municipal ordinance, resolution, facility rule, or posted restriction prohibits it?

This question should be answered for the **specific segment and specific device**, rather than inferred from a nearby Boardwalk sign.

## Possible policy concept

One possible future proposal is a **low-speed active-transportation / micromobility facility** that could accommodate appropriate devices, potentially including:

- Bicycles
- E-bikes where otherwise lawful
- Manual scooters
- Skateboards
- Roller skates / inline skates
- Other appropriate low-speed human-powered devices
- Mobility devices as required by applicable law

The concept is intentionally a possibility, not a claim that all of these devices should automatically share every bikeway.

Instead of relying solely on device labels, safety could be addressed with rules such as:

- Pedestrians have priority at crossings and shared areas.
- A posted maximum speed applies.
- Users yield where required.
- Passing is controlled.
- Racing and stunts are prohibited.
- Users dismount in designated high-conflict areas.
- Device dimensions and operating characteristics can be regulated where necessary.

The proposal could also be limited to particular blocks, hours, or facility designs if those conditions prove necessary for safety.

## Access and equity question

A related policy question is whether people have reasonable, free or low-cost public alternatives when an activity is restricted in ordinary public space but permitted in a commercial/private setting.

The relevant questions include:

1. What is the safety or operational reason for the restriction?
2. Is the restriction applied consistently to comparable activities?
3. What public space remains available for the activity?
4. Is there a free or low-cost alternative nearby?
5. Could a designated space or time period accommodate the activity safely?
6. Are the rules based on actual risk, or primarily on device/category definitions?
7. Are private/commercial permissions based on licensing, controlled premises, or other legal distinctions that do not apply to public space?

This framework preserves the concern without assuming that different rules for commercial and public spaces are automatically unlawful or discriminatory.

## Suggested civic request

A concise future request to the City could read:

> Santa Cruz provides separated bicycle infrastructure along the Beach Street corridor. I request that the City evaluate whether appropriate portions of the bikeway can be designated for shared use by bicycles and other low-speed human-powered wheeled devices, including nonmotorized scooters, skateboards, and roller/inline skates, subject to reasonable speed, yielding, pedestrian-safety, and operating rules.
>
> If nonmotorized scooters are currently prohibited from the specific Beach Street bikeway, please identify the exact statute, municipal ordinance, resolution, facility rule, or posted restriction establishing that prohibition and explain whether the City has evaluated a shared active-transportation/micromobility designation.

## Research questions / unresolved issues

These items remain open and should be verified before using this archive note for enforcement, advocacy, or legal purposes:

- Identify the exact location of the photographed Boardwalk sign.
- Identify the exact Beach Street facility/segment at issue.
- Determine whether the Razor scooter is manual or motorized and confirm its relevant legal classification.
- Identify all signs governing the specific segment, including signs on both sides of the corridor.
- Check the current California Vehicle Code provisions applicable to nonmotorized scooters, skateboards, roller skates, bicycles, and bikeways.
- Check the current Santa Cruz municipal code and any facility-specific resolutions or rules.
- Determine whether the relevant bikeway is a bicycle-only facility, a shared-use path, or another facility type.
- Determine whether any City rule distinguishes manual scooters from motorized scooters or other micromobility devices.
- Verify current rules rather than relying on historical ordinances or older project documents.

## Important limitation

This document preserves an exploratory conversation and a possible policy direction. It **does not determine that a particular Razor scooter is currently legal or illegal at a particular location**, and it does not establish that any restriction is discriminatory, irrational, or unlawful.

Any such conclusion would require verification of the current law, exact location, posted signs, facility design, and device characteristics.
