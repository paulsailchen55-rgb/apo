# Concept and Provenance

## User-originated starting point

The user described a personal memory from homelessness: wishing there had been an online network of neighbors where a person could pay a modest amount—less than a hotel and roughly in a campground-like range—for a short stay and then continue traveling through the community.

The imagined network could include:
- a driveway or other permitted parking location
- a place for a car, van, trike, bicycle, or small camper
- a couch or other hospitality resource
- water
- vehicle washing
- a place to perform limited vehicle work
- a small host-provided tool or maintenance kit

The user's stated goal was not simply cheap lodging. It was to create a legitimate pathway through a neighborhood that might reduce the pressure to sleep or work on a vehicle in unauthorized places.

## Important interpretation

The idea combines several models:
- hospitality network
- low-cost campsite/parking network
- neighborhood mutual aid
- marketplace
- resource-sharing network
- trust and safety system

It should not be forced into only one of those categories yet.

## User hypothesis

The user proposed that if people have an affordable, explicitly permitted place to stop, they may cause less disruption than if they have no lawful alternative.

This is a hypothesis to test.

## User safety concern

The user also identified the opposite risk: if an app exposes helpful people's homes, a bad actor might use the network to locate and exploit those people.

This is a design requirement, not evidence that network participants are dangerous.

## Assistant-generated organization

The assistant organized the idea into:
- jurisdiction-aware participation
- property authority checks
- lease/mortgage/insurance checks
- privacy-first location disclosure
- trust and abuse resistance
- resource-specific eligibility
- short-stay design
- a potential API/marketplace layer

These are organizational interpretations, not claims that the user had already designed a final architecture.

## Existing-service context

The conversation compared the concept informally with Airbnb, Couchsurfing, Nextdoor, Neighbor, and other hospitality/camping networks. These comparisons are exploratory. Current service capabilities and legal structures require independent verification before being treated as factual precedents.

## Open status

No final name has been selected. “Traveling Neighborhood Resource Network” is an archive label, not a claimed organization or business name.
