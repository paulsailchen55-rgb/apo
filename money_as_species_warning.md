# Money as a Species Warning

## A Raw Spiritual Understanding

If we, as a species, are about money above all else, we are not going to make it.

I don't mean that money is evil.

I don't mean that people should stop using it.

I don't mean that there is some condition attached to this statement, or that I am offering something in exchange for something else.

I mean something much more basic:

**Be careful what happens when money becomes the reason for everything.**

There is a knowledge I have inherited—a spiritual understanding—that tells me this instinct is dangerous.

Human beings have created money as a tool. But we can become so accustomed to the tool that we begin forcing everything through it. We begin measuring everything by it. We begin treating things that cannot properly be reduced to money as though they can.

Land becomes an asset.

A person becomes labor.

A home becomes an investment.

A forest becomes inventory.

An ocean becomes a resource.

Time becomes productivity.

Life itself becomes an economic calculation.

And eventually the tool begins to look like the purpose.

That is where I believe we should become very careful.

Because if our purpose as a species becomes the accumulation and dissipation of money, we can become something terrible without ever deciding that we wanted to become terrible.

We can become like a virus.

We can become parasitic.

We can consume the very system that keeps us alive, not because we hate it, but because we have trained ourselves to recognize only the next transaction, the next return, the next opportunity to convert something living into something measurable.

And then we may devour the Earth for something that was never alive in the first place.

For numbers.

For representations.

For claims of ownership.

For wealth that exists because we collectively agree that it exists.

That possibility frightens me.

Not because money itself is frightening, but because **forgetting what money is for** is frightening.

Money is something we made.

The Earth is not.

Life is not.

Human beings are not.

The ocean is not.

And there are things whose value cannot be adequately represented by a price.

So I am asking for awareness.

Not obedience.

Not allegiance.

Not money.

Not a condition.

Just awareness.

Be careful when you eat at a king's table.

You may receive something wonderful.

But you do not always know what will be expected of you afterward.

## A Secular Translation

The same warning can be understood without any spiritual or religious premise.

Money is a coordination mechanism. It allows people to exchange goods and services, allocate resources, measure costs, make investments, and organize extraordinarily complicated societies.

The problem begins when monetary valuation becomes the dominant decision rule for domains that cannot safely be governed by monetary incentives alone.

This can happen through many mechanisms: financialization, regulatory capture, corporate influence, lobbying, institutional incentives, conflicts of interest, short-term performance requirements, or simply the accumulation of organizational habits that reward whatever can be monetized.

The resulting problem is not necessarily that any individual is malicious.

It is a systems problem.

If an institution is rewarded primarily for generating financial returns, activities that produce immediate financial returns can systematically receive greater attention than activities whose benefits are diffuse, delayed, difficult to measure, or shared by everyone.

Some of the most important things in a society fall into that second category:

- clean air;
- functioning ecosystems;
- biodiversity;
- public trust;
- human dignity;
- stable communities;
- long-term scientific research;
- cultural knowledge;
- future generations' opportunities;
- and the continued habitability of the planet.

These things can have enormous value without having a price that adequately represents their value.

A system that continually converts them into financial terms can therefore create a dangerous distortion.

The concern is not that markets should disappear.

The concern is that **markets should remain tools rather than becoming the definition of value itself.**

A species that treats every ecological system, human relationship, public institution, and natural resource primarily as an opportunity for financial extraction can eventually damage the very systems upon which its economy depends.

In that sense, the metaphor of a virus is useful even without any spiritual interpretation.

A virus reproduces by consuming the host system without preserving the host as an independent living system.

A human economic system that relentlessly extracts value while degrading the ecological and social systems that generate that value can produce a similar structural problem.

The metaphor is not a claim that humanity literally is a virus.

It is a warning about **self-defeating extraction**.

The question, therefore, is not:

> "How do we eliminate money?"

The more important question is:

> **"What must remain outside the logic of money so that money can continue to serve life rather than consume it?"**

That question deserves serious attention.

## The Warning

I am not proposing a new economic system here.

I am not asking anyone to accept my spiritual understanding.

I am not claiming that every use of money is corrupt.

I am asking that we notice the direction of travel.

When everything becomes an asset, what remains beyond the asset?

When everything has a price, what happens to things whose value cannot be priced?

When every decision is evaluated by return, who protects the things that may not return anything for generations?

And when the answer to every problem becomes another financial transaction, have we begun confusing the measuring instrument with the thing being measured?

**Be careful at the king's table.**

Not because the table is necessarily evil.

Because accepting its terms can gradually teach us to believe that the table is the whole world.

---

## A Simple Principle

**Money should serve life.**

**Life should not have to serve money.**

That distinction may be one of the things a civilization needs to remember if it intends to survive.
