# Letter Principles and Intentions

The outward letter should be simple and readable. Its depth should come from the life and meaning behind it.

Requirements:
- preserve the difficulty of the transition;
- do not reduce the story to generic gratitude;
- do not require exact dates;
- do not expose unnecessary private details;
- do not accuse government or programs;
- do not portray housing as guaranteed or simple;
- do not erase spiritual meaning;
- do not assume recipients share Christianity;
- keep government-process research separate;
- do not turn the letter into an academic biblical paper;
- do not call the housing arrangement a gift;
- preserve construction and housing-design perspective without turning the letter into a technical proposal.

Romans 16 is the principal rhetorical model: individually recognizing people and their contributions within a wider community.

Each recipient should receive a personalized opening and, where appropriate, a specific paragraph explaining a documented role. The reusable body can remain substantially consistent.
