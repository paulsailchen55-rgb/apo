# Atmospheric Water and Mobile Power

## Concept

A movable cart or trailer-mounted service unit would use an atmospheric water generator (AWG) to produce water from local air, then provide cold or heated water at an authorized community location.

The unit should be transportable in a truck or towable trailer, capable of being unloaded and wheeled into position, operated, and removed without permanent site installation.

## Proposed Systems

- Atmospheric water generator
- Water storage and sanitation controls
- Filtration and disinfection appropriate to the selected equipment
- Heating system capable of producing safely dispensed hot water
- Battery power station and charging system
- Monitoring and service records
- Secure storage for ingredient packets and cups
- Human-operated dispensing and supervision

## Verification Principle

Water being generated from local air does not, by itself, establish that the resulting water is potable. The project must verify the complete system, including air quality assumptions, condensation surfaces, storage, filtration, disinfection, plumbing materials, maintenance, and dispensing conditions.

ASSE 1090 is a relevant drinking-water atmospheric-water-generator performance standard, but it does not by itself verify every chemical, particulate, or site-specific purity claim. A local public-health authority must determine the applicable requirements.

## Location Data

The system should maintain a record for each service session, potentially including:

- Device identifier
- Date and time
- Geographic location
- Water production and dispensing volumes
- Filter and disinfection status
- Maintenance history
- Testing results and laboratory references
- Operator identity or shift record
- Incident and corrective-action records

Location data should be designed with privacy, security, retention, and access controls in mind.

## Research Questions

1. Which AWG models are designed and certified for drinking-water use?
2. What output rate is realistic under Santa Cruz weather conditions?
3. How much battery energy is required for generation, heating, and sanitation?
4. Can the system safely operate from stored battery power?
5. What testing frequency is required by the relevant authority?
6. What happens when humidity is too low or demand exceeds production?
7. Is an approved backup potable-water source necessary?
8. What maintenance and calibration records must be retained?

## Status

Early design concept. No equipment, certification, permit, or safety approval has yet been selected or obtained.
