# Anonymized Recent Synthesis — AI Secretary / Election GIS / Civic Institutional GIS

Date: 2026-09-24

## Privacy scope

This synthesis intentionally omits personal names, exact location, housing-provider identity, health information, personal financial information, and other identifying circumstances.

It preserves the project-level ideas that developed across recent conversations.

## 1. From AI note-taking to AI Secretary

The starting idea was an AI tool that could accept speech, webpages, PDFs, notes, and conversations; make an immediate brief; develop rough ideas into fuller concepts; and route different tasks to appropriate AI systems or tools.

The concept became an **AI Secretary / AI Router**:

RAW INPUT
-> identify the task
-> create a brief
-> route specialized research
-> synthesize evidence
-> map relationships
-> preserve provenance
-> return control to the human

The Secretary is a conductor rather than necessarily the only AI model.

## 2. Election Secretary

The election use case developed into:

DISCOVERY
-> BRIEF
-> EVIDENCE DOSSIER
-> ADVERSARIAL RESEARCH
-> SOURCE COMPARISON
-> RELATIONAL MAP
-> DECISION TREE
-> HUMAN DECISION
-> ARCHIVE

The purpose is to help a person understand what they are actually being asked to decide.

The system must not decide how the person should vote.

## 3. Votable versus relevant

A central distinction:

**VOTABLE BY ME != RELEVANT TO ME**

Three circles:

### MY BALLOT
Things the person can vote on.

### MY GOVERNMENT
Officials, institutions, programs, and policies governing the community even when not up for election.

### MY ENVIRONMENT
State, federal, regional, neighboring, regulatory, economic, environmental, and other decisions that affect the community even when the person cannot directly vote on them.

## 4. Election GIS

The project moved beyond a list of candidates.

The map can contain layers for:

- jurisdictions
- offices
- candidates
- measures
- laws
- policies
- organizations
- funding
- appointments
- affected populations
- evidence
- uncertainty
- time

Candidates are research objects, not merely biographies.

Two candidates can be placed on the same geographic and institutional terrain without producing a ranking.

## 5. Election consolidation as a GIS layer

A local election-consolidation resolution demonstrated that election administration itself has geography.

A single election can contain overlapping:

- county jurisdictions
- cities
- school districts
- community college districts
- fire districts
- water districts
- recreation districts
- health-care districts
- other special districts
- statewide and federal structures

A consolidation resolution can put measures into the same election machinery without deciding the merits of those measures.

Therefore election GIS needs overlapping boundaries and authorities, not just candidate names.

## 6. Measures

A measure should be modeled through:

- legal text
- existing law
- proposed change
- authority
- implementation
- fiscal effects
- affected populations
- geographic effects
- time horizon
- documented arguments
- official analysis
- independent analysis
- uncertainty
- interactions with other laws or measures

## 7. Public-service sustainability as a case-study pattern

A local publicly funded service/housing system became a case study for a larger question:

> Does the funding and institutional structure provide enough durable capacity to perform the services, maintain the physical infrastructure, satisfy regulatory requirements, and preserve continuity over time?

This is deliberately different from asking whether an organization is good, bad, profitable, insolvent, or extractive.

The research variables include:

- annual revenue
- annual expenses
- assets
- liabilities
- net assets
- restricted resources
- contract reimbursement
- other funding
- staffing
- property
- maintenance
- compliance
- administration
- outside vendors
- local economic retention
- durable local capacity

Financial flow alone should not be treated as proof of extraction.

## 8. Institutional knowledge

A major systems insight:

**written procedure is not the same as institutional knowledge.**

Experienced personnel may know:

- historical context
- relationships
- pending matters
- property history
- procedural sequencing
- exceptions
- timing
- practical dependencies

When experienced personnel leave, some knowledge can disappear even when formal documentation remains.

Therefore staff continuity can be a service-capacity variable.

## 9. Sustainable Service Capacity Review

A proposed analytical tool:

**contract obligations**
+
**actual documented cost**
+
**required staffing**
+
**property/infrastructure capacity**
+
**regulatory burden**
+
**continuity capacity**
versus
**available funding**

The objective is not to presume a funding shortage.

The objective is to determine whether the system can demonstrate that the resources available are sufficient for the obligations being purchased.

## 10. Economic-flow layer

A broader research question emerged:

> When resources enter a community, how much becomes local wages, local services, physical assets, durable capacity, outside spending, administration, or future public cost?

Possible flow:

FUNDING SOURCE
-> CONTRACT
-> ORGANIZATION
-> PEOPLE / PROPERTY / VENDORS / SERVICES
-> LOCAL OR EXTERNAL DESTINATION
-> DURABLE CAPACITY / CONSUMPTION / FUTURE COST

This is descriptive economic mapping.

The system should not begin with the conclusion that a system is extractive.

## 11. Civic Institutional GIS

The project then expanded from Election GIS to **Civic Institutional GIS**.

### Geography
Where things are.

### Authority
Who can decide or regulate.

### People
Who occupies roles and how documented careers move through institutions.

### Organizations
Which institutions interact.

### Money
Where funding originates and flows.

### Programs
What public functions are being delivered.

### Property
Who owns, leases, develops, maintains, and regulates physical infrastructure.

### Decisions
Which bodies make which decisions.

### Time
When relationships exist and how they change.

## 12. Institutional trajectories

A static biography gives a snapshot.

A time-aware map asks:

> Where did the person or institution come from, what changed, what relationships appeared or disappeared, and where did the trajectory lead?

Possible snapshots:

T0 -> T1 -> T2 -> T3 -> T4 -> T5

The map should show documented relationships without converting career movement into an accusation of improper influence.

## 13. Government beyond elected officials

The civic system includes more than elected representatives:

VOTERS
-> ELECTED OFFICIALS
-> APPOINTMENTS / ADMINISTRATORS
-> DEPARTMENTS / BOARDS / COMMISSIONS
-> CONTRACTS / FUNDING / REGULATION
-> PROGRAMS / SERVICE PROVIDERS
-> RESIDENTS

with feedback:

OUTCOMES
-> REPORTS
-> EVALUATION
-> BUDGETS
-> RECOMMENDATIONS
-> FUTURE DECISIONS

This is a feedback system.

## 14. Relationships that are hard to see in a normal candidate dossier

A dossier may not reveal:

- who appoints whom
- who supervises whom
- where administrators previously worked
- how programs connect departments
- how contracts connect institutions
- how funding connects jurisdictions
- how property connects service organizations
- how people move between public, nonprofit, and administrative roles
- how relationships change over decades

The GIS should expose these as documented relationships and time-series transitions.

## 15. Global extension

The Election Secretary is intended to work across:

- multiparty democracies
- presidential systems
- parliamentary systems
- proportional systems
- preferential voting
- referendums
- dominant-party systems
- competitive authoritarian systems
- single-party systems
- transitional or hybrid systems

The political system itself must be a layer.

An election should not automatically be interpreted as a competitive democratic transfer of power.

## 16. Risks

The same technology could become:

- a persuasion engine
- a political profiling system
- a citation-laundering machine
- a false-balance machine
- a visual persuasion system
- automated political publishing infrastructure
- a civic-information gatekeeper

The project therefore needs:

- source provenance
- visible uncertainty
- human review
- inspectable routing
- privacy protections
- no inferred voting preferences
- no automatic political ranking
- separation between research and publication

## 17. Evidence language

Use:

**KNOWN / CALCULATED / TESTED / PROPOSED / SPECULATIVE / UNKNOWN**

Where useful:

**DOCUMENTED CLAIM / CONTESTED / UNVERIFIED**

Do not silently transform:

relationship -> influence

financial flow -> extraction

correlation -> causation

AI synthesis -> fact

## 18. Core emerging definition

> **Civic Institutional GIS is a time-aware relational map of geography, authority, people, institutions, funding, programs, property, decisions, and outcomes that helps people understand how governmental systems connect to lived effects.**

Election GIS is one application of this larger model.
