# Emergency Risk Designation, Monitoring, and Closure

**Classification:** PROPOSED research/design project  
**Status:** Early-stage archival concept  
**Primary domain:** Emergency resilience, public-sector system design, privacy, AI governance, records governance, crisis-response closure  
**Origin:** Conversation on 2026-09-24 concerning a proposed county suicide-prevention proclamation and a hypothetical failure mode in which emergency-risk identification becomes persistent monitoring, record accumulation, secondary use, or indefinite intervention.

## Why this belongs in APO

This is not simply a critique of suicide prevention. It is a broader emergency-resilience design question:

> **How does a system intended to protect a person during an emergency prevent the emergency designation from becoming a durable mechanism of surveillance, extraction, classification, punishment, or control after the original safety purpose has ended?**

The concept belongs as a separate APO project because it crosses several existing bodies of work—AI-assisted closure, Virtual Twin user authority, transient information pathways, civic communication, and public-service design—without being identical to any of them.

## Core concern

A legitimate emergency system may begin with:

**CONCERN → ASSESSMENT → PROTECTIVE ACTION → REASSESSMENT → CLOSURE**

A failure mode could instead become:

**CONCERN → LABEL → MONITOR → RECORD → INFER → REUSE → NEW CONCERN → MORE MONITORING**

The second pattern can become a self-reinforcing loop.

The research question is not whether such a loop necessarily exists in a particular county program. It is whether governance and technical architecture should contain explicit controls that prevent it.

## Important distinction

Substance use, alcohol use, homelessness, unusual behavior, prior crisis contact, or other vulnerability indicators should **not automatically be treated as equivalent to suicidal intent**.

This project therefore asks how a system can identify genuine emergency risk without allowing broad proxy categories to become permanent risk identities.

## Central design principle

> **Emergency authority should be bounded by purpose, evidence, time, review, and closure.**

A resilient system should be able to answer:

1. Why was the intervention initiated?
2. What evidence justified it?
3. Who authorized it?
4. What information was necessary?
5. Who can access it?
6. What other uses are prohibited?
7. When must the situation be reassessed?
8. What ends the intervention?
9. What happens to the records afterward?
10. Can AI generate new conclusions from retained information?
11. Can information be reused for an unrelated purpose?
12. Can the person correct an inaccurate classification?

## Proposed resilience model

**1. Trigger** — a concern is raised.

**2. Threshold** — establish what evidence is sufficient.

**3. Scope** — limit intervention to the stated safety purpose.

**4. Observation** — gather only necessary information.

**5. Reassessment** — determine whether the emergency condition remains substantiated.

**6. Closure** — when the emergency purpose ends, the intervention ends.

**7. Data disposition** — delete, de-identify, segregate, or otherwise govern information according to a defined rule.

**8. Non-repurposing** — the end of a safety intervention should not silently become the beginning of an unrelated investigation, eligibility determination, disciplinary process, intelligence profile, or behavioral dossier.

## The critical stop condition

> **Every emergency-monitoring mechanism needs a real stop condition that is independent of the system's ability to continue extracting information.**

Continued observation can produce more information, and more information can create reasons for further observation.

Potential positive feedback:

**monitoring → information → interpretation → justification → monitoring**

Potential stabilizing feedback:

**monitoring → reassessment → sufficient resolution → closure → restricted disposition**

## AI-specific concern

AI can increase the scale and persistence of this loop.

A system may move from:

- detecting a stated safety concern;
- classifying a person;
- inferring unspoken characteristics;
- correlating the person with unrelated records;
- generating additional “risk” signals from the resulting profile.

Therefore the project proposes a strict separation among:

**OBSERVE → CLASSIFY → INFER → RETAIN → SHARE → ACT**

Authorization for one stage should not automatically authorize the next.

## Non-condemnation boundary

This archive does **not** establish that:

- a Santa Cruz County proclamation creates surveillance;
- a particular Board member intends surveillance;
- substance users are categorically suicidal;
- suicide-prevention programs are inherently abusive;
- county employees or contractors will misuse information;
- AI is currently being used for the hypothetical purpose described here.

Those claims require separate evidence.

This archive preserves a failure-mode analysis and design question.
