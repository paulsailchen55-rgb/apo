# 01 — Sink and Vortex Experiment

The starting point was a two-basin sink with two drains. When both basins contain water and drain, one basin may form a visible vortex while the other may not. Changing a boundary condition by blocking one drain can alter the behavior of the other basin and cause a vortex to appear.

The exact result depends on basin geometry, drain geometry, water depth, flow rate, air entry, plumbing connections, pressure, and imperfections. The observation should therefore be documented as an experiment rather than treated as a universal fluid rule.

## Conceptual significance

The important observation is that apparently separate local regions can be parts of one coupled dynamical system.

> A connection does not have to be visible at the scale at which two systems are being observed.

A vortex is a temporary organized structure in a flow field. It is not an independent object detached from the fluid and its boundary conditions.

## Research value

The sink experiment can provide an intuitive physical model for:

- local versus global connectivity;
- hidden coupling;
- transient pathways;
- boundary conditions;
- flow topology;
- transmission of changes between regions.

It does **not** establish wormholes, alternate universes, or astrophysical information channels.

A future controlled version should record basin dimensions, drain dimensions, water depth, open/blocked conditions, plumbing geometry, time to vortex formation, duration, direction, photographs/video, air entry, and repeatability.
