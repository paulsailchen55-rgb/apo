# Civic Process and Public Sources

This section supports careful research into laws, regulations, agency policies, public records, community organizations, and lived-experience sources. Questions should be used to clarify responsibility and evidence, not to presume wrongdoing.

## Ten questions for further inquiry
1. Which city, county, state, or federal jurisdiction governs the issue?
2. Which agency or institution has authority over the decision?
3. What current law, regulation, policy, or program rule applies?
4. What is the date and original source of each important claim?
5. Which primary documents or public records are available?
6. Whose lived experience or community knowledge should be included?
7. What information is public, restricted, confidential, or personally identifying?
8. How can a person request review, correction, explanation, or public accountability?
9. Which claims are verified facts, interpretations, proposals, or unresolved questions?
10. What would make the research understandable and useful to an ordinary resident?
