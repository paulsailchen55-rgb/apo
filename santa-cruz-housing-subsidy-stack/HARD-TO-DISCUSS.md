# Hard to Discuss

These are questions that may be uncomfortable, politically difficult, technically complicated, or easy to oversimplify. They are preserved as research questions rather than conclusions.

## Housing production

- Can an affordability requirement be individually reasonable while its cumulative interaction with land, zoning, fees, financing, and construction costs reduces total housing production?
- Does maximizing the affordable percentage of each feasible project necessarily maximize the number of households ultimately housed?
- When a project becomes infeasible, where does the intended housing capacity go?

## Administration

- How much staff time and household time does each affordability mechanism consume?
- Does administrative complexity affect landlord/provider participation?
- Which requirements protect residents, and which primarily document compliance?
- Can a system reduce paperwork without reducing accountability?

## Subsidies

- How much of a subsidy reaches the household as housing stability versus paying for transaction costs, delay, compliance, or overhead?
- Are subsidies repeatedly compensating for structural design problems that could instead be removed?

## Ownership and land

- What changes if public land is leased rather than sold?
- What changes if land value is separated from housing-use value?
- Can mixed-income housing remain permanently affordable without permanent income classification?

## Measurement

- Are agencies measuring units produced, units leased, occupied units, retained households, or stable households over time?
- Can “affordable” be measured without measuring successful lease-up and retention?

## Accountability

- When a legitimate requirement contributes to cumulative friction, who is responsible for measuring the interaction?
- Does each generation of reform eliminate friction, relocate it, rename it, or add another interface?

## Rule

A hard question is not an accusation. It is a question that should survive evidence testing.
