# 1214 Soquel Avenue / Seabright Plaza — Project Action Packet

Status: working community reference; site-specific; questions and proposals remain subject to verification.

Purpose: turn the broad discussion into actions/questions relevant to the current 1214 Soquel Avenue project without importing unrelated experimental material.

## Immediate community questions
- What is Workbench's role on the project: architect, developer, builder, or a combination?
- What is the overall project timeline from groundbreaking to completion, without requiring a specific date?
- What is the status and timing of the required traffic-effects work, and will construction-period effects be included?
- Which preliminary concept is more likely to proceed: market-rate senior living or affordable housing?
- What definition and income mix will apply if an affordable-housing concept proceeds?

## Community concerns preserved as concerns/questions
The community discussion raised concerns about height, scale, trees/landscaping, sidewalk relationship, traffic and parking, construction impacts, existing businesses, commercial space, and neighborhood character. These are not treated here as established facts.

## Plan B / negotiation ideas recorded from the community document
1. Explore a lower building, including a preference for fewer than four stories, with trees/landscaping and sidewalk setback.
2. Explore substantially more ground-level commercial space (the community document proposed at least 15,000 square feet), plus coordination regarding relocation compensation and possible first-right-of-refusal or long-term lease options for displaced businesses.
3. Avoid beginning demolition merely because entitlement has occurred if doing so would unnecessarily displace businesses or create prolonged vacancy.
4. Require/seek a fully developed traffic and parking plan covering construction and post-construction conditions.
5. Explore a mix of affordable and market-rate units where legally and financially feasible.
6. Explore distributing the housing total over multiple sites so individual buildings can be lower or less intrusive; Riverwalk Apartments on Lindberg Street was cited in the community document as a model to examine.

These are community proposals, not conclusions about what the law requires or what the project must do.

## Additional questions developed during the broader conversation
- Can the project meet today's housing need while preserving future owners' ability to expand, reduce, reconfigure, or repurpose portions of the property?
- Which parts of the proposed building are intended to last 10, 30, 50, or 100+ years?
- Could the structural/infrastructure layer outlast the initial occupiable configuration?
- Which components are modular or replaceable?
- What would have to change in financing, code, structural design, fire/life safety, utilities, accessibility, or ownership for future adaptability to be practical?
- What materials are being installed, what are their expected service lives, and what happens when they are removed?
- Could a future owner understand the building without destructive investigation?
- If demolition eventually occurs, can components be separated and recovered rather than treated entirely as waste?
- If the project were designed with a 100-year useful-life horizon, what would the designers do differently?

## Core question
Can we meet today's needs while preserving tomorrow's freedom to choose?

## Working stance
This packet is not a simple pro/anti-housing position. It is a structured place for questions, documented facts, community proposals, and alternatives to be evaluated by the people with relevant authority.
