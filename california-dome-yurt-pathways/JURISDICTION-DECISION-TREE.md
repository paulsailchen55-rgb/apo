# Santa Cruz Area Jurisdiction Decision Tree

**Project:** California Adaptive Dome & Yurt Housing Pathways  
**Status:** PROPOSED operational framework; verify all current details before use

## Purpose

Determine which public agency or jurisdiction should be contacted first for a dome, yurt, ADU, replacement home, or community-scale proposal.

## Decision Tree

### Step 1 — Identify the parcel

Collect:

- Full street address, if assigned
- Assessor's Parcel Number (APN), if available
- Unincorporated area or incorporated city
- Existing structures and known permit history
- Whether the property is vacant, developed, or being redeveloped

Do not rely only on a mailing address. Confirm the governing jurisdiction through an official parcel or planning source.

### Step 2 — Which jurisdiction governs?

- **Unincorporated Santa Cruz County:** Begin with the County Community Development & Infrastructure / Unified Permit Center.
- **City of Santa Cruz:** Begin with City Community Development, Current Planning, and Building & Safety.
- **City of Watsonville, Capitola, or Scotts Valley:** Use that city's planning and building departments; do not automatically apply County rules.
- **Uncertain:** Ask the relevant planning counter to confirm jurisdiction before paying for detailed design work.

### Step 3 — What is the intended development?

#### A. Replace an existing home

Ask planning and building staff about:

- Demolition and replacement requirements
- Whether the proposed structure is a dwelling under applicable code
- Zoning, setbacks, height, lot coverage, and floor-area rules
- Coastal, environmental, geotechnical, fire, access, and utility reviews
- Whether discretionary permits or design review are required

#### B. Retain the existing home and add an ADU or smaller unit

Ask:

- Whether the parcel and existing primary dwelling qualify
- Whether the proposed dome/yurt can legally function as an ADU
- Maximum size, height, setbacks, and placement
- Water, sewer, septic, fire, and utility requirements
- Whether coastal, grading, environmental, or other reviews apply

County ADU materials identify zoning, building, fire, water, sanitation, and environmental considerations that may apply depending on the parcel. City requirements must be checked separately.

#### C. Create a new property or community

Ask for a preliminary meeting covering:

- Number and type of dwelling units
- Whether units are detached, attached, linked, or clustered
- Ownership and operating model
- Subdivision, lot-line, land-use, or density questions
- Roads, emergency access, parking, water, sewer/septic, and utilities
- Fire protection and evacuation conditions
- Environmental, grading, drainage, geotechnical, and coastal review
- Accessibility and common-area requirements

Do not assume that calling a project a “community,” “camp,” “temporary structure,” or “yurt site” removes ordinary land-use or building requirements.

## County First-Contact Matrix

| Need | Starting contact | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Zoning and development potential | County Zoning Counter | What zoning and General Plan rules apply to this parcel? |
| Building permit process | County Building Counter | What plans, code path, and professional documents are required? |
| Coastal or discretionary review | County Planning | Does the site or proposal require a coastal or discretionary permit? |
| Septic / onsite wastewater | Environmental Health Services | Can the existing system support the proposed use and occupancy? |
| Sewer / sanitation | Public Works or sanitation agency | Is service available, and are capacity or connection reviews required? |
| Water | Local water provider | Is service available, and is a will-serve letter required? |
| Fire | Applicable fire protection agency | What access, defensible-space, water supply, and construction requirements apply? |
| Site hazards | Planning, Public Works, and qualified professionals | Are grading, drainage, flood, landslide, or geotechnical studies required? |

## First Conversation Script

> I am evaluating a dome or yurt as a proposed dwelling or accessory dwelling. I am not asking for automatic approval. I want to identify the governing jurisdiction, applicable land-use rules, required review agencies, and the correct next step before investing in detailed plans. What information should I provide, and which staff member or department should review the proposal first?

Then ask:

1. What is the parcel's zoning and jurisdiction?
2. Is the proposed use allowed in principle?
3. Which development standards are likely to control?
4. Which reviews or agencies may be triggered?
5. Is a pre-application meeting recommended or required?
6. What documents should be obtained before hiring a designer or engineer?
7. What statements can only be confirmed after formal plan review?

## Evidence Discipline

Each result should be recorded as one of:

- **KNOWN:** Directly supported by a current official source or written agency response.
- **CALCULATED:** Derived from documented inputs, such as dimensions or estimated capacity.
- **TESTED:** Confirmed through an actual workflow, form submission, or documented agency interaction.
- **PROPOSED:** Suggested process that has not yet been validated.
- **SPECULATIVE:** Hypothesis requiring research or professional review.
- **UNKNOWN:** Not yet established.

## Important Limitation

This decision tree directs questions. It does not establish parcel eligibility, legal occupancy, structural safety, permit approval, or entitlement. Final determinations belong to the applicable jurisdiction and qualified professionals.
