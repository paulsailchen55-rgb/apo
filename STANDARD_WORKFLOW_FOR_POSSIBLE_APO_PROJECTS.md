# Standard Workflow for Possible APO Projects

## Purpose

This document defines the standard procedure for identifying, evaluating, organizing, and preserving possible projects and other reusable bodies of work within the `apo` archive.

The `apo` repository is an archive and reference system—not the project itself.

The purpose of this workflow is to preserve the development of thought while allowing ideas to remain open to future classification, revision, combination, or abandonment.

> **Preserve the possibility before deciding what it is.**

---

## 1. Identify the Archival Candidate

When a conversation includes an indication that material may belong in APO—for example:

- "This might become an APO project."
- "We should archive this."
- "Let's make this an APO project."
- "Push this to the APO repository."
- "This could become a research direction."

Treat the statement as a signal to evaluate the relevant conversation for archival material.

Do not automatically assume that every idea is a finished project.

Do not force every idea into a project structure.

---

## 2. Review the Conversation as a Whole

Before deciding what to archive, review the entire conversation available for the task.

Identify distinct bodies of work, including:

- Projects and potential projects
- Proposals and research directions
- Experiments and prototypes
- Frameworks and conceptual models
- Article or book ideas
- Technical concepts and tools
- Supporting ideas
- Personal or philosophical explorations
- Important questions and unresolved problems

Preserve meaningful connections between ideas, while keeping unrelated subjects separate.

Do not silently merge distinct projects merely because they share themes.

---

## 3. Create a Conversation-Wide Inventory

Create an inventory of the possible bodies of work discovered in the conversation.

Where appropriate, classify each item as:

- Project
- Potential project
- Research question
- Framework or concept
- Article or book idea
- Experiment or prototype
- Supporting idea
- Personal or philosophical exploration

The inventory should explain why each item was classified in that way.

Classification is provisional. An item may change categories as its meaning or purpose develops.

---

## 4. Preserve the Development Record

Archive more than polished conclusions.

Preserve relevant:

- Original ideas and unfinished reasoning
- Corrections and changes in direction
- Rejected alternatives
- Technical difficulties
- Unresolved questions
- Important tangents
- Rationale behind decisions
- Connections to other projects
- Limitations and uncertainties
- Changes in terminology or interpretation

The objective is to preserve how an idea developed, not merely the final version produced during the conversation.

---

## 5. Compare Against Existing APO Contents

Before creating the archival folder:

1. Inspect the existing repository contents sufficiently to identify related or potentially overlapping material.
2. Note projects, folders, or documents that may be relevant to the current conversation.
3. Avoid creating unnecessary duplicate work when the current conversation clearly belongs in the new archival folder.
4. Keep the current conversation's archival material together in **one clearly named folder**, unless a different structure is explicitly approved.
5. **Do not modify, reorganize, merge, rename, or rewrite existing projects or folders during routine archival.**
6. If existing material may benefit from updating, merging, or restructuring, record the possibility as a maintenance recommendation.
7. Present those recommendations for later review and obtain explicit permission before making changes.

Repository maintenance—including merging related projects, reorganizing folders, resolving duplication, or revising older documentation—is a separate activity and is not automatically performed during archival.

---

## 6. Create Self-Contained Documentation

When substantial archival material is identified, organize it into a clearly named, self-contained folder or document.

Depending on the material, documentation may include:

- Overview and scope
- Conversation archive
- Project or concept description
- Decisions and design parameters
- Open questions
- Experiments and test plans
- Supporting context and sources
- Related projects
- Current status
- Assumptions and limitations
- Unresolved issues

Not every possibility requires a full project folder.

Research questions, fragments, and early concepts should be preserved at an appropriate level without being forced into a more developed structure than the evidence supports.

---

## 7. Preserve Provenance and Distinctions

Maintain clear distinctions between:

- User ideas
- Observations
- Personal experiences
- Interpretations
- Hypotheses
- Proposals
- Documented facts
- External evidence
- Disputed claims
- Assistant-generated interpretations
- Unresolved questions

Do not silently turn an idea, metaphor, hypothesis, conversation, or possibility into an established fact.

Where practical, preserve the development path:

**Original input → AI transformation → Human revision → Later transformation → Resulting artifact**

AI-assisted organization does not automatically establish the truth, authorship, validity, or authority of the resulting material.

---

## 8. Archive the Conversation Inventory

When appropriate, preserve the conversation-wide inventory in a clearly named location, such as:

```
project-inventory/conversation-project-inventory.md
```

The inventory should preserve the reasoning behind the archival structure, including:

- What was identified
- What was excluded
- What was already represented
- What was newly archived
- Why items were grouped or separated
- What remains unresolved

---

## 9. Apply the APO Repository Rules

- Treat `apo` as an archive and reference repository, not as the project itself.
- Keep substantial work in clearly named, self-contained folders.
- Do not modify the root `README.md` unless explicitly requested.
- Do not overwrite existing work.
- Preserve uncertainty and provenance.
- Use clear filenames and sensible structures.
- Keep related supporting files together.
- Commit directly to `main` unless another branch is specified.
- Confirm the actual GitHub operation before reporting completion.

---

## 10. Report the Archival Result

After pushing work to GitHub, report:

1. The conversation-wide project and possibility inventory.
2. Which items were already represented in APO.
3. Which folders and files were added or changed.
4. The resulting commit SHA.
5. Important assumptions, ambiguities, and unresolved issues.

Do not report an upload as completed unless the GitHub operation has been confirmed.

---

## Guiding Principle

> **The APO repository should preserve the development of thought—not merely the final product.**

This workflow is the default standard whenever a possible APO project or archival candidate is identified.

It is intended to preserve the development of ideas while allowing their classification, structure, and eventual purpose to remain open.
