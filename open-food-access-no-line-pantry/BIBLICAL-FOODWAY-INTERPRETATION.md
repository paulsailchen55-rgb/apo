# Biblical Foodway Interpretation

## Status

**PERSONAL SPIRITUAL INTERPRETATION / PROPOSED PATHWAY**

This document preserves Paul's stated biblical and spiritual interpretation. It is not presented as established biblical scholarship, linguistic fact, or a requirement imposed on all users of the general platform.

## Core interpretation to preserve

Paul connects daily food, freshness, communal eating, anger, preservation, and spiritual discernment. He interprets the instruction not to let anger remain until sunset as related to a daily foodway in which food is harvested, prepared, and eaten within the day.

He further connects:

- the word `agro` with anger;
- the biblical warning about anger and sunset;
- the tree of knowledge of good and evil with food that was not ripe or freshly gathered that day;
- preservation and claims of avoiding death with the biblical concept of sorcery;
- Leviticus with same-day slaughter, preparation, communal witnessing, and consumption;
- Jesus and the day of the Lord with a distinction between food of the present day and food retained beyond that day.

These connections are Paul's interpretation and require separate theological, historical, linguistic, and textual research before being described as claims about ancient practice or biblical meaning.

## Practical implementation pathway

The Daily Harvest & Biblical Foodway version would support:

1. community gardens and participating growers;
2. inspection of what is ready that day;
3. staged selection and quality review;
4. same-day harvest;
5. same-day catalog publication;
6. same-day ordering;
7. same-day bundling and distribution or sale;
8. communal participation where the organization chooses to implement it.

## Relationship to the general platform

The general Open Food Access platform can support many food models. This pathway remains separate so that its spiritual purpose and interpretation are preserved without being silently secularized, universalized, or treated as verified fact.

## Source and provenance note

The practical model is informed by Paul's experience volunteering with and observing the Homeless Garden Project in Santa Cruz, including inspecting the farm, selecting suitable produce through multiple stages, and preparing bundles for same-day distribution or sale. This is a personal account requiring independent confirmation if used as an institutional description.
