# Immediate Community Action Network

## Status
Potential project / research and prototype concept. Separate from WAYFINDER.

## Purpose
Help people quickly discover practical, legal, reasonably safe ways to contribute time, attention, knowledge, materials, or energy to community needs. The primary question is: **What can I do right now that may help?**

The project is intended for churches, faith communities, nonprofits, civic groups, informal neighborhood networks, and individuals. It should also support people who cannot complete lengthy organizational onboarding or who lack stable housing, identification, a phone, transportation, or predictable free time.

## Two participation tiers

### Tier 1: Immediate low-barrier actions

Actions that may be performed without formal enrollment when permission, safety, and legality are clear. Examples include safe litter cleanup, reporting hazards, distributing sealed food or water appropriately, helping verify public information, or completing a clearly described public task.

### Tier 2: Coordinated participation

Scheduled or supervised activities offered by churches, nonprofits, and other organizations. Listings must state requirements in advance, including registration, training, identification, background checks, address or phone requirements, supervision, cost, and accessibility.

Formal safeguards should not be bypassed. However, broad labels should not substitute for a specific, explained requirement, and organizations should identify alternative tasks whenever possible.

## Foundational principles

- Anyone should be able to browse useful opportunities without an account.
- A person's housing status, diagnosis, recovery history, probation status, or other broad label should not automatically determine their ability to help.
- Legitimate safety, legal, insurance, and safeguarding requirements must be stated clearly and narrowly.
- Information should distinguish confirmed facts, firsthand reports, unverified suggestions, and outdated records.
- The platform should not become a reputation-scoring, condemnation, surveillance, or insider-control system.
- Human judgment remains important; AI organizes and assists but does not become the sole authority.
- The project should preserve original sources, dates, uncertainty, corrections, and removal pathways.
- The end purpose is increased practical participation, dignity, and public usefulness.

## Scope

The long-term vision is a common structure for cities, neighborhoods, counties, and regions across the United States. A first prototype should use a limited locality, such as Santa Cruz County, and a small number of carefully documented listings.

## Relationship to WAYFINDER

WAYFINDER is primarily an information and navigation layer. This project focuses on matching available human capacity with identified needs and actionable tasks. The projects may exchange data through defined interfaces but remain separate archives and concepts.

## Open questions

- What is the smallest useful immediate-action interface?
- How can listings show permission and eligibility without reproducing stigma?
- How can the system support people without phones, addresses, or accounts?
- Which tasks are safe for independent action, and which require coordination?
- How can local groups contribute without surrendering control of their own information?
- What evidence is needed before an action is marked available now?
