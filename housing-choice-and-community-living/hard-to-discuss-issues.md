# Hard-to-Discuss Issues

This document preserves difficult questions without presenting them as settled conclusions.

Topics include whether scarcity can make an apparent offer coercive; whether housing systems prioritize administrative convenience over resident agency; how ownership concentration and market power affect freedom; whether people are treated as cases rather than participants; and how public institutions can acknowledge lived experience without abandoning evidence.

These issues should be expressed carefully, with concrete examples, competing interpretations, and clear distinctions between observation, accusation, hypothesis, and policy question.
