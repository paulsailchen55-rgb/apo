# 1.2 — Sensory and Environmental Movement

## Core Observation

A being does not only move through an environment; it senses and responds to the medium in which it exists.

Examples for investigation include:

- Birds moving through air.
- Fish moving through water.
- Humans moving through sound, temperature, gravity, architecture, and social space.
- People using hearing, touch, balance, proprioception, vision, and other sensory systems to orient themselves.

The environment may be difficult to see directly while still being continuously sensed. Air and water, for example, can be understood as physical media that influence movement through resistance, pressure, flow, vibration, and sound.

## Sound as a Connecting Field

Sound is a sensory phenomenon that can make a person highly aware of being situated within an environment. Hearing is not only the reception of isolated signals; it can also contribute to a sense of surrounding space, presence, distance, rhythm, and connection.

Individual perception of sound can change with age, hearing ability, attention, fatigue, substances, context, and other factors. These differences should be studied descriptively rather than treated as proof of one universal perceptual experience.

## Research Questions

1. How does a movement language represent the medium through which movement occurs?
2. How do sound, vibration, pressure, resistance, and spatial feedback alter movement?
3. How do animals and humans sense environmental conditions that are not always visible?
4. Can sound be used as a translation layer between movement and environmental experience?
5. How should the system represent differences between physical sensory data and a person's interpretation of that data?

## Design Implication

The movement-to-music system should not treat music as an unrelated soundtrack. It may function as a model of environmental relationship: pressure, rhythm, direction, resistance, stillness, and response can potentially be translated into sound while preserving uncertainty about what the participant is experiencing internally.
