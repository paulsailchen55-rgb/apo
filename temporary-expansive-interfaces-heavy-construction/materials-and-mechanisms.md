
# Materials and Mechanisms

## Water

Potential functions: wetting, lubrication, slurry carrier, cooling, hydrostatic pressure, chemical reaction medium, and control of granular behavior.

Ancient drilling experiments show that water can help retain abrasive at a cutting interface.

## Sand and granular material

Potential functions: grading, temporary road construction, load distribution, adjustable support, and reusable fill.

Wet and dry sand can have different friction behavior. The effect depends on moisture content and granular structure; more water is not automatically better.

## Plant-derived surfactants and polymers

Potential functions include lowering surface tension, generating or stabilizing foam, increasing viscosity, forming films, binding particles, and providing temporary gels.

Saponins and marine polysaccharides are candidate material families, not validated construction materials.

## Foam

Potential functions to test: conformability, pressure transmission, micro-lifting, particle retention, chemical transport, and vibration damping.

A foam is a structured gas/liquid system, not simply water with bubbles.

## Expansion

An expanding material can convert chemical energy into mechanical displacement. Modern polyurethane is an established example.

A biodegradable or naturally derived substitute would require separate testing for pressure, expansion rate, stability, toxicity, biodegradation, and controllability.

## Abrasive slurry

A carrier can keep abrasive particles at a cutting boundary.

Variables include abrasive mineral, particle size, carrier viscosity, surface chemistry, pressure, rotation speed, temperature, and debris removal.

## Reactive boundary layer

The “rock-eating foam” concept is translated into:

carrier + reactive agent + mineral boundary

Possible mechanisms vary by mineralogy.

The research should begin with low-risk materials and controlled laboratory characterization rather than field-scale chemical treatment.

## Disappearing infrastructure

Desired lifecycle:

make -> use -> recover/reuse or disperse -> biodegrade where appropriate

Biodegradable is not synonymous with safe, and rapid disappearance is not automatically desirable. A temporary structural material must remain functional for the required time.

## Water + land + plants

The user's broader conceptual formulation can be recorded as water + mineral/land + plants as a possible basis for locally sourced temporary construction systems.

In formal research this becomes a materials question, not a theological or metaphysical claim.
