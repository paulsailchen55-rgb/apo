# Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk — Micromobility Access Question

## Context

A photo was taken of a Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk safety-rules sign. The sign lists, among other rules:

- No smoking
- No weapons
- No amplified music
- No pets
- No wheeled, roller, or sport devices
- No drones
- No disruptive, abusive, or threatening behavior
- No explicit or graphic imagery
- No tripods
- No glass containers
- No masks, except for health/medical-safety reasons
- No soliciting or sales

The question raised was whether a **manual Razor scooter** may be used in the nearby public bikeway/transportation facility, and whether the rules distinguish between the private Boardwalk property and the public Beach Street corridor.

## Key distinction to investigate

The Boardwalk's own property rules are not necessarily the same thing as City of Santa Cruz traffic or bikeway rules. A Boardwalk prohibition on wheeled devices should therefore not automatically be treated as a prohibition on every public street or bikeway near the Boardwalk.

The useful legal question is:

> If a nonmotorized Razor scooter is not permitted on the specific public bikeway in question, what exact statute, municipal ordinance, resolution, or posted restriction prohibits it?

## Policy concept

Instead of treating transportation infrastructure as exclusively for bicycles, a possible policy proposal is a **low-speed active-transportation / micromobility facility** that could accommodate appropriate devices such as:

- Bicycles
- E-bikes where otherwise lawful
- Manual scooters
- Skateboards
- Roller skates / inline skates
- Other appropriate low-speed human-powered devices
- Mobility devices as required by applicable law

Potential operating rules could address the actual safety issue rather than device labels:

- Pedestrians have priority at crossings and shared areas.
- A posted maximum speed applies.
- Users yield where required.
- Passing is controlled.
- Racing and stunts are prohibited.
- Users dismount in designated high-conflict areas.
- Device dimensions and operating characteristics can be regulated where necessary.

## Access and equity question

A related issue concerns public access to activities that may be permitted in commercial/private spaces but restricted in ordinary public spaces. The relevant policy question is not necessarily whether every private commercial rule should be eliminated; rather, it is whether residents and visitors have reasonable, free or low-cost public alternatives for lawful recreation and transportation activities.

For example, if a particular activity is restricted in a public area, policymakers can be asked:

1. What is the safety or operational reason for the restriction?
2. Is the restriction applied consistently to comparable activities?
3. What public space remains available for the activity?
4. Is there a free or low-cost alternative nearby?
5. Could a designated space or time period accommodate the activity safely?
6. Are the rules based on actual risk, or primarily on device/category definitions?

## Suggested civic request

A concise request to the City could read:

> Santa Cruz already provides separated bicycle infrastructure along the Beach Street corridor. I request that the City evaluate whether appropriate portions of the bikeway can be designated for shared use by bicycles and other low-speed human-powered wheeled devices, including nonmotorized scooters, skateboards, and roller/inline skates, subject to reasonable speed, yielding, pedestrian-safety, and operating rules.
>
> If nonmotorized scooters are currently prohibited from the specific Beach Street bikeway, please identify the exact statute, municipal ordinance, resolution, or posted restriction establishing that prohibition and explain whether the City has evaluated a shared active-transportation/micromobility designation.

## Important limitation

This document is a policy/research note, not a determination that a particular Razor scooter is currently legal or illegal at a particular location. The exact facility, signs, municipal code provisions, and device characteristics should be verified before relying on it for enforcement or legal purposes.
