# Step 4 — Key Challenges

## Technical and physical challenges

- Stable three-wheel geometry, steering, braking, visibility, and safe weight limits.
- Push comfort, ride comfort, maneuverability, weather resistance, and accessibility.
- Compatibility between solid or pneumatic wheels, rough pavement, curbs, ramps, and uneven ground.
- Safe integration of any future motor, battery, lighting, or charging system.

## Storage and transportation challenges

- Secure storage that protects both the trike and personal belongings from theft, weather, fire, and damage.
- Clear procedures for check-in, access, reservations, forgotten property, emergencies, and abandoned items.
- Uncertain acceptance of cargo trikes on buses, transit racks, sidewalks, and inside buildings; local rules must be verified.

## Recovery and legal challenges

- Creating a realistic process for reporting theft, removal, confiscation, or discovery of a trike.
- Establishing agreements concerning property handling, inventories, consent, privacy, liability, and unclaimed belongings.
- Avoiding assumptions that police, city workers, cleanup contractors, or private property owners must cooperate without an agreement.

## Operational and social challenges

- Ongoing repair, replacement parts, training, staffing, insurance, funding, and maintenance capacity.
- Preventing misuse, coercion, resale, exclusion, or unequal access while avoiding excessive surveillance or burdensome eligibility rules.
- Protecting participant privacy and separating vehicle identification from unnecessary tracking of a person.
- Preventing mission drift if paid services or commercial partnerships are introduced alongside public-benefit access.

## Working conclusion

The first prototype should reduce complexity: test the frame, cargo capacity, pushing, seating, steering, and braking before adding shelter systems, electronics, or a large coordinated storage network.
