# Mercy Constraint Loop

Status: Framework / social-systems hypothesis.

## Origin

The user described a recurring scene:

A person who needs help asks another person for modest assistance. The potential helper says, in effect, “Sorry, I cannot,” because a lease, contract, policy, or other restriction prevents the help. The hardship is then described as unfortunate, while the upstream rule that produced the restriction may remain outside the conversation.

The user's concern is that this can become a repeating social pattern in which everyone treats the result as an individual misfortune while the combined system remains unexamined.

## Structural formulation

1. A person needs a modest resource.
2. A nearby person could potentially provide it.
3. A rule or contract restricts the potential helper.
4. The help does not occur.
5. The resulting hardship is treated as unfortunate or unavoidable.
6. The restriction is not examined.
7. Similar cases repeat.

## What this does not establish

The model does not establish:
- malicious intent
- coordinated action
- that a landlord wanted the resulting hardship
- that a tenant agrees with the restriction
- that the restriction is unlawful
- that removing the restriction would solve homelessness

The “smirk” or intentionality described in the originating conversation is preserved as part of the user's interpretation and emotional description, not as an empirical finding.

## Research question

Can repeated contractual, insurance, financing, zoning, or institutional restrictions create an aggregate reduction in lawful neighbor-to-neighbor assistance?

If so:
- which restrictions matter most?
- which are legally required?
- which are contractual choices?
- which exist because of insurance or financing requirements?
- which could be modified without creating unacceptable risk?
- can a regulated alternative preserve the intended protection while allowing limited assistance?

## Design response

The Traveling Neighborhood Resource Network can serve as a test of whether some barriers can be replaced with:
- explicit permission
- narrow resource definitions
- short stays
- clear departure rules
- privacy protection
- documented property authority
- insurance/coverage review
- incident reporting
- measurable accountability

The purpose is not to prove that every restriction is bad. It is to make the restriction-and-consequence relationship visible enough to investigate.

## Relationship to Lease Harm Analysis

Lease Harm Analysis already provides a general method for examining clauses and burdens. This document applies one narrower question to the proposed traveler/resource network.

The two bodies of work should remain separate.
