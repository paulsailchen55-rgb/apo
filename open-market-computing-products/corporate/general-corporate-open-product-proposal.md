# General Corporate Open Product Proposal

## Purpose

Develop a reusable framework for asking electronics and computing manufacturers to add a deliberately open product or product line alongside their proprietary products.

## Core proposition

A company does not need to make its entire business open source.

It can retain its proprietary products, proprietary software, patents, services, algorithms, industrial designs, and other protected technology while deliberately creating one product designed for independent development.

The proposal is therefore a **parallel product category**, not a conversion of the entire company.

## Suggested question

> Would your company consider creating at least one genuinely open computing product that customers can purchase specifically because they are legally and technically permitted to modify, study, repair, and develop the hardware and software?

## Possible characteristics

A qualifying product might provide some combination of:

- documented hardware interfaces;
- accessible or documented drivers;
- an unlockable or developer-controlled boot process;
- firmware modification or replacement;
- open reference software;
- documented sensors and peripherals;
- repair documentation;
- development tools;
- explicit legal permission for independent development;
- clear security and safety boundaries.

The final definition should be developed through research.

## What is not being requested

The proposal does not automatically ask a company to:

- open every product;
- disclose trade secrets;
- abandon legitimate intellectual-property protection;
- eliminate proprietary products;
- publish every source-code component;
- expose safety-critical or security-sensitive systems without controls.

The objective is to create a genuine market choice.

## Market rationale

The open product category could support an ecosystem of independent developers, researchers, educators, students, makers, artists, accessibility developers, startups, and small businesses.

It could also produce innovations that the manufacturer would not otherwise develop.

## Research requirement

Before approaching any particular company, determine:

1. What the company already offers.
2. What developer access already exists.
3. What the hardware architecture permits.
4. Which parts are proprietary for legitimate reasons.
5. What minimum openness would make the product meaningfully independent.
6. What legal terms would be required.
7. What safety and security boundaries are necessary.
8. Whether comparable companies already offer such a product.
