# Conversation Context

This body of work emerged from research into 1214 Soquel Avenue / Seabright Plaza and a discussion about commercial property-data services such as Redfin and similar aggregators.

The user's concern was that useful information about property and government records can appear to be locked behind subscriptions or professional services, creating an economic barrier to understanding one's community.

The proposed response was not to reproduce a commercial database. It was to map the underlying public-record trail and distinguish source-of-record information, secondary aggregation, research clues, information not yet verified, and access barriers/costs.

Intended principle: a person should be able to learn which public office or authoritative system holds a record without first purchasing a private data subscription.

This is connected to the Civic Flow Chart and Property Risk, Value & Options Map, but remains distinct because its subject is information access itself.

No conclusion is made here that a particular company, agency, or database is intentionally withholding information.
