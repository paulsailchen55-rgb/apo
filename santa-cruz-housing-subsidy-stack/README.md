# Santa Cruz County Housing — Subsidy Stack, Stability & the Weird Housing Lab

**Status:** PROPOSED / RESEARCH IN PROGRESS  
**Updated:** September 24, 2026

This project studies housing as a connected feasibility and stability system rather than as a single inclusionary-percentage question.

Core stack:

LAND → ZONING → DENSITY → DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS → FEES/EXACTIONS → CONSTRUCTION → FINANCING → SUBSIDIES → AFFORDABILITY → MARKET REVENUE → ADMINISTRATION → STABILITY → FEASIBILITY

## Latest finding

> **Affordability is not the same as housing stability.**

A unit can satisfy an AMI or affordability definition at a point in time while the household, provider, or program remains vulnerable to administrative delay, income interruption, compliance requirements, provider participation constraints, or long-run operating and financing pressures.

## Outside the Stack: The Weird Housing Lab

The next research phase asks:

> **What if the housing affordability problem is partly caused by repeatedly subsidizing transactions instead of designing a housing asset that requires fewer transactions to remain stable?**

The lab tests designs that remove or reduce dependencies underneath the conventional subsidy stack.

### Core experiments

1. **Remove land from the rent** — retain land in public, trust, or community ownership and use long-term building rights.
2. **Make developers compete on housing outcomes, not land price** — evaluate durable housing outcomes rather than maximizing a land bid.
3. **Make the building the subsidy instead of the tenant** — design buildings to generate resources, avoid costs, and build reserves.
4. **Permanent housing without permanent poverty classification** — preserve affordability in the asset while allowing resident incomes to change.
5. **Housing First, but invert the bureaucracy** — make housing the stable platform from which other needs are coordinated.
6. **The Housing Operating System** — organize payment, maintenance, assistance, notices, and continuity around the housing relationship/address rather than disconnected agencies.
7. **Turn subsidy into a reserve** — create continuity capacity for defined disruptions instead of treating every problem as a recurring monthly payment.
8. **Make the building generate assets** — combine housing with energy, storage, workspace, commercial uses, services, and resilience.
9. **No conventional landlord, but professional operations** — separate ownership/control from professional building management.
10. **Housing that does not care what your income is** — investigate cost-based, mixed-income housing where AMI is secondary to the long-run economics of the asset.
11. **The Stable Housing Utility** — combine public/community land, long-term building rights, cost-based housing, mixed incomes, resident governance, professional operations, low-friction assistance, reserves, and building-generated resources.
12. **The AMI feedback-loop hypothesis** — investigate whether upstream income classification becomes a downstream administrative dependency.
13. **Housing Stability Yield** — measure durable housing outcomes rather than unit designation alone.

## Santa Cruz Weird Housing Lab

The deliberately unusual question:

> **What housing arrangement would sound unreasonable under today's financing, AMI, zoning, and administrative assumptions — but become reasonable if those assumptions were changed?**

Research prompts include:

- What if land is never sold?
- What if land appreciation is not required to sustain housing?
- What if public land is awarded through outcome competitions?
- What if the subsidy belongs primarily to the housing asset?
- What if the building earns part of its operating resources?
- What if housing remains available as income rises?
- What if a housing reserve follows a person between homes?
- What if a building has a collective reserve?
- What if there is no conventional landlord but professional management?
- What if one address replaces many disconnected administrative relationships?
- What if AMI is used for targeted assistance rather than as the organizing principle of the housing asset?
- What if housing is financed more like infrastructure than a speculative asset?
- What if verified resilience reduces long-run operating/public-service burdens?
- What if existing buildings are acquired and converted into permanent housing stock?
- What if stability is measured at 5, 10, 20, and 30 years?
- What if becoming economically stable does not require leaving affordable housing?

## Evidence discipline

Use:

- **KNOWN** — documented fact or existing program/model.
- **CALCULATED** — derived from stated inputs.
- **TESTED** — empirically evaluated.
- **PROPOSED** — design concept to investigate.
- **SPECULATIVE** — mechanism not yet demonstrated.
- **UNKNOWN** — information that still needs research.
